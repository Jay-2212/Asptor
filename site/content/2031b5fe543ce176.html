Supreme Court refuses bail to Asaram, permits round-the-clock medical assistance in jail

• THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-08-06

The Supreme Court of India on Thursday (August 6, 2026) refused to grant interim bail on medical grounds to self-styled godman Asaram, but permitted him to engage a trained caretaker of his choice to provide round-the-clock medical assistance in jail.

On May 27, 2026, the Rajasthan High Court upheld the conviction and life sentence imposed on the octogenarian for sexually assaulting a minor devotee at his Jodhpur ashram in 2013.

A Bench of Justices M.M. Sundresh and P.B. Varale took on record a medical report submitted by a board constituted by the Director of the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) to assess Asaram’s health condition. The medical board concluded that while he did not require hospitalisation, he may need round-the-clock medical assistance.

“We have taken note of the letter dated July 31, 2026, by AIIMS. On the statement of the Solicitor General that the contents of the letter will be considered in letter and spirit, accordingly the petitioner will have liberty to have assistance of a trained caretaker of his choice on round-the-clock basis,” the Bench ordered.

The court, however, granted Asaram liberty to seek interim relief if his health condition deteriorated. “The application stands adjourned with liberty to make a mention in case the health of the petitioner deteriorates,” the Bench said.

On Thursday (August 6, 2026), Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, appearing for the Rajasthan government, apprised the Bench that Asaram had suppressed the fact that he had also sought parole from the Rajasthan High Court, which subsequently granted him 20 days’ parole on health grounds. Mr. Mehta further contended that the pendency of the interim bail application before the top court had not been disclosed to the High Court while seeking parole.

“They have suppressed one thing. After filing of this petition, they moved an application for parole. In the parole court also, they did not point out that there is a petition pending here,” Mr. Mehta submitted.

Justice Sundresh expressed reservations over the grant of parole and asked whether it had been allowed after the Bench was already seized of the matter. “After our order? It’s not correct,” he orally remarked.

The counsel appearing for Asaram, however, submitted that the parole application had been filed much earlier and was not based on medical grounds.

On August 3, the Rajasthan High Court granted the 85-year-old 20 days’ parole, taking into account his age and the fact that he had spent more than 13 years in prison.

“There is no case of misuse of bail on his part, nor has there been any instance of him causing danger to society or to any of the witnesses,” a Bench of Acting Chief Justice Sanjeev Prakash Sharma and Justice Sanjeet Purohit had observed.

The case stems from allegations that a minor girl, who was a devotee of Asaram, was confined at his ashram in the Manai area of Jodhpur in August 2013, where she was allegedly sexually assaulted and subjected to criminal intimidation.

In April 2018, a special court under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act convicted Asaram of rape, wrongful confinement, criminal intimidation and other offences under the Indian Penal Code, as well as the POCSO Act, and sentenced him to imprisonment for the remainder of his natural life. He subsequently challenged the verdict before the Rajasthan High Court.

In its judgment delivered in May this year, the High Court upheld the trial court’s findings, affirming Asaram’s conviction for rape and the other principal offences. However, it held that the prosecution had failed to establish the charges of gang-rape and criminal conspiracy beyond reasonable doubt, and acquitted him of those offences.

The High Court declined to show leniency on account of Asaram’s advanced age and ailments. “We are unable to grant any indulgence, since the shadow of his frailty cannot justify ignoring the victim’s voice. Quiet. Devastating. Irrefutable. To ignore it would be to shake society’s faith in the criminal justice system, and send a wrong message no court must ever send, least of all when the perpetrator hid behind the cloak of a self-styled godman,” the High Court had observed.
