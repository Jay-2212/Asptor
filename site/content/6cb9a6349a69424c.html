Honorary degrees in Calicut University: pro-Left senate members raise lapses, authorities deny charge

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-21

Three pro-Left members in the senate of the University of Calicut (CU) have alleged procedural lapses in the institution’s decision to confer honorary degrees on filmmaker Sathyan Anthikad, industrialist P.K. Ahammed, and scientist E.D. Jemmis. However, the university authorities denied the charge on Sunday (September 20).

While Mr. Anthikad and Mr. Ahammed will be given a Doctor of Letters (DLitt) degree, Mr. Jemmis will be presented with a Doctor of Science (DSc) degree on October 6. Governor Rajendra Vishwanath Arlekar is scheduled to attend the event to be held on the main campus.

V.S. Nikhil, V.K. Brijesh, and P.K. Dinesh, the pro-Left Senate members, in a letter to Vice-Chancellor P. Raveendran, said that Statute 1(1) (3), Chapter 5 of the Calicut University First Statutes (CUFS) clearly states that the Senate is vested with the power to confer honorary degrees or other distinctions on distinguished persons in accordance with the conditions prescribed by the statutes.

Also, Statute 1, chapter 31, which deals with the procedure for conferring honorary degrees, says that the recommendation of the Syndicate to confer honorary degrees on distinguished persons has to be accepted by not less than two-thirds of the members present at a Senate meeting.

The pro-Left members pointed out that the Chancellor is competent to award honorary degrees on the recommendation of the Syndicate only in special cases. “But no circumstances to consider the ensuing conferment of honorary degree as a special case can be made out either from the letter or notification [related to the convocation ceremony],” they said.

Meanwhile, Registrar C.D. Sebastian told The Hindu on Sunday that the university confers three types of honorary degrees as per Chapter 31 of the CUFS, 1977: DSc, DLitt, and Doctor of Law. He said the recommendation of the Syndicate shall be sent either directly to the Chancellor or with the two-thirds majority of the Senate. In both cases, the Chancellor, who is the head of the university, is the competent person to take a decision. Mr. Sebastian said that the Syndicate, at its meeting on July 29, 2026, decided to confer the degrees on three persons. The proposal was subsequently sent directly to the Chancellor’s office with all the required documents, which was approved as well. “There are no procedural lapses as such. The Syndicate had unanimously passed the move to confer the honorary degrees on Mr. Anthikad, Mr. Ahammed, and Mr. Jemmis. None of the members recorded their dissent,” Mr. Sebastian added.

However, the pro-Left members claimed that the decision to confer honorary degrees without the Senate's approval was an “infringement” on its powers and contravened the provisions of statutes. They also sought a special meeting of the Senate urgently as per Statute 3(3), Chapter of CUFS 1977, to discuss the issue.
