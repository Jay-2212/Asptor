Surgical strikes presented bold example of India's counter-terrorism policy: PM Modi

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-27

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday (September 27, 2026) described Census 2027 as a national responsibility requiring public participation. With over 32 crore families recorded and 2.6 crore people self-enumerated digitally, he said accurate information about people and their families would help create a precise picture of the country’s future.

During his Mann Ki Baat address, Mr. Modi said: “The facility to self-register your census details is very convenient. You can register information about yourself and your family from your mobile itself.”

“The first phase of this exercise is nearly complete across all States and Union Territories; data of over 32 crore families has already been recorded. Work is currently under way also in the remote, snow-clad regions of Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, and Uttarakhand,” he said. Over 2.60 crore people had already entered their details on the census portal through mobile phones without uploading any documents, he added.

‘Decisive response’

During his address, Mr. Modi paid tributes to the victims of the September 2008 Delhi serial blasts, the 26/11 Mumbai and the 9/11 U.S. terrorist attacks. Recalling India’s surgical strike on September 28-29, 2016, he said the Indian Army demonstrated a decisive response by crossing the Line of Control to target terrorist hideouts in Pakistan. He also highlighted the Balakot air strike and Operation Sindoor as instances of India’s zero-tolerance counter terrorism approach.

Noting that September 27 marked the 100th birth anniversary of late Ashok Singhal, the former international working president of the Vishwa Hindu Parishad, he said: “The very direction and energy he imparted to the Ram Janmabhoomi movement veritably resulted in us witnessing the magnificent temple in Ayodhya today.”

“I have not only observed his life closely but also have had the opportunity to join him in this mission,” he said, adding that Singhal “also waged a major battle against social discrimination and inequality”.

On the “Somnath Swabhiman Parv”, he said Mahmud of Ghazni demolished the Somnath Temple for the first time in 1026 and the same year, the Sun Temple in Gujarat’s Modhera was also attacked and destroyed. He said despite invasions and destruction of temples, the people and rulers of that period did not lose their resolve and rebuilt them.

Mr. Modi hailed the contribution of A. Selvaraju, associated with the Department of Tamil Studies at a college in Manapparai in Tamil Nadu’s Tiruchirappalli. He said Selvarajuji discovered several sculptures and ancient temples, helping people understand and connect with the past.

Referring to the recently concluded Ganesh Chaturthi celebrations, Mr. Modi noted that India’s spiritual and cultural heritage transcended national boundaries and continued to inspire people internationally, citing Thailand resident Panthoun Thirkhanon’s decades-long study and promotion of Ganesha traditions in that country.

With Mahatma Gandhi’s birth anniversary approaching on October 2, he said cleanliness was one of Gandhi’s guiding principles. Mr. Modi highlighted various cleanliness drives across Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Mumbai, Delhi, and West Bengal as examples of public participation.

Green drive

On environment conservation, he noted the achievements made in the Sonai Rupai Wildlife Sanctuary, Assam, where land was cleared of encroachment for the once-displaced elephants to return to their natural habitat. He also appreciated the efforts to save pygmy hdog in Assam, due to which their number has risen from six to over 190.

In West Bengal’s Sundarbans, some women from Jharkhali – most of whom lost their husbands to tiger attacks – were creating mangrove forests, which were not only serving as a natural protective shield for villages but also creating income opportunities, said Mr. Modi.

About the government’s ‘Drug-Free Youth Campaign’, he said millions of youth were taking the resolve to stay away from drug abuse and encouraging others to do the same. The campaign will run for 100 weeks.

Highlighting the need to discover sporting talent in villages and towns, Mr. Modi praised Punjab’s Rajinder Singh, the 1980 Olympic gold medallist and former coach, for his efforts through the Round Glass Hockey Academy.

About the “One District One Product” programme, he mentioned that amla was now becoming a source of new possibilities in Pratapgarh, Uttar Pradesh, boosting local entrepreneurship. He said such accomplishments strengthened the “Vocal for Local” campaign, inspiring the country to become self-reliant.

Stating that the month of September is dedicated to the fight against malnutrition, he noted that Chhattisgarh was promoting moringa through “Har Ghar Munga”; Gujarat was encouraging nutritious millet dishes through “Vangi Spardha”; Maharashtra was conducting nutrition-awareness campaigns; and Mizoram was supporting “Poshan Vatikas”.
