‘ISKCON’, ‘Tantric’, ‘Gorak Nath’: 287 ‘other’ religions briefly listed on Census portal

By Vijaita Singh,Abhinay Lakshman • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

Going by the government's initial classification of “other” religions in the self-enumeration portal for the Population Enumeration phase of Census 2027, ‘ISKCON’, ‘Ramakrishna Paramahans’, ‘Sanatan’, ‘Shri Ram’, ‘Shri Krishna’, ‘Balmiki’, ‘Ramdev’, ‘Bishnoi’, ‘Sarna’, ‘Tantric’, ‘Yogi’, ‘Shia’, ‘Sunni’, ‘Methodist’, and ‘Pentecostal’, are among the religions practised in the country.

While these were among 287 options given under the ‘Others’ category for the question pertaining to the respondent's religion in the self-enumeration portal that went live on Monday (November 16, 2026) for four poll-bound States, the drop-down menu with these options was removed later in the day after The Hindu raised a query with the Union Ministry of Home Affairs regarding the classification.

Along the same lines as the previous Census exercises, there are seven main categories in the religion column: Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, and Others. In a first, the 'Others' category had been expanded into at least 287 further options, many of which have previously been classified by the Indian government as sects within the six major religions or as respondents’ entries in the “other religions and persuasions” category in the 2001 and 2011 Censuses. The drop-down menu also had several options that had not been recorded before, such as ‘ISKCON’, ‘Ramakrishna Paramhans’, ‘Ramdev’, ‘Khalsa’, ‘Tirupati Balaji’, ‘Gorak Nath’, ‘Basaveshwara’, ‘Baldev’, ‘Devi Maa’, ‘Devi Bhakt’, and ‘Dev Samaj’, among others.

‘Predictive text entries’

After the removal of the drop-down menu, a representative of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India (RG&CCI) said that, apart from the six major religions, there is the ‘Others’ category in which the “respondent is free to write any other religion as in previous censuses.” The official also said that it was “incorrect to say the mentioned terms have been shown as other religions”.

However, a government official told The Hindu that the list of options had been prepared as "predictive text entries" for the 'Other' religions category to help enumerators fill in the forms without “spelling variations and mistakes” when a respondent mentions any faith other than the six major ones. Giving an example, the official explained that if a respondent said they were a follower of ISKCON, the predictive text list would complete the entry without spelling variations or mistakes. How these appeared as options in a drop-down box is being inquired into, the official said.

The self-enumeration portal (se.census.gov.in) went live on Monday (November 16, 2026) for the States of Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Goa, and Punjab, where elections are scheduled to be held in 2027. For the rest of the country, the portal will open in January 2027.

A retired Census official told The Hindu that the options in the now-removed drop-down box were “strange”, particularly because there is no previous data to compare them with. The retired official also said that these entries appeared to be sects or movements within major religions. In 2001 and 2011, the tables on classified sects within major religions and respondents’ entries in the ‘other religions and persuasions’ category were tabulated later, after the enumeration was completed.

Before the drop-down box under the other religions category was removed, a government official in Uttar Pradesh, who provided training to Census enumerators, said, “The instructions are to record religion from the six mentioned categories. If a respondent chooses the ‘Other’ category, they can pick from the specified drop-down box with more than 280 names, and if the preferred religion’s name is not there, they can enter the name and it will be recorded as is."

There has been a sustained movement by tribal communities in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and West Bengal, calling for a separate religion column for Adivasi faiths, which they asserted was different from Hinduism.

Many firsts

The current Census, which has been delayed by six years with the last one having been conducted in 2011, is being referred to as the first digital Census. It is also the first to enumerate caste and the first to allow self-enumeration. The Office of the RG&CCI has decided to use an open column for caste enumeration despite demands and protests for a drop-down box of all castes. The government’s justification for using an open column was that there was no single available list of all castes, apart from the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes lists.  

The RG&CCI had on August 14 notified a set of 40 questions to be asked during the Population Enumeration phase of Census 2027, which included 13 new questions or data fields that were not part of the Census 2011 questionnaire.

The questions that have been added for the first time are spouse’s name, nationality, father’s particulars and mother’s particulars, digital literacy, permanent residential address, place of Covid-19 vaccination, number of bank accounts, passport number, driving licence availability, and mobile, Aadhaar and voter ID numbers, if available.
