West Bengal government to appoint coordinators for all 209 wards in Kolkata: Union Minister Sukanta Majumdar

By Shrabana Chatterjee • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Union Minister of State and Bharatiya Janata Party MP Sukanta Majumdar on Saturday (November 21) said that they are preparing to appoint coordinators for all 209 wards in Kolkata.

The announcement by the senior BJP leader from West Bengal, who has been entrusted with responsibility for the civic polls by the party, comes as elections to the Kolkata Municipal Corporation (KMC) remain suspended after a stay by the Calcutta High Court. “We are preparing for all 209 wards. If needed, we will appoint coordinators in 209 wards. Appointing a coordinator is a temporary matter, so there should be no legal issue in it,” Mr. Majumdar said. 

Only a day before. Mr. Majumdar had said that they would appoint coordinators in the 144 wards, which was the number of wards before delimitation. Calcutta High Court had granted a stay on the announcement of dates of civic polls on a petition challenging that delimitation was not done as per established norms.

Opposition raises alarm

Meanwhile, the Opposition have raised alarm over the BJP appointing its own people as civic body coordinators instead of legally appointing elected representatives. “BJP people becoming coordinators is unacceptable. If BJP people want to come to power, get elected and take the seats or increase the tenure of the previously elected representatives, or appoint government representatives,” Mamata-Trinamool Congress MLA Kunal Ghosh said.

Communist Party of India (Marxist) leader Sujan Chakraborty said that this is the BJP using backdoor methods to get BJP people at the top of the civic bodies as coordinators and control the Corporation.

“This is the backdoor policy to control the KMC, and it is a forceful acquisition of the civic body without a free and fair election,” Congress State general secretary Ashutosh Chatterjee told The Hindu on Saturday (November 21, 2026).

Civic polls chaos

On August 22, KMC published a draft list of 209 wards, increasing it from 144 wards.

The Calcutta High Court on November 6 had restrained the West Bengal State Election Commission from issuing any notification for the KMC elections based on newly redrawn wards until a pending petition challenging the delimitation exercise is decided. The State government has filed a petition with the Supreme Court to ask it to lift the ban on holding the civic polls. The top court is likely to hear the matter of the KMC ward delimitation and civic poll petition on Monday (November 23). 

The last KMC election was held in 2021, in which the Trinamool Congress won 134 seats out of 144. The Trinamool Congress board at KMC was dissolved weeks after the BJP came to power in West Bengal on May 4, 2026. KMC Mayor Firhad Hakim resigned from his post, stating that he wanted a dignified exit from the post as the civic body has become “practically defunct” after the regime change in the State.
