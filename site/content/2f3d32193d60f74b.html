Nobody in India faces religious discrimination: Rijiju amid Siraj row

By PTI • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-21

Minority Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju and Uttar Pradesh BJP leaders on Friday (November 20, 2026) slammed Congress MP Imran Masood, dismissing his insinuation of communal bias in dropping fast bowler Mohammed Siraj from the playing XI in the ongoing cricket Test match against New Zealand.

They said nobody in India is discriminated against or overlooked on the basis of their religion.

The remarks of the Minister and BJP leaders came after Mr. Masood, the MP from Saharanpur and U.P. Congress working president, allegedly said that Mr. Siraj was not given a place in the side because of his name.

Asked about Mr. Masood's comments, Mr. Rijiju told reporters here, "Nobody here is discriminated against or overlooked on the basis of their religion. In our country, opportunities are given based on merit."

One's religion or caste has nothing to do with it, he added.

"If someone feels this way, it is not right. The PM said that we are all one and we have a goal of 'Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas' and 'Ek Bharat Shreshth Bharat', then there is no need to create divides on the basis of caste or religion," Mr. Rijiju said.

Slamming the Congress over Mr. Masood's remarks, BJP national spokesperson Pradeep Bhandari said on X, "Shame on Congress party! Congress party did not even leave the Indian cricket team from its vote bank politics! Congress party is communal!"

"A person wearing Indian jersey is Indian! Congress party always chooses vote bank politics over India's National interest!" Mr. Bhandari said.

Mr. Masood's remarks drew a sharp rebuttal from the U.P. BJP leaders who accused the Congress MP of engaging in appeasement politics.

"He is playing the dangerous game of polarisation and appeasement politics. In India, people aren't selected for national teams on the basis of their religion," U.P. Minister of State for Minority Welfare and Haj Danish Azad Ansari told PTI.

Accusing the Congress party of stirring the pot of communal polarisation, he said, "The Congress is unable to digest that the country is moving forward with the motto of 'sabka saath, sabka vikas', and thus, it (the Opposition party) is busy playing its divisive game."

The U.P. Minister demanded that "instead of indulging in communal politics", the Congress that ruled the country for decades "must reveal" how many sports academies it built in minority-dominated pockets and what it did to improve sports infrastructure in the country.

U.P. BJP spokesperson Rakesh Tripathi told PTI that Mr. Masood had made the statement with an eye on polarisation ahead of U.P. Assembly elections and was attempting to portray himself as a champion of the Muslim community.

"Muslims have made significant contributions across fields, including Bollywood, cricket, and science, and their achievements reflected India's inclusive character," Mr. Tripathi said.

He cited former India cricket captain Mohammed Azharuddin, cricketers Yusuf Pathan and several others who had brought laurels to the country, as well as former President and renowned scientist A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.

The BJP spokesperson also pointed to Muslim actors who had made a mark in the film industry, saying Mr. Masood's remarks were driven entirely by political polarisation.

Samajwadi Party spokesperson Abbas Haider said the BJP was using Mr. Masood's remarks deliberately "with an intention to polarise the public."

"Selections in Cricket, or any other sport for that matter, should be based on merit," Mr. Haider added.

The Samajwadi Party and the Congress are allies in the broader INDIA bloc.

India cricket's heavyweights Sunil Gavaskar and Ravi Shastri on Thursday (November 19, 2026) had questioned the controversial exclusion of Mr. Siraj from the playing XI for the ongoing first Test against New Zealand, saying the team management should have considered the pacer's experience.

India picked four pacers for the Test, led by Jasprit Bumrah. The other three who made the cut were Prasidh Krishna, Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj.

While Mr. Gavaskar called the non-selection baffling, Mr. Shastri said on social media that Mr. Siraj should have been part of the team considering his experience and work ethic.

However, India batting coach Sitanshu Kotak defended the team management's decision to leave senior pacer Mohammed Siraj out of the playing XI for the first Test against New Zealand, saying the combination was picked with all five days of the match in mind and not just the opening day.
