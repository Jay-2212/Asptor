    Looking back at Film and Television Institute of India's role on making India’s filmmaking rebels

    In the start-up years, the Institute’s annual budget was just ₹3 lakh. There were shortages galore. In fact, the teacher shortage was so dire that Jagat Murari could hardly keep the institute running. The solution? He persuaded some of the finest filmmakers to come to Pune as guest faculty. Directors such as David Lean, Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, Raj Kapoor, Ismail Merchant, James Ivory, and many others walked through the FTII gates. Cinematographers like R.D. Mathur (of Mughal-e-Azam fame) and Subrata Mitra (Ray’s cameraman) came. Kamini Kaushal and Balraj Sahni taught acting. In the academic year 1963-64, he pulled in 50 such guest lecturers.
