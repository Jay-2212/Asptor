Bisleri water case: The chemicals, conflicting safety tests and Reliance angle

India’s apex food safety regulator, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), on Monday (November 22) announced enforcement action against nine entities — including ‘sanction of prosecution’ for India’s largest bottled water manufacturer, Bisleri.

‘Sanction of prosecution’, under Section 42 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, refers to the official approval required from the Commissioner of Food Safety before criminal legal proceedings can be launched against an offender.

The move comes after a fresh round of inspections and compliance verifications. Here is what is known so far.

What is the case against Bisleri?

In a social media post, the FSSAI said that the regulatory sample of “Packaged Drinking Water (other than Mineral Water)” was collected from the premises of M/s Bisleri International Pvt. Ltd. for analysis, and the sample was found “non-compliant with the prescribed bromate limits and declared unsafe under the FSS Act, 2006”.

“Upon appeal by the FBO (Food Business Operator), the sample was referred to the Referral Food Laboratory, which also declared it unsafe due to the detection of polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). The laboratory findings established violations relating to unsafe food under the FSS Act, 2006. Sanction has been granted to launch prosecution under the FSS Act, 2006, against M/s Bisleri International Pvt. Ltd,” the regulator said.

What risk do bromate and PAHs pose?

According to the World Health Organisation (WHO), the chemical compound is not typically present in natural raw water at high levels; rather, it primarily forms as a disinfection by-product during drinking-water treatment processes.

Exposure to bromate carries several significant health risks, categorised by toxicity and long-term carcinogenic potential.

High-dose ingestion causes severe gastrointestinal distress (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea), central nervous system depression, hemolytic anaemia (a disorder involving destruction of red blood cells), and pulmonary edema (excessive fluid in the lungs), according to the WHO.

Further, severe acute poisoning can result in irreversible renal failure and deafness/hearing loss. Animal studies demonstrate that elevated bromate intake leads to severe kidney and liver toxicity, evidenced by marked increases in blood urea nitrogen and liver enzymes, as well as kidney tissue lesions.

Polynuclear Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs) are chemicals that occur naturally in coal, crude oil, and gasoline, and are produced when coal, oil, gas, wood, garbage, and tobacco are burned, according to the US Environmental Protection Agency.

What has Bisleri argued?

In its statement, the company said, “We are aware of recent media reports that the FSSAI has issued a notice to Bisleri. The matter dates back approximately 14 months, to a batch produced in September 2025 at Bisleri’s Goa manufacturing facility, one of 143 plants in our network.

On September 30, 2025, FSSAI collected six samples from the Goa facility. Initial testing found that five samples met all applicable standards. The sixth recorded a bromate level of 12 micrograms per litre, against the prescribed limit of 10 micrograms per litre.”

Following Bisleri’s appeal dated November 22, 2025, the FSSAI sent that sample to a referral laboratory for retesting. The referral laboratory found bromate levels to be within the prescribed limit and separately flagged PAHs in the sample.

Bisleri said the difference between the two results raised questions and warranted clarification. It arranged for the same sample to be tested independently at an FSSAI-approved laboratory. That test found bromate within the prescribed limit, the company said.

“We submitted the independent test results to FSSAI’s Western Regional Office with a formal letter dated 2 February 2026. We received no further communication from FSSAI after that submission and understood the matter to be closed,” the company said.

“To date, Bisleri has received no notice by email or in hard copy and has been unable to locate the reported notice on FSSAI’s website. Drinking water safety is a priority for Bisleri. We maintain stringent quality controls of over 90 tests across our 10-step manufacturing process and will continue to cooperate with FSSAI to address the matter through the appropriate regulatory process. A trusted brand for years, Bisleri remains safe for consumption,” Bisleri said in a statement.

Timing of FSSAI inspection

Bisleri said the enforcement action was announced on Monday, but the issue dates back to last year. As per the company, it did not receive any notices in recent months and considered the “matter closed”.

The sample collection also came amid changes within the sector. It happened days before Reliance Consumer Products, the FMCG unit of Reliance Industries, was set to launch its new packaged water product called Campa Sure in October 2025. Campa Sure’s launch was scheduled just days after the government’s September 22 decision to reduce tax on packaged water to 5% from 18% as part of a broader rate rationalisation plan covering multiple product groups.

Reliance Consumer Products in June 2025 also announced plans to invest up to Rs 8,000 crore over 12-15 months to expand beverage manufacturing. The Rs 30,000 crore bottled water segment in India is dominated by players like Parle Products’ Bisleri, Coca-Cola’s Kinley, and PepsiCo’s Aquafina.
