How EPFO’s new plan for PF withdrawals will work

• INDIAN EXPRESS EXPLAINED • 2026-11-21

The Central Board of Trustees (CBT) of the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) held its 240th meeting on Thursday (November 19) to discuss the key proposal of allowing a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) with periodic payouts at the time of retirement instead of lump sum withdrawal of the entire corpus.

The EPFO also eased withdrawal rules by reducing the minimum balance requirement to 10% from 25% — a reversal of its earlier decision that had faced social media backlash and criticism from Opposition parties at the time of its announcement last year. There was also relief provided to those who opt for premature final settlement, especially after being unemployed, with a reduction in the waiting period for final provident fund settlement.

Members will now once again be allowed to claim premature final settlement of their provident fund after remaining unemployed for two months, instead of the 12-month waiting period introduced in October 2025 for full withdrawal. The minimum waiting period for final pension withdrawal will remain 36 months.

Systematic withdrawal plan

The SWP proposes to allow retiring members to retain their savings within the EPF ecosystem, continue earning EPF interest, and receive post-retirement income without surrendering ownership of the residual corpus. Multiple withdrawal options are proposed to be provided to EPF subscribers: full systematic withdrawal, or partial lump-sum withdrawal with SWP for the balance, or interest-only withdrawal models. Under the SWP, the retiring members will be able to withdraw their accumulated PF savings at regular intervals, such as monthly, quarterly or half-yearly, based on their preference.

The facility would be “entirely voluntary, with no compulsion on members to opt for withdrawals”, the Ministry of Labour and Employment said in a statement. The option may also be extended to eligible family members and beneficiaries, for greater flexibility and financial security for the family. “This aims to ensure stable post-retirement income, reduce reinvestment risks, strengthen fund sustainability and facilitate the extension of social security coverage to gig and platform workers,” it said.

Also, the SWP proposes to allow the members to make voluntary contributions directly into their EPF accounts through digital channels designated by EPFO independent of employer payroll systems. Such contributions would not require employer matching contributions and would be credited directly into the member’s provident fund account.

The retiree will also be permitted to terminate the SWP and withdraw the remaining balance at any stage. It is also proposed that the spouse or nominee rights will be continued for the SWP for the balance EPF corpus of the member on same terms in the event of death of the member during the SWP tenure.

At present, EPFO members can withdraw 100% of their accumulated provident fund balance on retirement at the age of 58. Members with at least 10 years of eligible service can separately receive a monthly pension under the Employees’ Pension Scheme.

Around 6.32 lakh members above the age of 55 possess provident fund savings exceeding Rs 5 lakh and are expected to benefit from the SWP. The EPFO, which has over 8 crore active contributing members and over 34 crore total members, manages a retirement corpus of nearly Rs 32 lakh crore.

As per internal estimates listed in the SWP proposal, the periodic withdrawals by a member may generate higher monthly payouts when compared with comparable bank fixed deposit and annuity arrangements.

Coverage for gig, informal workers

The proposal will also introduce a framework to allow direct voluntary contributions and third-party micro-contributions (such as customer tips) directly into EPF accounts through Universal Account Number (UAN)-linked digital payment mechanisms, completely independent of traditional employer payroll systems. This would enable informal and incidental income streams to be converted into long-term retirement savings. Such contributions, however, will be subject to limits prescribed in the Income-tax Act and will be governed by rules and procedures specified by the CBT.

“This SWP scheme intends to provide flexible options to the member for enhancing his corpus in PF by means of voluntary contributions through payroll, direct additional contributions other than payroll and third-party micro-contributions. The proposed architecture of the SWP scheme provides for the possibility that the scheme may be extended to facilitate social security coverage and retirement savings mechanisms for gig workers, platform workers, construction workers, domestic workers, street vendors, home-based workers, among others,” an official said.

Investment proposal for AIFs

The EPFO’s Board gave also in-principle approval to allow investment of up to 3% of fresh accretion (annual inflows) in eligible Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), within the existing overall investment ceiling, initially through government-backed entities. Investment in government-anchored funds under National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF) and through “Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0” scheme managed by the Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) will be in focus.

AIF means any fund incorporated in India in the form of a trust or a company or a limited liability partnership or a body corporate which – (i) is a privately pooled investment vehicle which collects funds from investors, whether Indian or foreign, for investing it in accordance with a defined investment policy for the benefit of its investors; and (ii) is not covered under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996, SEBI (Collective Investment Schemes) Regulations, 1999 or any other regulations of the Board.

The Secretary, Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) had written to the EPFO in September 2026 inviting EPFO to examine participation in the Fund of Funds for Startups 2.0 through an appropriate co-investment or contribution structure, subject to EPFO’s applicable investment framework and requisite approvals, The Indian Express had learnt.

Also, the EPFO’s Investment Committee had earlier discussed the proposal for investment in AIFs, where the risk-return profile of AIFs versus Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) or Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) as an asset class was highlighted. While InvITs are pooled investment vehicles that own and manage operational infrastructure assets such as highways and power transmission lines, REITs pertain to income-generating commercial real estate such as office parks and warehouses.

The committee members said that AIFs — particularly those investing in start-ups and venture capital — have a significantly different risk-return profile and do not necessarily generate regular income. The committee then gave an in-principle approval for investment in AIFs limited to 3% of fresh accretions during a financial year.
