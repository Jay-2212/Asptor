Congress reaches out to DMK, other Opposition parties to stall passage of delimitation Bill

By Sandeep Phukan • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-28

Accusing Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah of seeking to change the Constitution under the garb of implementing women’s reservation after carrying out the delimitation exercise, the Congress on Friday (November 27, 2026) made a last-minute attempt to reach out to all Opposition parties, including the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) and the Sharad Pawar-led Nationalist Congress Party (NCP), to stall the proposed legislation.

The party is also considering urging the Chief Ministers of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka and Telangana to address press conferences explaining their opposition to the delimitation Bill in its current form.

The Congress is planning a nationwide campaign against the delimitation exercise, arguing that it is divisive and against national unity and integrity.

Meeting held

Some of these options were discussed at a meeting of the party’s parliamentary strategy group on Friday (November 27, 2026) evening at the official residence of Congress Parliamentary Party (CPP) chairperson Sonia Gandhi. The meeting discussed possible strategies to stall the passage of the Bills – the Constitution (132nd Amendment) Bill, 2026 and the Delimitation (Second) Bill, 2026 – in the Lok Sabha.

“They [the BJP] wanted 400 seats to change the Constitution and now, through delimitation, by increasing the strength, they are attempting it again. The Congress will fight this along with other parties. It is in talks with other parties and will continue to do so,” deputy leader of the Congress in the Lok Sabha Gaurav Gogoi told reporters after the meeting.

Party president Mallikarjun Kharge, Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi, general secretary (organisation) K.C. Venugopal, general secretary Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, Chief Whip in the Lok Sabha Kodikunnil Suresh and Chief Whip in the Rajya Sabha Jairam Ramesh attended the meeting, apart from Mr. Gogoi.

Sources said Mr. Ramesh made a detailed presentation on the likely strength of the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance (NDA) and the Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance (INDIA) bloc in Parliament, based on the developments since April, when the government failed to secure a two-thirds majority in the Lok Sabha, resulting in the defeat of the Bills.

He is learnt to have argued that the government would again fall short of the required two-thirds majority if the DMK voted against the Bills.

While the Sharad Pawar-led NCP, a key constituent of the INDIA bloc, has officially announced its support for the Bills, the DMK is yet to make a categorical statement on its position. The DMK had left the bloc after the Congress extended support to the TVK government in Tamil Nadu last year.

Mr. Kharge or Mr. Gandhi could make a last-minute attempt to persuade Opposition leaders to vote against the legislation.

Several leaders, including former West Bengal Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee and Left leaders in Tamil Nadu, are also trying to persuade DMK president M.K. Stalin to oppose the Bills, sources said. The INDIA bloc may also make a public appeal to the DMK.

Big trap, says Venugopal

Earlier in the day, Mr. Venugopal accused the Prime Minister and the Home Minister of seeking to set up a “big trap” for the people of India. “We are approaching everybody. The DMK always stands with the Constitution of India. I think whoever loves the Constitution of India cannot support these types of divisive agendas. So, we will approach all the Opposition parties,” Mr. Venugopal said.

In a post on X, Mr. Ramesh termed the move an assault on the Constitution and reiterated the Congress’s three demands: retaining the existing strength of the Lok Sabha and the State-wise distribution of seats for another 25 years, and implementing one-third reservation for women, including sub-quotas for women from the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, from the 2029 Lok Sabha election.

In a post on X, former Union Minister P. Chidambaram said the Centre’s proposed 25-year “freeze” on the States’ share of seats in the Lok Sabha will address two of their concerns but absolute numbers also matter.

He said the gap between Southern States and the other States will increase from 413 to 620. “Besides, the increased strength of the Lok Sabha will entail delimitation of the constituencies within each State. That will give a free licence to the government to gerrymander the constituencies within the State – as the government did in Assam and J&K. Gerrymandering is the greater evil and the greater danger,” Mr. Chidambaram added.
