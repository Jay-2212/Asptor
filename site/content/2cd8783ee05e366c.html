    SC declines to transfer the proceedings pending before the Delhi High Court

    Taking note of the submissions made by the amicus curiae, SC declines to transfer the proceedings pending before the Delhi High Court.

    “Having regard to the aforesaid, we accept the suggestions of the learned amicus curiae and do not disturb the position of the case before the Delhi High Court. The High Court is requested to monitor the case at short intervals,” the Bench says.

    Supreme Court 'not inclined' to transfer proceedings to itself

    Acknowledging that substantial progress has been made pursuant to the High Court’s directions, the Bench says it is not inclined to transfer the proceedings to itself.

    We have never taken this matter as adversarial, says SC

    The Supreme Court tells SG Mehta not to treat the matter as adversarial.

    “After two months, you will understand why we are saying this. Have faith in us. We have never taken this matter as adversarial… we need assistance from you. We will take care of your apprehensions,” the Bench says.

    The Bench is hearing ongoing proceedings relating to violation of land-use norms and building bye-laws. In March, the apex court had widened the scope of the proceedings and sought information from civic bodies in several State capitals, including Delhi, Lucknow, Jaipur, Chennai and Kolkata, on action taken against similar violations.

    SC hearing on Satya Niketan building collapse begins

    A Bench of Justices Ahsanuddin Amanullah and R. Mahadevan says it proposes to issue directions to restrict non-conforming use of land in Delhi.

    “Residential areas are in a mess... children cannot play, elders cannot walk,” the Bench says.

    The Bench also tells Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, appearing for the Centre, the Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD) and the Delhi Police, that it will not allow the Delhi High Court to hear petitions related to the Satya Niketan building collapse.

    “Since it is a local issue, may it be handled by the Delhi High Court,” SG Mehta says.

    “It will be more in control if the proceedings are before us,” the Bench responds.

    After Satya Niketan collapse, MCD continues drive against unsafe and illegal buildings across Delhi

    The Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD) continued its demolition and enforcement drive against dangerous buildings, unauthorised construction and violations of building norms across all 12 zones of the national capital on Thursday (September 10), days after the collapse of a paying guest accommodation in Satya Niketan killed seven people and injured at least five.

    Visuals from areas such as Laxmi Nagar, Jagatpuri and Pandav Nagar showed civic teams carrying out demolition and enforcement action against structures found in violation of building norms.

    In the last 2-3 days, MCD teams also carried out action in Vishwas Nagar, Mukherjee Nagar, Jamia Nagar, Shaheen Bagh, Tilak Nagar, Karam Pura, Karol Bagh, Ramesh Nagar, Mansarovar Garden, Chandni Chowk and Nehru Vihar, with the drive expected to continue there.

    Delhi High Court notice on plea for DU hostels, safety audits of PGs

    The Delhi High Court on Wednesday (September 9, 2026) issued notice to the Centre, Delhi government and Delhi University (DU) on a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) seeking formulation of a comprehensive policy to establish safe, affordable, and adequate hostel facilities for students of Delhi University across the Capital.

    PG operator, labour contractor sent to two-day police custody

    The Delhi police on Wednesday arrested Sudhanshu Lovneesh, a 31-year-old operator of the paying guest (PG) facility housed in the five-storey building that collapsed in south-west Delhi’s Satya Niketan killing at least seven persons and injuring several others.

    The police also formally arrested labour contractor Sanoj Kumar, who had been apprehended in Uttar Pradesh’s Kasganj a day earlier. He was produced before a Delhi court along with Mr. Lovneesh.
