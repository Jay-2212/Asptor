Shifting political allegiance under guise of dissent can topple elected governments, Uddhav Thackeray tells Supreme Court

By PTI • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-25

If shifting political allegiance under the guise of dissent is permitted then elected governments can be destabilised by engineering defections even among a handful of legislators, the Uddhav Thackeray-led faction told the Supreme Court on Wednesday (November 25, 2026).

A Bench of Chief Justice of India Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana was told by senior advocate Kapil Sibal that the Maharashtra Assembly Speaker committed a constitutional error by treating the Eknath Shinde group’s legislative majority as proof that it represented the original Shiv Sena.

Mr. Sibal, advancing rejoinder submissions on behalf of the Thackeray faction, said that the legislative majority cannot transform a legislature party into the original political party under the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution.

The defence of the Shinde group that it had expressed dissent was not even the foundation of the Speaker's decision recognising it as the original Shiv Sena.

"If shifting political allegiance under the guise of dissent is permitted, governments can be destabilised by engineering defections even among a handful of legislators," Mr. Sibal told the Bench which is likely to reserve the verdict on the two petitions.

The lead plea, filed by Sunil Prabhu of the Uddhav Thackeray faction, challenges the Maharashtra Assembly speaker's January 10, 2024 decision declining to disqualify the MLAs of the Shinde faction under the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution.

A connected plea filed by Mr. Thackeray challenges the ECI’s decision recognising the Shinde faction as the official Shiv Sena and allotting it the party symbol.

During the hearing, Mr. Sibal submitted that the Speaker's approach was contrary to the Constitution Bench ruling in the Subhash Desai case.

"The identity of the original political party has to be determined with reference to June 21, 2022, when the alleged act of defection first occurred," Mr. Sibal said, adding that the Election Commission’s subsequent recognition of a faction under the Symbols Order could not retrospectively determine whether an earlier act amounted to defection.

He also emphasised that disqualification proceedings before the Speaker and proceedings before the Election Commission were legally distinct and could not be conflated.

"This is not an act of dissent, but an attempt to topple the government", he said.

Countering the Shinde faction's argument that its legislators had merely dissented against the Thackeray leadership, Mr. Sibal said their actions went beyond an expression of disagreement and were aimed at displacing the government led by Mr. Thackeray.

"If you want to dissent, you call a meeting of the party. You have a discussion... Instead, you say, forget it, I don't even recognise you.

"You're not the leader. I am the leader now. It's my whip, not your whip. That's not an act of dissent. That's to topple my government," he said.

Mr. Sibal said that the legislators could have convened meetings of the party's organisational bodies and resolved their ideological differences through internal discussions if their intention was genuinely to dissent.

He referred to the June 2022 developments, including the removal of the party-appointed whip, the reaffirmation of Mr. Shinde as Legislature Party leader and the communication of the resolution to the Governor, as evidence of an attempt to undermine the existing government rather than an exercise of legitimate dissent.

Referring to the Governor's decision to call a floor test in June 2022, Mr. Sibal said that the constitutional premise behind the move was that a substantial number of Shiv Sena legislators had moved away from the Thackeray-led party.

He said the top court subsequently held that the Governor's decision to call for a floor test was illegal, saying that the Governor could not use internal differences within a political party as the basis for directing a floor test.

"Their submission that on June 21 they were the political party is not just perverse, it is contrary to the constitutional mandate of the Tenth Schedule," he said.

Mr. Sibal also referred to the events of June 30, 2022, when Mr. Shinde, backed by the rebel MLAs and the BJP, met the Governor to stake claim to form the government in Maharashtra and subsequently took oath as Chief Minister.

He said that this development could not be dismissed as a subsequent event unrelated to the disqualification proceedings.

"That cannot be an act of dissent. It is an act which is inconsistent with his conduct and results in his having voluntarily given up membership of the party," he said.

He also referred to the election of BJP candidate Rahul Narwekar as Maharashtra Assembly Speaker, saying that 37 MLAs belonging to the Shinde faction had voted against the MVA-backed candidate Rajan Salvi.

He questioned how voting against the candidate supported by the then Shiv Sena leadership could be characterised as mere dissent.

On November 18, Shinde faction, represented by senior lawyer Dhruv Mehta, had said that the decision of the Uddhav Thackeray-led leadership to sever ties with the BJP and form the Maha Vikas Aghadi government in November 2019 with the Congress and the NCP was a departure from the ideological platform on which the Shiv Sena had sought votes.
