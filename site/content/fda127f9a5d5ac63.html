    Traffic restrictions, diversions in central Delhi till 9.30 p.m.

    Traffic movement across several key stretches in the national capital will remain regulated on Saturday (September 12, 2026) as Delhi hosts the 18th BRICS Leaders’ Summit, with commuters likely to face diversions and restrictions in central Delhi and adjoining areas, police said.

    The restrictions will be in force from 9.30 a.m. to 9.30 p.m., particularly around Bharat Mandapam and the Pragati Maidan area, as well as on major corridors connecting central Delhi with other parts of the city, according to the Delhi Police.

    Several important roads, including Ring Road, Mathura Road, Bhairon Marg, Tilak Marg, the India Gate and C-Hexagon area, Sardar Patel Marg, Patel Road, NH-48 and Parade Road, will see regulated movement at different points during the day.

    Police have advised commuters to plan their journeys in advance and use metro services wherever possible to avoid delays caused by traffic restrictions and VIP movements linked to the summit.

    How Delhi is managing 35 special aircraft during BRICS Summit

    Behind the red carpets, motorcades and ceremonial welcomes for BRICS leaders is a tightly coordinated aviation operation involving around 35 special aircraft, multiple airports, controlled airspace and carefully timed movements to accommodate foreign dignitaries without disrupting Delhi’s regular commercial flights.

    The operation spans the Indira Gandhi International Airport, the Indian Air Force’s Palam technical area and other airports being used to park visiting aircraft. The CISF, Air Force, aviation authorities, airport operator and other security agencies are coordinating the movements.

    From keywords to digital trails: How Delhi Police are tracking social media ahead of BRICS 2026

    The Delhi Police have stepped up monitoring of social media platforms ahead of the BRICS Summit, with specialised units tracking public posts for possible security threats, misinformation and activity that could have implications for law and order.

    Xi Jinping leaves for New Delhi for BRICS Summit, to hold talks with Modi

    Chinese President Xi Jinping left for New Delhi on Saturday morning to attend the 18th BRICS Summit.

    He is scheduled to hold talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on the sidelines of the summit.

    Mr. Xi left Beijing to attend the BRICS Summit at the invitation of Prime Minister Modi, state-run Xinhua news agency reported.

    His entourage includes close associate Cai Qi, who is a member of the high-powered Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China (CPC) and director of the General Office of the CPC Central Committee, and Wang Yi, Foreign Minister and member of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee, the news report said.

    BRICS negotiators work late into evening to find consensus for ‘Delhi Declaration’

    As New Delhi prepared to host the BRICS Summit of 11 emerging economies, negotiators were working late into the evening on Friday (September 11, 2026) to forge a consensus for the “Delhi Declaration”.

    Diplomatic sources and officials said that all member countries had agreed to “almost all the text, with the exception of one paragraph”, indicating that the references to the war in Iran started by the U.S. and Israel, and Iran’s attacks on installations in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and other West Asian countries, had still not been resolved.
