When the Election Commission cancelled a bypoll in Tamil Nadu after Jayalalithaa launched welfare schemes

By D. Suresh Kumar • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-02

In 1993, the first AIADMK Government headed by Jayalalitha (as her name was written then), was preparing to face by-elections in the Ranipet Assembly constituency and the Palani Lok Sabha seat on May 19. Relations between the AIADMK and Congress, which had faced the general elections in 1991 as allies, had soured by then.

The Congress was also expected to enter the bypoll fray to flex muscles with the AIADMK but it delayed announcing its candidate.

Tamil Nadu Food and Cooperative Minister G. Viswanathan presenting lion cub to Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalitha during her visit to Ranipet in 1993. | Photo Credit: The Hindu Archives

On April 24 that year, journalists asked Food Minister G. Viswanathan (now Chancellor, VIT deemed university), if there was a possibility of the bypolls getting cancelled. He responded, such cancellations might have been effected in some north Indian States earlier. But in regard to Ranipet, he could say that the AIADMK had already made preparations, including opening of election offices and campaigning, etc. He did not see any reason why the elections should be cancelled.

However, on the night of August 26, the Election Commission cancelled the byelection scheduled for the Ranipet Assembly constituency stating that the State Government had violated the model code of conduct by announcing welfare programmes.

An Election Commission notification, rescinding its earlier announcement for the byelection, said the Commission had sought clarification from the State Government on press reports on the launching of welfare schemes to the tune of ₹503 crores and the bifurcation of South Arcot district.

The Commission said the State Government’s silence was “tantamount to admission of the facts reported with regard to the launching of the said schemes.” The Commission pointed out the bifurcation decision was announced in the State Assembly only on April 4.

“The Commission, being charged with the sacred responsibility of ensuring free and fair election under the Constitution, cannot be a mute spectator to such blatant and deliberate violations of the model code of conduct which have a definite effect of vitiating the entire electoral process in the said Ranipet Assembly constituency,” it said.

“The Election Commission’s decision yesterday to cancel the byelection in the Ranipet Assembly constituency of Tamil Nadu is based on the violation of the ‘model code of conduct for the guidance of the political parties and candidates’ by the State Chief Minister, Ms. J. Jayalalitha,” The Hindu reported on April 28, 1993.

“It is regrettable, but the situation has been brought about by a series of misdeeds indulged in by the AIADMK Government,” was how S. R. Balasubramanian, leader of the Congress (I) Legislature Party, responded to the development. He said it was highly improper for the Chief Minister to announce massive welfare schemes at a gala function at Ranipet. “The responsibility for the cancellation of the byelection entirely rests with the Chief Minister,” he said.

Soon, the AIADMK Ranipet candidate Kovi S. Moghanan moved the Madras High Court challenging the the Election Commission’s notification cancelling the bypoll.

Mr. Moghanan argued the Election Commission had sent a communication to the State Government on April 16 seeking certain clarifications on the launching of welfare schemes, expenditure on election tour of the Chief Minister at Government cost and creation of a new district. The Chief Secretary had sent his reply on April 19 and the Commission received it the same day.

He contended the model code of conduct was to be enforced only from April 6 and, as the Chief Minister had made the announcements of the schemes at Ranipet on April 5, the code was followed in letter and spirit.

“The notification to cancel the poll was done at the behest of Mr. T. N. Seshan, Chief Election Commissioner, who was actuated by mala fides and bias against the AIADMK and its leader,” he alleged. “As Mr. Seshan was not in New Delhi on April 26, it was not clear whether he had applied his mind before the issue of the notification,” the petitioner said (The Hindu Archives).

However, the Madras High Court declined to stay the Election Commission’s notification. “It is not a case for an ex parte interim order. I am not inclined to stay the notification,” Justice T. Somasundaram said while issuing notices to the Election Commission.

According to contemperanous reports, referring to the objection to the creation of a new district after Ramaswamy Padayachi, the petitioner’s counsel said the policy decision to create a new district was taken as early as April 1992. Only the naming was done on April 15. Moreover, Ranipet was not part of South Arcot or the newly created district and therefore the naming did not violate the code.

At this point the judge said: “Anyway, the naming was done on April 15, after the code came into force. We need not be technical. We must read the whole order of the Election Commission.”

Subsequently, in late July the Election Commission notified that the bypoll in Ranipet would now be held on August 19.

The votes polled were counted on September 24. The AIADMK candidate was declared elected by a margin of 12,661 votes over his nearest DMK rival, Arcot N. Veerasamy (former Minister).

Except Mr. Veerasamy and K. L. Elavazhagan of PMK, all the other 26 defeated candidates, including M. Thulasi of the Congress(I), lost their deposits. Incidentally, Thulasi had “retired from the contest at the last moment owing to illness”. He polled less than 1,000 votes.
