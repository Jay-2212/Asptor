Congress hopeful of U.P. alliance with SP, seeks ‘respectable’ seat-sharing

By Sandeep Phukan • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

The Congress is hopeful of forging an alliance with the Samajwadi Party (SP) for the 2027 Uttar Pradesh Assembly election but wants a “respectable” seat-sharing arrangement, All India Congress Committee in-charge for the State Rajendra Pal Gautam has said.

Mr. Gautam, in an interview with The Hindu, said the two parties are expected to hold formal seat-sharing discussions this week and asserted that the Congress would make the alleged “chanda chori” (theft of temple donations) a major campaign issue.

Though he did not disclose the exact date, the first meeting is likely to be held on Monday (November 23, 2026) afternoon. Senior leaders, including Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, K.C. Venugopal, and Mr. Gautam, are expected to participate.

Commenting on SP chief Akhilesh Yadav’s recent statement that the Congress had sought 181 seats, Mr. Gautam said, “I am not going to reveal which seats, but we have given them a list. We will try to convince them, and I am sure they will try to convince us. The outcome depends on these talks.”

Asked whether the Congress would settle for an alliance at any cost, Mr. Gautam said: “It is not like that at all. The understanding will be a respectable one.”

Countering suggestions that the Congress lacked the organisational strength to bargain for a substantial share of seats, Mr. Gautam pointed out that while the SP won 37 seats and the Congress six in the 2024 Lok Sabha election, the SP’s alliance with the Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP) in the 2019 Lok Sabha polls had not yielded similar results.

“We were weak, but we are not weak now,” he said, adding, “We have the best leader in this country right now, as only we can handle the BJP better than any other party.”

Key campaign issues

On whether the Congress was attempting social engineering to regain the support of the Brahmin, Dalit, and Muslim communities that had once formed its core constituency, he claimed that every section of society is now gravitating toward the party. “Every section is feeling cheated, whether Brahmins, Dalits, OBCs, or Muslims,” he said.

The party also intends to highlight the alleged chadhava-chori (theft of offerings and donations) at Lord Ram’s temple in Ayodhya. The campaign, scheduled to begin in the first week of December, will take the issue to villages, chaupals, and gram panchayats.

“Chadhava-chori, chanda-chori will be one of our main issues in the election. The BJP came to power using faith and then betrayed the people. In every press conference and public meeting, we will raise it,” he said.

Mr. Gautam also listed key campaign issues including the alleged closure of 27,000 government schools, reduced scholarships, rising education costs, discrimination in recruitment, failure to fill vacancies reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, alleged atrocities against Dalits and OBCs, distress among farmers, questionable police encounters, and the demolition of houses using bulldozers.

On the emergence of a new political front involving AIMIM leader Asaduddin Owaisi and Dalit leader Chandrashekhar Azad, Mr. Gautam said: “Voters in U.P. are intelligent enough to understand who this is intended to benefit,” hinting that the move would help the ruling BJP by splitting Opposition votes.

Rejecting the BJP’s claims regarding law and order, he said, “We will expose the selective use of bulldozers and excesses against Dalits, OBCs, and marginalised communities. We will puncture the government’s law-and-order narrative.”
