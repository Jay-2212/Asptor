Congress attacks ‘weak Modi govt’ over controversial U.S. advertisement

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-11

The Congress on Friday (September 11, 2026) questioned the Narendra Modi government’s silence over a controversial U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) post asking a “Mr. Singh” to self-deport.

The party said the episode amounted to an insult to Indians and reflected the Modi government’s inability to firmly defend the country’s interests before the Donald Trump administration.

Congress general secretary (communications) Jairam Ramesh said the advertisement, which used an artificial intelligence (AI)-generated image of a turbaned Indian and carried the messages, “Get off our roads” and “Self-deport or find out”, warranted a strong public rebuttal from the Government of India.

‘Continuous insults'

“Modi ji, who has been showcasing his international clout by calling Donald Trump his ‘friend’ and who even coined the slogan ‘Abki Baar Trump Sarkar’ among Indian-Americans, is now seeing continuous insults against Indians and people of Indian origin under his administration,” Mr. Ramesh said in a post on X.

He said the U.S. government’s post was “not just an insult to one individual or community” but an affront to the dignity of all Indians. The post was subsequently taken down following protests in the U.S., Mr. Ramesh said, but the Department of Homeland Security continued to defend its campaign and its language.

“Why has there been no strong public rebuttal from the Modi government on this insult to Indians?” he asked.

Mr. Ramesh linked the controversy to the deportation of Indians from the U.S. in handcuffs and leg shackles on military aircraft and the imposition of additional tariffs on Indian goods, arguing that the government had failed to respond effectively in both instances.

“Why has our diplomatic strength become so feeble during the tenure of the Modi government, which boasts of being the ‘vishwaguru’, that India’s interests and sovereignty are facing one blow after another?” he asked.

Contrast with Congress government

Leader of the Opposition in the Punjab Assembly Partap Singh Bajwa sharpened the attack by contrasting the treatment of “Mr. Singh” with India’s standing in Washington during the Manmohan Singh government.

“There was a time when ‘Singh is King’ captured the stature of India’s Prime Minister. Dr. Manmohan Singh commanded enormous respect in Washington,” Mr. Bajwa said, recalling former U.S. President Barack Obama’s description of him as an “honest and wise man” and George W. Bush’s role in strengthening India-U.S. ties.

“Today, the Trump administration’s Department of Homeland Security targets ‘Mr. Singh’ — a turbaned Sikh — with the words: ‘Get off our roads’ and ‘Self-deport or find out’. What a tragic fall from ‘Singh is King’ to ‘Mr. Singh, get off our roads’,” he said.

The DHS campaign was aimed at unlicensed commercial drivers. A related video featured the fictional character negotiating the release of truck drivers, including Harjinder Singh, Rajinder Kumar and Jasveer Singh, who were identified as having been involved in road accidents and other offences.
