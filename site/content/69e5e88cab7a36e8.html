Amid cold wave, students’ indefinite strike against J&K recruitment board scam gathers steam

By Peerzada Ashiq • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Protesting government job aspirants on Saturday (November 21, 2026) launched an indefinite strike in Srinagar amid a cold wave over the government’s failure to act against the J&K Service Selection Board (JKSSB), the main recruitment agency of J&K that has become mired in a cheating scam that has led to 16 arrests so far.

While former Member of Parliament (MP) Aga Syed Ruhullah joined the protesters in Srinagar, other regional parties also demanded the cancellation of all examinations held by the Board and called for “fixing the responsibility”.

The peaceful sit-in was forcibly broken up by police on Saturday night (November 21), said the protesters. “Students and Aga sahib were removed from the site against their will. The demand stands: a fair probe into the alleged FAA [Finance Accounts Assistant] rigging. This fight isn’t over,” said one protester. 

Speaking at the Sher-i-Kashmir Park earlier, Mr. Ruhullah said the protest is a “rare effort to open up space where youth could fight for their rights”.

“We demand that heads must roll. We have the elected government and an unelected government ruling J&K. Both share responsibility for it. Unfortunately, there is a sense of complacency. The examinations should be postponed, responsibility for the irregularities fixed and reforms carried out. The administration should engage students and address their concerns,” said Mr. Ruhullah.

Mr. Ruhullah joined scores of students and aspirants who held a sit-in all day in Srinagar. With evening temperature dipping, a spokesman of Mr. Ruhullah said, “There was no food or blankets. No entry. Students on a peaceful sit-in are being frozen out on a bitter Srinagar night. Their only crime is asking for justice over the alleged Finance Accounts Assistant recruitment rigging.”

The night temperature has dipped to around zero degrees Celsius in Srinagar amid a severe cold wave.

On November 15, the J&K Police busted four “cheating modules” in Jammu during the Board’s examination for the FAA posts.

Nearly 1.62 lakh aspirants had appeared for around 600 posts across J&K. Besides 16 arrests made in the case, the police said it filed three First Information Reports (FIRs) and recovered eight wireless devices, 22 mobile phones and four vehicles of the accused.

However, Board chairman Shrikant Balasaheb Suse said the “examination was not compromised by a paper leak or mass malpractice” and announced that the upcoming exams will be held “as per the schedule”.

Facing a growing public outcry, J&K Education Minister Shamim Firdous said strict action should be taken “if any irregularities or rigging are found”.

“Students, who have worked hard, deserve justice. We have already ordered an inquiry to identify the irregularities, and action will follow if evidence is found,” said Ms. Firdous.

In spite of assurances of action by Ms. Firdous, there was no let-up in demands of regional parties to postpone all examinations.

“We demand a thorough investigation into all lapses. It is very imperative. The syndicate must be exposed and dismantled. There should be no further recruitment until the mafia is identified and held accountable,” said Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) president Mehbooba Mufti.

PDP legislator Muntazir Mehdi wrote a letter to J&K Chief Secretary Sajjan Singh Yadav and sought his intervention into “the concerns surrounding the FAA examination and the growing distrust among aspirants”.

“Elected representatives must be heard, and their concerns addressed. That is how people’s faith in democratic institutions is strengthened,” said Mr. Mehdi in the letter.

Kashmir chief cleric Mirwaiz Umar Farooq said such frequent recruitment scams “amount to playing with the futures of thousands of educated young people”.

“Employment opportunities for young people are extremely limited in J&K. When even these few limited employment opportunities fall prey to allegations of scams, fraud and irregularities, it is a grave injustice to the students and young candidates who spend years preparing for these examinations and pin their hopes for a better future on them,” said the Mirwaiz.

J&K Peoples Conference legislator Sajad Gani Lone said, “I don’t understand why cheating happens more in Jammu. The Chief Minister was backing away from the issue by saying the JKSSB was not under his domain. In the last five years, the Board’s record was bad. I think this institution needs a complete overhaul.”
