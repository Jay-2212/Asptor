India, China hold 10-day talks to ease LAC tensions in Arunachal Pradesh

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-23

India and China have concluded a second round of Corps Commander-level talks aimed at easing tensions along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in Arunachal Pradesh and putting in place measures to prevent friction between troops deployed along the frontier ahead of the onset of harsh winter conditions.

Sources in the Ministry of Defence confirmed that the closely guarded discussions, which began on November 8, 2026, a day after Deepavali, continued for nearly 10 days, with a short break in between. The talks were described as cordial, though it remains unclear whether the measures discussed will be implemented on the ground before winter sets in fully.

Lieutenant-General Girish Kalia, General Officer Commanding of the Army’s III Corps, also known as the Spear Corps, led the Indian delegation. The formation is headquartered at Rangapahar near Dimapur in Nagaland. His Chinese counterpart and other military officers participated in the discussions. An official from the Ministry of External Affairs was also present.

The latest round of talks primarily focused on establishing engagement protocols between the two sides, preventing face-offs and misunderstandings between troops deployed along the frontier, maintaining open communication channels and reducing tensions in the eastern sector. Both countries have deployed substantial numbers of troops in the region.

Harsh winter

The discussions assume significance as winter conditions along the LAC in Arunachal Pradesh can become extremely severe, posing considerable challenges to troop sustenance, movement and logistical support. Any delay in implementing the measures discussed could complicate the situation on the ground as temperatures fall and access to forward areas becomes increasingly difficult.

The second round follows the first Corps Commander-level meeting, held on September 6 and 7, 2026, at the Wacha-Damai Border Personnel Meeting (BPM) point in the Kibithoo sector. Wacha is India’s easternmost border personnel meeting point.

The first meeting was convened following reports of tensions in the Taksing area of Upper Subansiri district in Arunachal Pradesh, where troops from the two sides had reportedly come face to face.

The military-level engagement comes amid renewed diplomatic efforts by New Delhi and Beijing to stabilise bilateral relations. In August 2026, National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi, the Special Representatives for boundary negotiations between the two countries, held talks in Beijing.

The situation along the Arunachal Pradesh frontier also came into focus in October 2026, when Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP) Director-General Shatrujeet Kapur, days before his retirement, said Indian and Chinese troops had faced off at multiple locations in the State.
