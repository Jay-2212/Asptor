Adani’s Tarali project: The dispute over its cost and wildlife fee

The Maharashtra Pollution Control Board (MPCB), in a letter to Adani Green Energy Ltd, has directed it to stop excavation and construction work linked to its Tarali hydropower project in Satara district.

It said the “excavation/development work” at Sawarghar village was done without permission from the MPCB and without obtaining environmental clearance. The company has said these are construction-support facilities and do not form part of the permanent project components, which “have the requisite statutory clearances”.

This comes amid the controversy over Adani Green’s affidavit regarding the cost of the 1,500 MW Pumped Storage Project (PSP), proposed on the Tarali dam reservoir.

The Indian Express reported that the renewable energy company ow­ned by the Adani Group had sought to sharply revise the declared cost of its 1,500 MW hydropower project to Rs 56.75 crore from Rs 5,675 crore. This revision proportionally reduced the amount the company was required to deposit towards wildlife conservation and protection to Rs 2.27 crore, from Rs 227 crore.

The project spans 150.74 hectares of non-forest land in Nivade, Tondoshi, Kalambe and Jalu villages in Patan taluka of Maharashtra’s Satara district.

Here’s what to know about the project cost revision, the wildlife conservation deposit, and the questions it has raised.

Why was Adani Green Energy asked to deposit Rs 227 crore?

A letter issued by the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC) on February 11, 2025, recommending the grant of prior Environment Clearance to the Tarali PSP, mentioned the project cost as Rs 5,675 crore.

According to the state forest department, the project falls within the deemed Eco-Sensitive Zone (ESZ) of the Koyna Wildlife Sanctuary in Satara, which is part of the Sahyadri Tiger Reserve (STR). A senior forest officer told The Indian Express that, under existing norms, private companies setting up projects in an ESZ can be asked to deposit up to 4% of the project cost towards conservation and protection.

Accordingly, at its meeting on February 23, 2026, the State Board for Wildlife (SBWL) laid down 15 conditions for the project. One of these required 4% of the project cost to be deposited in the Sahyadri Tiger Reserve’s bank account.

In a letter to Adani Green Energy dated March 24, 2026, Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (Wildlife) M Shrinivas Reddy listed the 15 conditions laid down by the SBWL and asked the company to deposit Rs 227 crore — 4% of the project cost of Rs 5,675 crore — into the Sahyadri Tiger Reserve Conservation Foundation. Reddy also asked the company to submit an affidavit confirming that it would fulfil the 14 other conditions. The project proposal would then be forwarded to the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL) for consideration.

In another letter to Adani Green Energy, dated May 7, 2026, the Deputy Director of Koyna Wildlife Sanctuary said: “Cost of the project passing through the buffer/deemed eco-sensitive zone of Koyna Wildlife Sanctuary is Rs 5,675 crores and its 4% amounting to Rs 227 crores should be deposited in the bank account of Field Director of STR”.

How did the calculation change to Rs 2.27 crore?

On September 8, 2026, Adani Green Energy’s Chief Operating Officer Naresh Telgu submitted a notarised affidavit regarding the project cost to the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (PCCF), Maharashtra.

The affidavit stated: “While submitting the part IV the cost of the project is considered based on analogous estimation, we considered as INR 56.75 crore, request you to please consider the same as project cost.”

On October 12, PCCF Reddy informed Adani Green Energy that his office had accepted the revised project cost of Rs 56.75 crore. He asked the company to immediately deposit 1% of this amount (Rs 56.75 lakh) and pay the remaining 3% (Rs 1.7075 crore) after the NBWL’s approval. The company subsequently deposited Rs 56.75 lakh into the Sahyadri Tiger Reserve’s bank account.

On October 30, Rohan Bhate, Honorary Wildlife Warden of Satara district, registered a complaint on the Prime Minister’s grievance redressal portal and the Maharashtra government’s Aaple Sarkar grievance redressal portal, alleging an “incorrect cost of the project”. On November 6, the MoEF&CC directed the state forest department to investigate the matter on priority. The state forest department has initiated a probe.

What did the company say?

In its first response, an Adani Group spokesperson had said: “All the Tarali PSP elements are within private land ownership across three revenue villages in Patan taluka and have no Forest land. PSPs do not have any adverse impact on the downstream ecology, environment, forest, or wildlife in the area during operations.”

After The Indian Express published its report on November 13, the company issued a statement rejecting the allegation that it had sought to revise the project cost downwards to reduce its statutory obligations.

The spokesperson said: “The allegation that the company sought to revise project costs downwards to diminish statutory obligations is completely misleading. The online proposal in the Forest/wildlife portal was solely for site investigation purposes, while SBWL considered total project costs of Rs 5,675 crore. Since site investigation costs are around 1% of project costs, therefore clarification of the project Rs 56.75 crore representing solely the site investigations and survey costs, for the online proposal was provided.”

The spokesperson added: “Secondly, the allegation that downward revision was intended to lower conservation costs is false. The Tarali PSP is situated entirely on private, non-forest land and 3.2 kms away from the Koyna wildlife sanctuary. It is outside the notified buffer zone of Sahyadri Tiger reserve and in no way impacts or fringes upon the tiger corridor or any notified Eco Sensitive zone. The project does not cause diversion of any protected areas, any reserve forest or notified Eco Sensitive zone.”

Why did the forest department halt project work?

On November 13, within hours of The Indian Express report, the forest department ordered all work at the Tarali PSP site to stop immediately. The department said work had commenced without prior approval from the NBWL, which it considered mandatory because the project site falls within the deemed ESZ of the Sahyadri Tiger Reserve.

The forest department sought a detailed written explanation from the company, asking it to clarify under what authority and on what basis it had commenced work without NBWL approval. The company was also asked to submit all statutory clearance documents and maps related to the Tarali PSP, along with copies of permissions from the competent authority for cutting trees at the project site.

Responding to the order, Adani Green Energy said in a statement: “No component of the Tarali Pumped Storage Project falls within any notified Protected Area and no forest land is involved in the project. Further, necessary permissions/NOCs relating to land use and statutory requirements have already been obtained from the competent authorities.”

The company also said it had secured Environmental Clearance on February 11, 2025, and Consent to Establish on April 7, 2025, after which construction began. It said wildlife clearance proceedings had been initiated, and that its proposal was pending consideration by the NBWL.
