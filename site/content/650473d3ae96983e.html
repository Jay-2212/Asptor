PM Modi says cultural connect can guarantee global peace

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-21

Cultural connect is the biggest guarantee of global peace, Prime Minister Narendra Modi said on Friday.

Mr. Modi was speaking at the first civilisational dialogue forum of the 10 nation Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) grouping. He said and it was important that at a time when war and unrest were dominating the world, India was holding a dialogue on shared civilisations.

The SCO forum is being attended by Pakistan for the first time since the four-day conflict between the two countries in May 2025.

Pakistan has nominated Federal Minister for Climate Change and Environmental Coordination, Mr. Musadik Masood Malik, to lead its delegation to the event being held at the historic Purana Qila of the national capital from November 20 to 22.

Speaking to the media at the SCO forum, Mr. Malik said, “It is a tremendous opportunity for us to have a multilateral dialogue about civilisation. I think cultures and civilisations cut across boundaries, and it is fantastic that we are here and talking about our history, our geography, and our cultures and civilisations...”

The Pakistan Minister is scheduled to address the forum on November 21 along with other country representatives.

Global well-being

On Friday, Mr. Modi highlighted the ancient history of the Purana Qila and said that Indian culture had taught “‘Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam’, that is, the whole world is our family”. “Therefore, our desire for our own well-being is deeply rooted in the spirit of global well-being. We consider mutual harmony the foundation of global well-being.”

He said empires had certainly changed the map of the world, but civilisational dialogues had changed the way the world thinks, adding: “That is why the SCO civilisational summit is not merely a ceremonial platform. It can become a platform for giving the world direction.”

“In recent years, SCO has significantly increased cooperation in security, economy, connectivity. Cultural and civilisational cooperation will strengthen relations among various countries,” Mr. Modi said.

Digitisation of ancient texts

Pointing out that that 21st century technology has created new possibilities for cultural connectivity, Mr. Modi proposed that the SCO countries collaborate on digitisation and translation of ancient texts.

“The museums, libraries and archives in our countries safeguard a priceless heritage spanning thousands of years. Ancient manuscripts, rare texts, historical documents and artworks reveal many facets of our connections”.

“Today, technology offers excellent opportunities to bring this heritage to the new generation. Through joint research projects, we can uncover new aspects of our shared history. This will enable a young person anywhere in the world to understand another country’s cultural heritage much better”.

“Digital platforms have made knowledge sharing, cultural exchange and international collaboration easier than ever before. SCO countries should take full advantage of these new possibilities,” he added.

The SCO civilisational dialogue initiative builds upon the proposal made by the Prime Minister at the SCO Summit in Tianjin, China, in 2025 to establish a dedicated platform for civilisational dialogue under the SCO framework. The proposal was subsequently welcomed in the Bishkek Declaration of SCO.

The forum also has exhibitions showcasing India’s archaeological, artistic and literary heritage titled “ASTITVA – The Essence of Our Existence.” It offers an immersive account of Delhi’s history through archaeological evidence dating from 1200 BCE to 1200 CE, including excavated remains, artefacts, architectural fragments and other significant discoveries.

Four thematic sessions will be held from November 21 to22, bringing together scholars, academics, subject experts and cultural representatives. Discussions will cover cultural expressions such as music and performing arts, textiles, cuisine, traditional games; shared traditions in art and architecture; language and literary traditions, including scripts and manuscripts; and monastic trails and philosophical traditions.
