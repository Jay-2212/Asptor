Kolkata Medical College tense as junior doctors gherao principal; outsiders attack protesting students

By Shrabana Chatterjee • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-19

Tensions rose at the Medical College, Kolkata, late on Wednesday (November 18, 2026) night as junior doctors gheraoed the principal for hours, after which a group of outsiders attacked the protesting doctors and damaged property at the city’s oldest medical college.

A section of students of the medical college blamed the attack on members of the Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP). The right-wing student organisation has denied the allegation. The junior doctors were protesting at Medical College, Kolkata on the issue of a show-cause notice to eight junior doctors who had spoken up against the authorities during the Durga Puja stampede at Chorbagan.

At around 9 p.m. on Wednesday (November 18, 2026), many members of the West Bengal Junior Doctors Front (WBJDF) gave out an SOS call and said that the Medical College was under attack.

“Over 70/80 outside goons entered the hospital and started vandalising, attacking interns and doctors. Many have been physically injured,” the WBJDF said and shared a video of the alleged attack on their social media pages. Meanwhile, ABVP members denied any role in the clashes.

“The incident that took place at the Medical College has no connection whatsoever with ABVP. As per the information received by us, a group of individuals was staging a protest by gheraoing the Principal. During the protest, some students of the college opposed their actions, following which a verbal altercation broke out between the two groups,” Ananta Barui, State Media In-charge, ABVP said in a statement late on Wednesday (November 18, 2026).

The eight junior doctors had been sent a show-cause notice by the authorities on Tuesday (November 17, 2026). The next day, several junior doctors started a sit-in demonstration inside the campus and gheraoed the principal inside his room.

“We, the junior doctors who spoke against the failing government medical system during the Chorbagan incident, have been sent show-cause notices, but we are the ones who managed the situation on the ground. We are being wrongfully targeted,” Shaswata, president of the Medical College student union, said during the protest.

Junior doctors have also asked to see the “investigation report” that led to the show-cause notice issued to them. They asked for the notices to be scrapped if they are not provided with the findings of the said report.

However, Health Minister Sharadwat Mukherjee said that if junior doctors break the law, there are rules to take action against them. “These junior Maoist doctors are students in India, West Bengal. We will take all necessary legal steps; they cannot escape the rule of law. They are anti-nationals and do not abide by any rules or laws,” Mr. Mukherjee had said as the doctors’ protests unfolded.

As the Chorbagan stampede was unfolding and several dead and injured were being rushed to the Medical College, Kolkata, many doctors held a press conference and alleged that the government medical facility lacked emergency and essential medicines.

Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari raised questions about why doctors were holding a press conference during their duty hours. Meanwhile, Mr. Mukherjee called them “dimagi naxals” who “do not abide by any rules and laws”.
