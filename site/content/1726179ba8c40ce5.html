CM’s promise of ₹1 crore a mirage for Sigachi blast victims’ kin in Telangana

By V. Swathi • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-24

Bajrangi Lal Nishad cannot decide whether the stars were in his favour. The 30-year-old from Khapraha village in Jaunpur district of Uttar Pradesh had left his job at Sigachi Industries two years before the deadly blast of June 2025 that killed 54 people, including his brothers Akhilesh Kumar Nishad and Vijay Kumar Nishad.

“Work pressure was so high in the factory that we did not have time to eat. We processed 48 tonnes of material per month. The building was old, there was no safety for workers. While the machine was running, repairs were being done to the roof. A solid chunk of roof almost hit me on the head once,” Mr. Nishad recounted. He believes the collapse of the building may have triggered a fire that led to the blast.

With his Class IX qualification, he found a similar job in another chemical factory at Balanagar.

“In two years there, I witnessed another incident where a blast was averted at the last moment. I quit and left immediately for my village,” he said.

Eight days later, he received the news that both his brothers had been killed. Mr. Nishad is now the sole caregiver for his mother, father, two sisters-in-law and their children.

According to the victims’ kin, the company paid ₹37 lakh in three instalments, apart from provident fund, gratuity, insurance and other benefits, where applicable. The Central Government paid ₹2 lakh, which several families have yet to receive. The State Government disbursed ₹1 lakh when the bodies were handed over.

“My husband was a permanent employee for 20 years. We have got ₹45 lakh so far, including all benefits. I am uneducated and unemployed. I need to pay close to ₹4 lakh per annum as fees for my son’s B.Tech. and daughter’s intermediate. My mother is a widow too, hence I cannot depend on her. My life has become rudderless,” laments B. Malleswari, 39, who lost her husband.

She is still among the fortunate. Families of casual and temporary workers received much less, and it had to be divided among wives and mothers. Rajvanthi Devi, mother of the two Nishad brothers, took 50% of the compensation for each son. The wives and their children shared the rest, which amounted to less than ₹20 lakh.

Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy had announced compensation of ₹1 crore for the family of each victim immediately after the blast. In a letter to the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange two days later, Sigachi Industries committed to an ex gratia of ₹1 crore for the families of the deceased, and full medical and rehabilitation support for the injured.

However, the media later reported that, in response to a public interest litigation, the company told the Telangana High Court that its liability had been discharged with the payment of ₹42 lakh to each family of the deceased, and that the remaining amount was to be paid by the Government.

Puja Kumari, 20, is from a remote village in Rohtas district. She lost her father at 14 and was married off at 15. She has had three Caesarean sections, the last after her husband’s death in the blast.

“I lost one child, and was seven months pregnant when my husband died. The only work I can find in my village is crop harvesting, and I cannot do it after three C-sections,” she says.

According to data collected by the Telangana People’s Joint Action Committee (TPJAC), 19 families did not receive the payout under the Employees’ Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLI) scheme, 13 did not receive Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) dues, and 11 did not receive the Central Government’s compensation.

“We represented them at Praja Bhavan, the Sangareddy district collectorate, the Labour Commissionerate, and the EDLI and EPF offices, but to no avail,” says Kanneganti Ravi of the TPJAC.
