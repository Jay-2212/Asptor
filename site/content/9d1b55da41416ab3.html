Supreme Court to hear contempt pleas in Thirupparankundram lamp-lighting row on Nov. 27

By Krishnadas Rajagopal • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-27

The Supreme Court on Thursday (November 26, 2026) allowed the listing of a contempt petition on November 27 seeking a personal affidavit from the Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary detailing the government’s decision-making, including any role played by Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay in the failure to light the Karthigai Deepam at the Deepathoon on the Thirupparankundram Hill summit in compliance with the November 20 order of the court.

The petition filed by Rama Ravikumar was orally mentioned before a Bench headed by Justice Arvind Kumar at 2 p.m. The petition, settled by senior advocate P. Valliappan and filed by advocate G. Balaji, also sought the immediate preservation and production of all physical and electronic records relating to the implementation of the November 20 order. It has made both Tamil Nadu authorities and the temple administration parties in the contempt case.

Other documents sought to include the November 23 prohibitory order and the resolution of the Board of Trustees, including the opinions purportedly obtained from the Agama experts and Sthanikars. The petition said the court must initiate suo motu criminal contempt proceedings.

The plea also urged the apex court to invoke its plenary powers under Article 142 of the Constitution for lighting the ceremonial lamp at Deepathoon in Thirupparankundram Hill on a substituted date within seven days, preferably during the current Tamil month of Karthigai, subject to the safeguards prescribed in the November 20 order, and under the personal supervision of the State Chief Secretary, with the assistance of an independent Court Commissioner and the Central Reserve Police Force.

In the morning, Justice Kumar verbally conveyed that an application to shift the Karthigai Deepam flame from the Deepa Mandapam to the Deepathoon and contempt petitions filed against State authorities for failing to comply with the November 20 order to light the sacred lamp on the stone pillar would be listed on November 27.

In one of these contempt petitions, Hindu Dharma Parishad has sought the initiation of action against T.G. Vinay, Commissioner, Hindu Religious & Charitable Endowments Department; Nishant Krishna, District Collector, Madurai; and S. Rajendiran, Madurai Commissioner of Police.

The Hindu parties are seeking contempt action despite Mr. Krishna’s preliminary report of November 25, which stated that the district authorities had made all arrangements to facilitate the lighting of the Karthigai Deepam at the Deepathoon in compliance with the November 20 order. However, the Board of Trustees at the Arulmigu Subramaniaswamy Temple had not authorised persons for the lamp-lighting team on November 24.

Another contempt plea, drawn by advocate Yashika Anand and filed through advocate Narender Kumar Verma, said the non-compliance with the apex court order of November 20 to light the lamp on November 24 at the Deepathoon as a “limited one-time arrangement” only for this year was not only contumacious but also a patent violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.

“It amounts to invasion of legitimate expectation duly sanctioned by the Supreme Court,” the petition said.

A similar contempt petition filed by an advocate, V. Ramasubbu, represented by K.V. Muthukumar, pointed out that the court’s direction to keep out unauthorised persons from the lamp-lighting ceremony was taken advantage of by the State authorities to issue a John Doe injunction on the general public.

The Hindu parties’ separate application, orally mentioned by senior advocate Guru Krishnakumar, is seeking directions to the State and temple authorities “to light the lamp at the Deepathoon either by igniting from and out of the same Deepam which is still burning at the Deepa Mandapam near the Uchipillaiyar Temple or make necessary arrangements in terms of order dated November 20, 2026”.

The application said the four Sthanikars had denied giving any opinion to light the lamp at the Deepa Mandapam nor were they consulted.

“The State has sought to take shelter under the so-called resolution of the Board of Trustees to not comply with the Supreme Court order,” the application, filed through advocate V.C. Shukla, said.

The application had said it was curious that the State “trotted out” a resolution passed by the Board of Trustees to light the lamp at the Deepa Mandapam at the “last minute in a brazen attempt to not comply with the orders of the Supreme Court”.
