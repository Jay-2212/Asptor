Committed to goal of J&K’s State restoration, its constitutional guarantees: Omar

By Peerzada Ashiq • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-08-15

Terming federalism as “strength of the country”, J&K Chief Minister Omar Abdullah, said on the 80th Independence Day on Saturday (August 15, 2026) in Srinagar, that J&K should be returned “whatever was taken away from it”, in an oblique reference to the special status under Article 370.

“Whatever was taken away from us should be returned to us. The idea of federalism should be pursued seriously by empowering States, not mere by granting Statehood status. Federalism should not remain limited to words but should be reflected in practice,” said Mr. Abdullah, in his I-Day speech, in Srinagar.

Without mentioning Article 370, the J&K Chief Minister was pitching for the return of pre-2019 status for J&K.

Mr. Abdullah also highlighted the challenges of the States. “Federalism suffers when central agencies interfere in matters falling within the States’ domain or when powers vested with States are eroded or exercised by central agencies,” said Mr. Abdullah.

Referring to the national-level examinations such as NEET, NET and JEE, Mr. Abdullah said, “They have changed the manner in which admissions were conducted (earlier by the States itself). National examinations were appropriate for national institutions such as IITs, IIMs and NIFT, while State educational institutions should have the right to conduct their own examinations and admissions.”

CM on protest in Pakistan Occupied Kashmir

Mr. Abdullah also referred to the situation in Pakistan Occupied Kashmir (PoK), where scores of protestors died in security forces firing in the past two months. 

“The condition of people in that part of Jammu and Kashmir was a matter of sadness and regret. They are our own people. The J&K Assembly has seats earmarked for them. If 2019 had not happened to J&K, people there (in PoK) would have been agitating to join this part,” said Mr. Abdullah.

Meanwhile, Mr. Abdullah also paid tributes to National Conference founder Sheikh Mohammad Abdullah and termed him as the “greatest leader” and the “most courageous person” of J&K. “Any Independence Day reflection in J&K would remain incomplete without remembering his contribution,” he added.

He also remembered NC leader Master Abdul Aziz killed in Muzaffarabad while resisting the tribal invasion into J&K in 1947.

J&K Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha, who hoisted the National Flag at Lok Bhavan, said the truest tribute we can pay to our martyrs and freedom fighters “is to resolve to work with integrity and safeguard our hard-earned freedom”.

The Lieutenant Governor said citizens must unite to foster an inclusive society that prioritszes the public interest and extends compassion to the underprivileged.

Calling upon all the citizens of UT of Jammu and Kashmir to dedicate themselves in nation-building, Mr. Sinha said, “Transparency and accountability in governance was a necessity.”

He also congratulated all the brave personnel of the J&K Police and CAPFs who received gallantry medals on the eve of Independence Day.
