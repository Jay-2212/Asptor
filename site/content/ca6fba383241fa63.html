Graca Machel conferred Indira Gandhi Prize; Sonia Gandhi recalls shared ideals of social justice

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-20

Mozambican politician and women’s rights advocate Graca Machel was conferred the 2025 Indira Gandhi Prize for Peace, Disarmament and Development on Thursday, coinciding with the former Prime Minister’s 109th birth anniversary.

Sonia Gandhi, who heads the Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust, presented the award, which comprised a citation and a cash prize of ₹1 crore.

Ms. Gandhi said Ms. Machel’s life and work reflected Indira Gandhi’s ideals, particularly the commitment to social justice, human dignity and equality.

Both Indira Gandhi and Ms. Machel believed in people’s power to shape their own futures, she said.

Their lives, Ms. Gandhi noted, demonstrated that leadership was measured by the courage to challenge injustice and the commitment to bring about change.

Tracing Ms. Machel’s journey from Mozambique’s liberation struggle to her work on women’s and children’s rights, Ms. Gandhi recalled that Ms. Machel became the country’s first Minister of Education and Culture after it gained independence in 1975. During her tenure, school participation rose from 40% to over 90% among boys and 75% among girls.

Ms. Machel was also the wife of Mozambique’s first President, Samora Machel, serving as First Lady until his death in 1986. In 1998, she married former South African President Nelson Mandela, becoming the only woman to have been First Lady of two countries.

“Education, for her, meant giving a new generation the opportunity to shape its future,” Ms. Gandhi said, drawing a parallel with Indira Gandhi’s emphasis on education and nation-building.

Recalling the former Prime Minister’s words, she said, “Peace, like freedom and progress, is indivisible.”

Ms. Gandhi also referred to Ms. Machel’s landmark 1996 United Nations report on the impact of armed conflict on children, which helped shape the international response to the suffering of children in conflict zones.

“Her work reminds us that peace is not simply the absence of conflict. It must also enable communities to rebuild their lives and look towards the future,” she said.

Ms. Gandhi said there could be no lasting peace without justice and no meaningful development where violence and insecurity prevailed.

The award ceremony took place at Jawahar Bhawan. Former National Security Advisor Shivshankar Menon headed the jury.

In her acceptance speech, Ms. Machel said India and Africa shared a legacy that needed to be carried forward. “Our lives and our families have been connected across generations, through shared convictions, public service, friendship, and profound personal loss,” she said.

She said the next chapter of India-Africa relations should be written not merely in the language of trade and diplomacy but in that of “human possibility”. The two regions’ futures were interconnected, she added, and their shared humanity was greater than anything that divided them.

Former Vice-President Hamid Ansari, Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, former Rajasthan Chief Minister Ashok Gehlot, and former Union Ministers Anand Sharma and P. Chidambaram attended the award ceremony.

Earlier in the day, Mr. Kharge, Ms. Gandhi and party general secretary (organisation) K.C. Venugopal paid floral tributes at the former Prime Minister’s memorial, Shakti Sthal.

Mr. Kharge described Indira Gandhi as a symbol of “indomitable courage, struggle and strong leadership”, saying her contribution to protecting India’s unity, integrity and sovereignty would continue to inspire generations.

Congress general secretary Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, in a post on X, wrote: “Happy Birthday, tigress! There will never be another like you.”

Meanwhile, Mr. Venugopal recalled Indira Gandhi’s role in the nationalisation of banks, the Green Revolution and the 1971 war, saying she had demonstrated that India’s unity was protected by courage, not fear.
