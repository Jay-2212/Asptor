Plea in SC says ghost Form 7s haunt Mustafabad constituency during Delhi SIR

By Krishnadas Rajagopal • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

Congress leader Ali Mehdi approached the Supreme Court on Monday alleging that Assembly Constituency-69 (AC-69) of Mustafabad is seeing a “substantial” amount of ghost Form 7s seeking deletion of voters in the ongoing Delhi SIR, and a crisis of electoral integrity is brewing.

The writ petition filed through advocate Shariq Ahmed, and highlighting multiple reports by The Hindu, said voters whose names feature for deletion on the grounds of death, absence and permanent shifting were found to be very much alive, present and staying put at their recorded addresses on the electoral roll.

The persons in whose names Form 7s have been filed for deletion of these voters deny having filed or signed on the applications. The petition alleged that ghost Form 7s seemed to have been filed by phantom objectors in the Mustafabad constituency.

“The integrity of an electoral roll is compromised not only when an ineligible name remains upon it. It is equally compromised when an eligible elector is removed from it on a fact which never existed or upon an objection which the supposed objector never made. The present record discloses the latter danger in its stark form,” the writ petition said.

It said persons declared dead were alive, while persons said to have shifted were found at home. Thirty-seven applications examined by the authorities were found to be false or fabricated.

“The existence of a genuine objector and the existence of a genuine ground for deletion — In Mustafabad, both are under serious doubt at the same time,” Mr. Ahmed submitted.

Section 22 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 requires the Electoral Registration Officer to be satisfied, after proper verification of the facts, before an entry can be corrected or deleted.

Rule 13(2) of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 requires an objection to the proposed inclusion of a name or an application for deletion to be made in Form 7 and to be preferred by a person whose own name is included in the electoral roll.

Form 7 requires identification of the applicant, identification of the elector objected to, the particular ground for deletion and a declaration as to the truth of the particulars stated.

The guidelines accompanying the form place the onus of substantiating the ground upon the applicant.

“There must be an identifiable person who has actually invoked the statutory process and there must be a factual ground capable of sustaining the deletion sought. If either fails, the foundation of the objection fails,” the petition said.

The petition drew attention to an investigation by The Hindu which reported that 28 electors against whom deletion applications had been submitted were physically traced to the addresses at which they were enrolled.

The petition said that on November 5, an analysis by the Hindu of 1,021 Form 7 applications received in AC-69 Mustafabad found that 787 applications stood in the names of 20 persons recorded as BJP-appointed Booth Level Agents.

The petition urged the court to direct the Election Commission to initiate safeguards and correct the electoral roll before an error found a place in the final electoral roll.

“The final electoral roll has not yet been published. Under the presently revised schedule, claims and objections continue until November 25, disposal continues until December 15, and final publication is scheduled for December 22,” the petition said.
