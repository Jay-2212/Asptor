Balendra Shah visits India: What is on the table, from security to AI to cricket

• INDIAN EXPRESS EXPLAINED • 2026-11-25

Nepal’s Prime Minister Balendra Shah, popularly known as Balen Shah, is in India on his first bilateral visit abroad since taking office in March. He will meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi for bilateral talks on Monday, in their first in-person meeting.

Nepal’s Prime Ministers generally make their first visit to India, and quite early in their tenure. Yet, much is unusual about this visit, starting with the PM himself.

‘Nepal first’ PM

The 36-year-old rapper-turned-politician Balen Shah came to power on the back of massive youth protests, promising a break from Nepal’s old politics of patronage and dependence on regional big powers.

Shah was known for his administrative skills and development work as the Kathmandu Mayor. After taking the top office, in an effort to project difference from the old guard, Shah told ministers and officials to focus on domestic issues. He went so far as to issue a diktat: no foreign visits and no meetings with senior officials and ministers from other countries, unless with a President or a Prime Minister.

Thus, when US envoy for South and Central Asia Sergio Gor visited Kathmandu in April-early May, Shah’s office did not schedule a meeting with him. Around the same time, the visit of India’s Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri to Kathmandu was cancelled as Shah did not agree to a meeting.

Then, Shah said both India and Nepal had encroached on each other’s territory, and sought the involvement of China and UK to address the dispute, something India strongly rejected.

The June visit of Shishir Khanal, Nepal’s Foreign Minister, and Rabi Lamichhane, the chief of Shah’s party, Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP), smoothened matters and paved the way for Shah’s visit. His visit is being seen as an acknowledgement in Nepal of the need for continued engagement with India.

The strategic logic for engagement is clear as daylight: India is Nepal’s largest trade, transit and investment partner, Nepal enjoys duty-free access to the Indian market, and India is Nepal’s biggest export destination, receiving about 80% of Nepal’s goods exports. India is also the largest source of foreign investment in Nepal, accounting for almost 1/3rd of the total FDI received by the country.

Thus, even as Shah seeks to differentiate himself from his predecessors, there are key areas of cooperation which India and Nepal will look at.

Key areas of economic cooperation between India and Nepal

Energy: Bilateral cooperation in the power sector has been on an upward trajectory, with developments like the agreement for long term power trade that envisages the import of up to 10,000 MW power from Nepal to India over a period of 10 years. India currently imports about 1200 MW of power from Nepal annually.

Indian entities are involved in the development of several hydropower projects in Nepal, including the 900 MW Arun-3 Hydroelectric project under construction.

Energy supplies to Nepal were made more efficient and effective through a cross-border petroleum pipeline commissioned in 2019 between Motihari and Amlekhgunj. Phase-2 of this pipeline has also been implemented. Both sides are now exploring the development of new pipelines and terminals, and Balen Shah’s visit will look into this aspect.

Innovation ecosystem and AI: With Shah representing the youth, the start-up culture is a major area that India is likely to focus on.

Since 2025, several Nepali startups have received training through the India-Nepal Startup Partnership Network (IN-SPAN) in collaboration with IIT Madras and its Incubation Centre Pravartak.

Both sides are working to establish a collaborative framework for advancing Language AI, digital public infrastructure, and inclusive digital ecosystems across India and Nepal.

Education: For the first few decades after Indian independence, a majority of Nepal’s senior leaders were educated at Banaras Hindu University (BHU). But in recent years, Nepal’s youngsters are choosing Australia, Europe, Thailand, Singapore, and Dubai.

Scholarships provided by India for Nepali nationals provide extensive coverage, from school education to the Doctorate level, with 1,500 annual scholarships.

With Shah seen as the voice of the youth, the Indian government and Nepal are looking at the possibility of an IIT campus in Nepal.

Sports: Cricket has found huge enthusiasm among the Nepalese youth. In the past three years, there has been growth in the India-Nepal partnership in sports cooperation, particularly in cricket. In fact, the Nepal Premier League is said to be a huge money spinner, next to India’s IPL.

Tourism: India is the largest source of foreign tourists to Nepal. The data of air arrivals in the last Nepali financial year reflected 311,000 Indian visitors. This does not include the large number of visitors arriving by road. Both sides are thus working to promote shared circuits such as the Ramayana Circuit and the Buddhist Circuit.

The new direct passenger train services between Janakpurdham and Ayodhya, whose trial run was on November 5, will further facilitate religious tourism between India and Nepal.

India and Nepal already enjoy extensive cooperation in culture. Indian pilgrims visit Pashupatinath, Muktinath, Janaki Mandir and Lumbini, while Nepali pilgrims travel to Kashi, the Char Dham, Bodh Gaya and the Kumbh.

India-Nepal border management question

The most challenging issue during the visit is likely to be border management. While India and Nepal have contested some border areas for decades, in 2020, under former prime minister KP Oli, Nepal officially issued a new political map, showing Kalapani, Limpiadhura and Lipulekh in Uttarakhand as its territory. This locks all Nepal’s Prime Ministers in a position they find difficult to walk back from. Also, China has long sought a larger influence in Nepal, including over its boundary issues.

However, despite the differences, India and Nepal enjoy robust ties in defence and security. With the issue of Agniveers being resolved — by giving them a pathway for permanent commission — some misgivings among the Gorkha soldiers have been assuaged. India and Nepal also cooperate closely in areas such as countering narcotics, cross-border crimes, money laundering etc.

Balen Shah’s visit to India will be a key moment for a reset in the relationship. The effort is to bring the ties back on track, with a new PM who has been vocal about not being “pro-India” or “pro-China”, but “pro-Nepal”. The political establishment in Nepal is new and different from what India has dealt with, but their aspirational and ambitious political language is what India speaks, and well understands.
