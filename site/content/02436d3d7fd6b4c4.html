    Tamil Nadu's pleas over Cauvery water dispute dismissed

    The Supreme Court on Friday (November 20) dismissed two pleas of the Tamil Nadu Government seeking a direction to Karnataka for the release of 11 thousand million cubic (TMC) feet of Cauvery river water.

    In its pleas, the Tamil Nadu Government sought directions to Karnataka to make sure that the backlog of 11 TMC of Cauvery water as on October 7 is cleared immediately and ensure the same at the rate of 1.3 TMC per day in 10 days at Billigundulu, a key water-measuring station on the banks of the river along the Karnataka-Tamil Nadu border.

    On October 12, the top court reserved its decision on the two applications of the Tamil Nadu Government.

    SC dismisses Tamil Nadu's pleas seeking release of 11 TMC of water from Cauvery river

    The Supreme Court has dismissed Tamil Nadu’s application claiming a shortfall of 11.719 thousand million cubic feet in the Cauvery water, which flows from Billigundulu, according to the State’s formula.

    Karnataka had opposed the application on the ground that the CWMA had not ordered it. 

    The judgment was pronounced by a Bench led by Justice Vikram Nath.

    Karnataka was directed to release 6,000 cusecs of water a day to Tamil Nadu

    The Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA), which met in Delhi in September 2026, has upheld the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee’s (CWRC) direction to Karnataka to ensure flows to the neighbouring Tamil Nadu at a rate of 6,000 cusecs a day for the next 15 days.

    The CWRC had issued this direction earlier in the day and Karnataka had challenged it before the CWMA. However, the CWMA endorsed the CWRC’s decision and asked Karnataka to ensure a flow of 6,000 cusecs a day at Biligundlu inter-State water gauge for 15 days, beginning from 8 a.m. on September 9. The total quantum of water that has been ordered to be released amounts to approximately 7.8 tmcft.

    How can Karnataka clear the Cauvery backlog amid drought conditions, SC asks Tamil Nadu

    In an earlier hearing on October 12, 2026, the Supreme Court had questioned Tamil Nadu on how Karnataka could be expected to make good the backlog in the release of Cauvery water when the latter was itself grappling with water scarcity and drought conditions.

    “A backlog can be covered only when there is excess water. If there is less rainfall and there is scarcity, how do you expect them to fulfil the backlog? They are also reeling from water scarcity. If they have excess water and are not giving it to you, then only can you recover the backlog,” a Division Bench headed by Justice Vikram Nath observed.
