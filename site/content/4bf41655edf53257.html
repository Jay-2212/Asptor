SCO Civilisational Dialogue ‘leaves options open’ for Pakistan SCO meets next year, say sources

By Suhasini Haidar • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-23

The visit of Pakistan Minister for Environment and Climate Change, Musadik Malik, to India, the first by any senior Pakistani official since 2023, was a “Shanghai Cooperation Organisation” (SCO) initiative, not an Indian one alone, said officials as the government came under criticism from the Opposition.

However, former Indian diplomats who served in Pakistan said the visit and the invitation to the SCO Civilisational Dialogue in Delhi and the SCO Summit to be held in Pakistan next year could be seen as building a window of opportunity for “common civility” and providing a multilateral platform for the region, when bilateral moves, and the SAARC Summit are not taking place. 

Ministry of External Affairs officials have stressed that there were no “bilateral” or one-to-one meetings during the three-day event hosted at Delhi’s iconic Purana Kila (Old Fort) monument. Ministers from six countries – Pakistan, Tajikistan, Nepal, Azerbaijan, Iran, and the Kyrgyz Republic – and Deputy Ministers from eight other countries participated in the forum.

Government officials pointed out that the event allows India a chance to highlight its cultural prowess and strong roots and furthers India’s common links with other members of the SCO, which also see themselves as “civilizational” states. It is also significant that India has carved out a space for itself in the year when Pakistan is the Chair of the SCO, they said, and is leading a shift in the grouping’s more security-oriented and land-locked focus to a more South Asian, oceanic and people-to-people linked focus. 

They suggested that such meetings could also pave the way for next year, when External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar, National Security Advisor Ajit Doval and Defence Minister Rajnath Singh will all be invited to Pakistan for SCO meetings chaired by Pakistan, and Prime Minister Narendra Modi will be invited to the Heads of Council meeting to be held in the third quarter of 2027. Government officials said it was too early to speak about participation in these meetings in Pakistan yet, but that a decision closer to the dates would only be possible if options were kept open at present, and SCO events in the run-up to the Summit are kept away from the “negativity” of bilateral relations. 

“The meeting was in an SCO context so it would be inappropriate to read into it any bilateral content, leave alone a breakthrough,” said former Indian High Commissioner to Islamabad TCA Raghavan (2013-2015). He added, however, that “given the precarious and fraught state of India- Pakistan relations, an episode of common civility and normalcy is a plus and, even without intending to, creates possibilities.” 

Former High Commissioner to Islamabad Ajay Bisaria (2017-2019), who was the last to serve in Pakistan before both sides downgraded ties in 2019, said that Mr. Malik’s visit to India represented “more than a formality but less than a thaw” and demonstrated how such multilateral events allow India and Pakistan to “tolerate each other’s presence, even when they won’t engage directly”.  When asked about India’s attendance at SCO events in Pakistan next year, Mr. Bisaria said that the “ball is in Pakistan’s court” to “dial down both terrorism and rhetoric” if it hopes for Indian leaders to travel there. Significantly, he said such an event could be an opportunity to revive the SAARC process as well, that has been suspended for a decade. 

On Friday, Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the first SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum, making the point that while “empires had redrawn maps”, such dialogues had “transformed and enriched global thought”. Mr. Modi had proposed the Dialogue at the SCO Summit in Tianjin in 2025, and the proposal was adopted at the SCO Summit in Bishkek in October 2026, with the declaration announcing that it would be held in Delhi this month.

The criticism was particularly sharp over images this weekend of Mr. Modi and the Pakistani Minister seated in the first row, as well as of Culture Minister and host Gajendra Singh Shekhawat holding a conversation with Mr. Malik at a lunch table.

Several Opposition leaders, including former Ministers Salman Khurshid and Manish Tewari, had accused the government of “inconsistency” in its policy towards Pakistan, particularly the Modi government’s comments that “talks and terrorism” would not go together.

“This is a multilateral event within the SCO framework, for which invitations have been extended to all members of the SCO, including Pakistan,” MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said, underlining at briefings held in the past week that the government has not changed its position on other outstanding bilateral issues with Pakistan including over the Pahalgam terror attack, and the suspension of the Indus Water Treaty.

India and Pakistan have at present no bilateral dialogue between them, and since 2016, when the Modi government decided to boycott SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation), they have dropped the regional engagement as well. However, New Delhi continues to make an exception for the SCO, founded in 1999 by Russia, China, and Central Asian states as a security forum to which India and Pakistan were inducted in 2017, and Iran and Belarus subsequently. In 2024, Mr. Jaishankar had travelled to Islamabad to represent India at the SCO Heads of Government Summit.
