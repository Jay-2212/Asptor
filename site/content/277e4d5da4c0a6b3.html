Past El Niño years expose gaps in Keralam’s power crisis narrative

By Dhinesh Kallungal • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-13

Although the Keralam government attributes the emerging El Niño across the Pacific Ocean and associated poor rainfall to the current power crisis in the State, which has forced the government to implement peak-hour power restrictions from 6.15 p.m. to 12.45 a.m., data from the India Meteorological department (IMD) on previous El Niño years and their impact on monsoon rainfall in the country, particularly Keralam, do not support the government’s claim.

During 1951-2026, there were only 17 El Niño years in the country. The weather phenomenon—a climate pattern characterised by the abnormal warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean, which weakens trade winds and leads to reduced monsoon rainfall and drier conditions over the Indian subcontinent—resulted in deficient monsoon rainfall in Keralam only on a limited number of occasions (1965, 1972, 1987, 2002, 2015, 2023 and 2026).

In 2023

The worst-affected El Niño year in the recorded history of Keralam was 2023, when the State recorded a rainfall deficit of around 34%. Keralam received 1,326.1 mm of rainfall in 2023 against the average of 2,028.7 mm during the four-month southwest monsoon season. However, no power restrictions were imposed in the State that year.

With close to three weeks still left in the current season (2026), Keralam had received 1,373.5 mm of rainfall as of September 12, a shortfall of 26%. The IMD has also forecast moderate heavy rainfall across the State during the second half of the month, indicating that the deficit may not widen significantly. IMD data further suggest that the current year is likely to be the second mildest El Niño monsoon-deficient year in Keralam.

Heatwave

In 2024, the year following the El Niño year, Keralam recorded its first heatwave, with Palakkad meeting the heatwave criteria. Despite the severe El Niño-deficient monsoon season followed by a harsh summer, the State did not impose power restrictions in 2024.

The same was the case in 2015 and 2016. In 2015, an El Niño year, Keralam recorded a rainfall deficit of 25%. This was followed by the State’s second-hottest summer in 2016 and another severely deficient monsoon. Although 2016 was not an El Niño year, Keralam received only 1,353.3 mm of rainfall, a shortfall of 33%.

Despite these conditions, Keralam did not face a power crisis of a magnitude similar to the current one. The rainfall data, therefore, raise questions over the management of the power sector and suggest that factors beyond poor rainfall may have contributed to the present crisis.

Speaking to The Hindu, an IMD source said there was no direct one-to-one connection between the dry spell in the Indian subcontinent and an El Niño year. Among the 17 El Niño years, the country as a whole received normal to above-normal rainfall on six occasions.

Advance planning

However, atmospheric patterns and past trends indicate the possibility of a dry spell in the country, including Keralam, during an El Niño year. The State can better manage such a situation through effective advance planning. The El Niño alert is usually issued by various agencies well in advance, allowing State governments to prepare for a likely dry spell, the IMD sources said.
