AAIB probe report into AI 171 crash at ‘fag end’, Centre tells Supreme Court

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-18

The Centre, yet again, in the Supreme Court on Wednesday (November 18, 2026), sought “two or three weeks” more for the publication of the final report by the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) on the Air India Flight 171 crash.

The crash at the Ahmedabad Airport killed 12 crew members and 229 passengers on June 12, 2025. 

On July 28, the court was told that the report would be published anytime between the “middle of September”, with a buffer till the “end of October”.

“It is at the fag end,” Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, for the Union government and the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), said.

Advocates Prashant Bhushan and Pranav Sachdeva, appearing for petitioner-NGO, Safety Matters Foundation, said the authorities have been repeatedly seeking adjournments in the case. Mr. Mehta, on the other hand, denied any attempt to deliberately delay the report.

In July, the apex court had put on hold petitioners’ plea for a Court of Inquiry or a “fair, transparent, and technically sound investigation” in order to the wait for the report.

One of the petitioners is 91-year-old Pushkar Raj Sabharwal, the father of Commander Sumeet Sabharwal, one of the pilots on the ill-fated aircraft.

At the time, the court had clarified that the trajectory of the litigation would depend on whether the AAIB report on the crash would be “well-merited and accepted.”

The petitions, which include one by a pilots’ body, have argued that an investigation of such a magnitude ought to have gone to an independent agency in accordance with international protocols to which India was a signatory.

However, the apex court had opted to wait for the report, reasoning that the AAIB probe had “substantially progressed.”

On Wednesday (November 18), Mr. Bhushan submitted that the probe had now been on for a year-and-a-half now. He had earlier alleged “conflict of interest”, saying that out of the five members in the investigation team, three were from the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA). He said even the AAIB rules mandated a permanent body without members from the DGCA.

At the time, Mr. Mehta had explained that experts from the countries to which some of the passengers had belonged to were involved in the investigation.

He had said the investigation had taken time as each component of the aircraft had to be sent to the manufacturers, wherever they were located, for checking.
