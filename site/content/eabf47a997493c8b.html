Why is November 26 celebrated as Constitution Day in India

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-26

Constitution Day, also known as Samvidhan Diwas, is observed every year on November 26 to commemorate the adoption of the Constitution of India in 1949 by the Constituent Assembly.

The Constitution came into force on January 26, 1950, two months later, making India a republic. However, November 26 is celebrated as the Constitution Day to commemorate the moment the Constitution of India was formally adopted for “We, the People of India”.

But the event ‘Constitution Day’ was started only a little over a decade ago when Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced it in October 2015 while laying the foundation stone for the Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Memorial in Mumbai. Prior to this, it was called ‘National Law Day’, first initiated by former Supreme Court Bar Association (SCBA) president Laxmi Mall Singhvi in 1979.

Drafting of the Constitution

Members of the Constituent Assembly debated the governing charter of Independent India for over three years with an initial draft prepared in February 1948 by Constitutional Advisor B.N. Rau (following an earlier draft preamble presented in October 1947).

Dr. Ambedkar’s drafting committee revised the draft in 1948 and the Assembly spent a year discussing it. They considered more than 2000 amendments and accepted several before adopting the final draft. Handwritten in both English and Hindi, with artwork by Nandalal Bose of Santiniketan, the Constitution was signed by Constituent Assembly members on January 24, 1950, when Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected unopposed as India’s first President.

Shaping the Preamble

Dr. Ambedkar drafted a Preamble in 1948, drawing on the December 1946 Objectives Resolution and the July 1946 Declaration of the Expert’s Committee. It added Dr. Ambedkar’s own emphasis on fraternity and human dignity to establish India as a sovereign democratic republic securing Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.

The original Preamble, adopted on November 26, 1949, did not contain the words ‘socialist’ and ‘secular’. These were inserted in 1976 through the 42nd Constitution Amendment. In November 2024, the Supreme Court upheld the inclusion after a batch of petitions challenged the validity of the inclusion of the words ‘socialist, secular’ in the Preamble. The SC stated that ‘secular’ denotes a Republic that upholds equal respect for all religions and ‘socialist’ represents a Republic dedicated to eliminating all forms of exploitation (whether social, political, or economic).

The Hindu article, dated November 26, 2025, notes that Dr. Ambedkar “envisioned and crafted the Constitution as a site of struggle where seminal issues could be settled through constitutional means”. He shaped the Preamble as an instrument of social revolution grounded in the 19th and early 20th century anti-caste movement, it further argues.

Core values, Rights and Duties

As the supreme law of India, the Constitution guarantees citizens of India fundamental rights such as freedom of speech and expression, freedom of conscience and religion, protection against arbitrary arrest and detention, freedom of movement and cultural and educational guarantees, while also outlining fundamental duties in Part IVA. It emphasizes the principles of Justice (social, economic and political), Liberty, Equality and Fraternity alongside abolition of untouchability and Article 15’s bar on state discrimination on grounds including religion, race, caste, sex, and place of birth.

The Hindu article from November 26, 2022, contends that these provisions underpin our daily activities, travel, career choices, reading or watching the news, and even questioning why the police stop us, and underscores the importance of treating the Constitution Day as more than a form of symbolism. It particularly emphasizes teaching constitutional values and fraternity at home and in schools, and resolving socio-economic conflicts through constitutional methods, among others.
