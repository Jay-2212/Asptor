The language that works

By Amal Paul • THE HINDU OPINION • 2026-09-13

Years ago, during my ophthalmology training, there was this anecdote about a medical student from North India trying to assess the vision of a Tamil-speaking patient using a Snellens chart. With her broken Tamil and dumb-charades skills, she somehow managed to make the patient understand that he has to stand at a distance of 6 metres, and read out the letters printed on the chart.

However, to her utmost frustration, the old man claimed inability to read even the biggest of the letters, despite repeated attempts and manoeuvring. The budding doctor was furious, as the patient was obviously not as blind as the results of his responses seemed to suggest. Convinced that the old man was trying to have fun at her expense, she was about to vent her full fury at him, when someone handed her a Snellens chart with Tamil letters.

To her immediate surprise and relief, the visual acuity of the old man miraculously improved from ‘near total blindness’ to ‘near normal’ in a matter of seconds!

“It’s already 8.30,” came a loud announcement from the kitchen. I looked at the clock for a moment, and continued reading the newspaper.

“The time is 8.40.” The decibel strength seemed to double with each passing minute. I picked the cue instantly, kept the newspaper folded on the teapoy, and stretched myself to have a peep through the curtains into the dining room. The breakfast was neatly arranged on the dining table. Plates were in place. Glasses were filled. But the “chief guest” was not to be seen anywhere in the vicinity.

I prepared myself for the thunderous storms to follow.

“Your school-cab will come shortly. You need to finish the breakfast and get ready before that,”my wife pleaded with our nine-year-old boy, who was immersed in the children’s fortnightly which arrived that morning. I sharpened my ears to eavesdrop. Contrary to my expectation, a hollow silence followed; no response, no blasts, no whining, not even a whisper.

In a moment, the curtains were flung open, and my wife stormed into the sitting room. I picked up a random magazine from the teapoy and sat stiff, pretending to be unaware of the goings-on in the dining room.

“Our son needs to meet his aunty at the earliest,” she declared without an iota of ambiguity.

“Why? What’s the issue?” I was perplexed (the aunty whom she was referring to was our sister-in-law, an ENT surgeon).

“He has got selective deafness! Whatever I say doesn’t seem to reach his ears!”

I smiled at her. I could sense the frustration behind the veneer of witticism.

The old story of the visual acuity test flashed in my memory.

“Darling, the problem is not with his hearing. It’s just that he’s just not able to understand your language.”

She stared at me perplexed. I continued, “For the auditory stimulus to evoke a meaningful response, it’s not enough that the hearing apparatus is intact. The brain should be able to perceive the spoken language.”

“You mean to say that there’s some problem with the little one’s brain?! Should we consult the neurologist next door then?” By this time, a veil of alarm was spreading over her countenance.

I sensed the danger, and decided to act without further ado. I sprang from the chair and appeared before the boy in a jiffy. He seemed to be so engrossed in the book that my stormy entry failed to make any visible impact. My wife had followed me, wondering what I am up to.

As she looked on curiously, I spoke to him in a low tone, as soft, serene and composed as one could imagine. “Son, sorry about interrupting your serious reading for a couple of seconds. Just wanted to tell you this. When your cab comes at 9 o’ clock, as you run like a hare with the school bag in hand, just in case the breakfast happens to be unfinished as it was the other day, all the routine online entertainments will remain suspended for the rest of the week. Hope that won’t inconvenience you. By the way, which story are you reading?”

Even before I finished, the boy disappeared from the chair and landed at the dining table in a flash. I turned triumphantly at my wife, as she stood in sheer awe and disbelief.

Moral of the story: For a communication to yield desired results, choosing the most effective language is the first and foremost step. Impactful words may not always need the backing of a high-decibel broadcast.
