Rahul putting India’s identity, developmental journey at stake to seize power: Anurag Thakur

By PTI • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) MP Anurag Thakur on Saturday (November 21, 2026) criticised Rahul Gandhi, alleging that the Congress leader was playing a “dangerous gamble” by putting India’s “identity and developmental journey” at stake to seize power, after failing to gain it through democratic means.

“Rahul Gandhi’s current state is like that of a child at a fair who, upon failing to get the toy he desires, starts crying, throwing tantrums and speaking ill of everyone,” Mr. Thakur told reporters in New Delhi, taking a swipe at the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha.

“Rahulji, power is not gained through a gamble, it is earned through service,” the former Union Minister said, adding, “(Prime Minister) Narendra Modi has rendered that service. He came to power through penance, austerity and sacrifice and is serving the people.”

His remarks came against the backdrop of Mr. Gandhi’s “compromised PM” jibe at Mr. Modi and his remarks on the government’s policies during a recent visit to the United States.

Speaking at a fireside chat at Columbia University in New York earlier this week, Mr. Gandhi said India was sitting on an “economic disaster”, with massive unemployment, a broken manufacturing base and deep-rooted problems in its education system, warning that the country risked getting into serious trouble if it continued on the current economic path.

He also claimed India’s economic model “stopped giving results almost a decade ago”, and criticised initiatives such as “Make in India” and “Start-up India”, claiming they had failed to revive the country’s “broken” economic backbone.

Mr. Thakur criticised Mr. Gandhi for “abusing India and speaking ill of it” during his foreign visit. “Having failed to attain power through democratic means, Rahul Gandhi is resorting to cheap tactics to seize it,” he said.

In a post on X, he said: “In his pursuit of power, Rahul Gandhi is playing a dangerous gamble in which he has staked the nation’s identity, its developmental journey, and the trust of 1.40 crore Indians.”

Citing data to underscore India’s progress under Mr. Modi’s leadership, Mr. Thakur said: “One statistic after another shows that the world looks towards India and India’s prime minister with hope. But while opposing Modiji, Rahul Gandhi has started opposing India. He has started playing with India’s interests, striking at India’s pride and the trust of Indians, and wagering them in a gamble.”

“Why does Rahul Gandhi say things that India’s adversaries, especially Pakistan, like to hear? Rahul Gandhi’s friends sitting in Pakistan must be happy hearing this, but Indians are not,” he said. He said the foundation of Mr. Gandhi’s politics was “despair”, while the foundation of Mr. Modi’s politics was “development and trust”.
