Convicts’ lawyer rejects claims of Bilkis Bano being kept in dark, warns against media trial

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

The counsel for the two convicts granted bail by the Supreme Court in the 2002 Bilkis Bano gangrape and murder case issued a statement on November 21 rejected her allegation that she was kept in the dark about the proceedings while warning against an attempt to sensationalise a sub-judice matter through a media trial.

The rebuttal by advocate Ayush Anand, counsel for Bipinchand Kanaiyalal Joshi and Pradip Ramanlal Modhiya, followed a public statement issued by Ms. Bano, through her counsel and senior advocate Shobha Gupta, that she was neither informed nor heard before the apex court allowed bail to the two men in an order on October 28.

Joshi and Modhiya were two of the 11 men convicted in the 2002 case. The then 19-year-old Ms. Bano was gang-raped and 14 members of her family, including her infant daughter, were killed in 2002 during the riots.

The apex court order for the duo’s release on bail came in an appeal filed by them against a 2017 Bombay High Court judgment upholding a CBI court decision of 2008, convicting the men and sentencing them to life terms in prison.

Mr. Anand, in his statement, said the allegations of suppression issued by Ms. Bano’s side was factually incorrect.

“The matter had been listed on ten occasions before bail was granted and had taken more than a year. In these circumstances, the allegation of suppression by the legal team of the prosecutrix [Ms. Bano], according to the petitioners [Joshi and Modhiya], is entirely misconceived,” the statement said.

Mr. Anand said Ms. Bano’s legal team had been both aware of the court proceedings on the appeals and observing them. The lawyer said notice in the case was issued in March 2026, and was reported in the media. 

“Therefore, the allegation that the prosecutrix was unaware of the proceedings is, according to the petitioners, factually incorrect. The legal team of the prosecutrix did not initiate any proceedings at that stage, despite the matter being pending and notice having been issued, and raised the issue subsequently,” the statement read.

The counsel said Ms. Bano had not been a party before the Bombay High Court, and was accordingly not impleaded in the appeal filed in the Supreme Court. He pointed out that she had not filed a caveat seeking to be heard in the appeal before the apex court.

“The legal team representing the prosecutrix could have exercised the available procedural remedies, including seeking impleadment, and the absence of such action cannot be attributed to the petitioners or the court.

The statement delved into the health condition and physical disability of the two men, their age and 17 years they had spent behind bars - the very circumstances which the court noted while granting them bail and suspending their sentences. Mr. Anand referred to judicial precedents which allowed bail on the grounds of ill-health and years of incarceration.

“It is unfortunate that, 18 days after the grant of bail, from November 16 onwards, the matter has suddenly been portrayed in a manner suggesting that the prosecutrix was unaware of the proceedings. Such allegations, without due regard to the chronology and procedural history, risk creating a misleading impression about the judicial process and the pending criminal appeals… Publicity surrounding a pending matter should not come at the cost of factual accuracy, fairness or respect for the judicial process,” his statement said.
