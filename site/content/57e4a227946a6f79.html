By Abhinay Lakshman,Vijaita Singh • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-19

The 2027 Census self-enumeration portal is now showing a total of 76 entries under the “Other” religions that now appear as predictive text suggestions and not as a drop-down menu.  

This comes days after The Hindu reported that at least 287 entries such as ISKCON, Ramkrishna Paramhans, Gorak Nath, Sanatan, Vedic Dharm, Ramdev etc., had been listed in a drop-down menu for “Other” religions on the portal and then removed.  

An examination of the new list of available “Other” religions showed that the Union government removed over 200 entries that were previously listed under the same category on Monday (November 16, 2026), the day the portal went live for four poll-bound States — Uttar Pradesh, Goa, Punjab and Uttarakhand.  

The deletions include entries such as Satguru, Ramdev, ISKCON, Ramkrishna Paramhans, Gorak Nath, Sanatan, Bishnoi, Vaishnaw, Shia, Sunni, and many others that were either classified in earlier Censuses as sects within the six major religions (Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist) or seemed to be new entries not recorded in previous Censuses at all.  

Out of the 76 predictive text suggestions that now remain on the portal, nearly all appear to have already been recorded and tabulated based on the 2011 Census data on what people chose to write their faith as under the “Other” column. A majority of these entries include the religions and faiths of Scheduled Tribe communities across the country — such as Sarna, Doni Polo, Tana Bhagat, Birsa, Garo, Munda, Oraon, Santal, etc.  

Apart from these, five entries in this new list of suggestive “other” religions do not feature in the 2011 or 2001 Census tables for “Other religions and persuasions”. These include “Adibasi dharma”, “Bura Deo”, “Karupa”, “Tingkao Rangong”, and “Tingwang Hingde”.  

Question the integrity

Soon after The Hindu reported the presence of a drop-down for “other” religions and its quick removal, Opposition leaders and OBC associations alike questioned the integrity of the way the 2027 Census was being conducted, with many of them questioning how the government was comfortable introducing a drop-down for religions but not for caste. Experts had also questioned the introduction of previously unrecorded entries in this drop-down, saying there would be nothing to compare these with.

Interestingly, a section of general category communities in north India reacted to the presence of 287 “other” religions by saying this was an alleged attempt to “divide Hindus”. 

On Monday (November 16, 2026), when the self-enumeration portal went live, the initial list of 287 entries for “Other” religions appeared as a drop-down menu from which respondents could select one in addition to writing in whatever they wanted. Within hours of The Hindu sending a query to the Union Home Ministry, this drop-down menu was removed, and a representative of the Office of the RGI and the Census Commissioner said it would be “incorrect” to say these entries were shown as “other” religions.  

However, a government official had told The Hindu at the time that this list was part of “predictive text entries” to help reduce variations and mistakes in spellings. But officials did not clarify how previously unrecorded entries such as ISKCON, Ramkrishna Paramhans, Ramdev, Gorak Nath, etc. had appeared on this list either.   

The 2027 Census is the first time Independent India is enumerating caste. But despite sustained demands from Other Backward Classes groups and scholars for a drop-down menu for enumerating all castes (SC, ST, OBC, and General), the Union government decided to use an open column for this question in the Population Enumeration phase.  

The government’s justification was that there was no “available” list of all castes in the country apart from the SC and ST lists.  

A coalition of OBC associations and other civil society groups under the banner of the All India Caste Census Coordination Committee have called for a protest at New Delhi’s Ramlila Maidan on November 28.
