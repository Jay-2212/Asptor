Vande Mataram ends after 2 stanzas in Karnataka Assembly and Council; BJP legislators recite full version

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-21

The opposition Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and Janatal Dal (Secular) members (MLAs) on September 21 recited the full version of Vande Mataram in the Karnataka Legislative Assembly after the official rendition ended with the first two stanzas, in protest against the State Government’s decision to restrict the song’s rendition at official functions.

After two stanzas of the national song, Assembly Speaker G. S. Patil and the ruling Congress members read out the preamble of the Indian Constitution. However, BJP and JD(S) members sang the full version of the song before reading the Preamble of the Constitution. Later, BJP members raised ‘Bharat Mata Ki Jai’ slogans and then occupied their chairs.

The opposition members were holding a copy of the song while reciting the full version of Vande Mataram.

Even as the opposition members sang the song and read the preamble, the Speaker moved an obituary reference, and Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar began paying tribute to former MLA R.B. Shiriyannavar, who died on August 27.  

Neither the Speaker nor the Chief Minister spoke in the House about singing the full version of Vande Mataram by the opposition members.

A similar scene unfolded in the Legislative Council where BJP and JD(S) members continued reciting the remaining stanzas while Chairperson Saleem Ahmed began reading the preamble to the Constitution.

The Karnataka government led by D. K. Shivakumar had issued an order on September 8 directing that only two stanzas of the Vande Mataram should be sung at State functions and official ceremonies.

The exceptions are programmes attended by the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister or Governor.

The legislature had convened for a 3-day special discussion on the drought situation and the implications of the Kasturirangan report on the Western Ghats.

Ahead of the session, the BJP members said they would recite the full version of Vande Mataram in the House in protest against the State Government’s decision to restrict its rendition at official programmes. The BJP had opposed the State Government’s decision of limiting the national song.

On September 19, Shivamogga MLA S.N. Channabasappa objected to rendition of the national song Vande Mataram being limited to its first two stanzas at a public meeting attended by Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar at Tirthahalli in Shivamogga district.

Later in his address, Mr. Shivakumar said he would not play politics with people’s emotions and that his focus was only on uplifting people’s lives. “I welcome opinions from opponents and their criticism as well. The MLA has the right to express his opinion. He could have walked out in protest. Nobody would have stopped him. We have our own opinion on the issue, and we stick to it,” he said.

The government order has been challenged in the High Court of Karnataka. Advocate Girish Bharadwaj has filed a PIL questioning the State Government’s authority to prescribe that only two stanzas be sung at official functions.

The petition stated that the State’s order conflicts with a July 9 communication from the Union Ministry of Home Affairs, which stated that the official version of Vande Mataram should be sung in mass gatherings. The petitioner has argued that the subject does not fall under the State or Concurrent Lists of the Constitution and, therefore, comes under Parliament’s residuary legislative powers.
