Amit Shah says Congress neglected development, provided inadequate funds during its rule in Arunachal Pradesh

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Describing the development of border villages as a priority of the government, Union Home Minister Amit Shah on Sunday (November 22, 2026) said the upliftment of about 40 villages along Arunachal Pradesh's International Border, under the Centre’s flagship Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP), ensured that many of those who moved out from these areas were returning.

Launching four major development initiatives worth more than ₹5,610 crore, he said Prime Minister Narendra Modi changed the perception of border villages by describing them as the country's “first villages” and undertaking the VVP to provide such areas with healthcare, electricity, education, and drinking water.

On the last day of his three-day visit to the State, Mr. Shah also inaugurated the 12th battalion campus of the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) at Hollongi, near Itanagar, and said the government was following a "zero-casualty" approach to disaster management, under which timely warnings and immediate evacuation measures were being prioritised to protect vulnerable people.

The initiatives launched on Sunday include 199 Vibrant Villages projects worth ₹110.95 crore across 11 border districts; Mission Shikshit Arunachal Phase-II, with an outlay of ₹1,500 crore; and power and rural road development to the tune of ₹2,000 crore each.

“Developing border villages is our government’s priority. If villages are empowered economically, the lifestyle of the people will change, and their happiness will increase,” Mr. Shah said, adding that reverse migration helped repopulate 40 border villages in Arunachal Pradesh.

A lack of jobs and poor quality of life have forced several people in such villages, along Arunachal Pradesh’s borders with Bhutan, China, and Myanmar, to migrate to the State’s urban centres and elsewhere in India over the years. This raised concerns, as the border residents were seen as an important aspect of the country’s first line of defence.

Cooperatives push

The Union Home Minister said personnel of the Indian Army and the Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP) were helping border villagers increase agricultural production and facilitating the marketing of their locally grown produce.

“As the Army and ITBP personnel are consuming locally produced crops and food items, their emotional bonds with the border villages are becoming stronger,” he said.

Mr. Shah, who also holds the Cooperation portfolio, said Arunachal Pradesh could become self-sufficient in milk, eggs, fish, and meat production through cooperatives, thereby improving the economy of rural and border areas.

He assured the people that if they produced sufficient quantities of milk, the National Dairy Development Board and Amul would purchase their produce.

Financial assistance

Mr. Shah accused the Congress of neglecting development in Arunachal Pradesh and providing inadequate financial assistance during its rule, claiming that under Mr. Modi’s leadership, the Centre has substantially increased its support for the State’s progress.

He said the Centre provided ₹33,530 crore to Arunachal Pradesh during the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance’s tenure between 2004 and 2014, but this rose to ₹1,64,664 crore during the 2014-2024 period under the Bharatiya Janata Party-led National Democratic Alliance government.

The Home Minister said tax devolution to the State increased fourfold, while grants increased 18 times, compared to the time of the UPA government. “The figures reflect the Narendra Modi government’s commitment to the development of Arunachal Pradesh and the rest of the Northeast,” he said.

“The growth of Arunachal Pradesh’s budget from ₹12,000 crore to ₹36,000 crore suggests that development has expanded in the last 12 years,” he said.

Boosting preparedness

Inaugurating the NDRF campus, Mr. Shah said it would significantly strengthen disaster preparedness and response across the Northeast, one of the country’s most vulnerable regions to natural calamities.

He said the region is prone to various natural disasters and stressed the need for preparedness, modern warning systems and coordinated efforts to minimise loss of lives and property.
