    Total 12 Bills passed in Monsoon Session; very successful in terms of business: Rijiju

    Union Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju said a total of 12 Bills were passed in the Monsoon Session that concluded today.

    “In terms of business, it was a very successful session; we passed a total of 12 bills. However, from the perspective of debate and discussion, it was not as successful. In the Lok Sabha, we achieved only 19% productivity relative to the total time allotted. In the Rajya Sabha, productivity stood at 39%,” he said.

    Everything has been passed without discussion, very disappointing session: Congress MP

    After the Parliament Monsoon Session concluded, Congress MP Karti Chidambaram said it was a very disappointing session and that most Bills were passed without even a discussion.

    “It’s was very disappointing session. The government has the numbers. It will always have its way. They must give leeway for the Opposition to raise issues of concern. They can’t stifle, censor the Opposition, and decide what issues they can raise or not raise. You must give Opposition the space to have its say,” he said, speaking to reporters in the Parliament premises.

    Rajya Sabha Chair delivers valedictory remarks; 12 Bill considered and passed or returned

    Rajya Sabha Chair C.P. Radhakrishnan, delivering the concluding remarks, expresses deep concern over the session being marked by “persistent disorder and frequent disruptions”.

    He said the Upper House functioned for a total of 37 hours and 49 minutes. However, he said the House managed to complete core legislative business by considering and passing of or returning of 12 Bills.

    “The floor of the House was open, but we didn’t make the fullest of the opportunity to raise the concerns of those we represent. Repeated disruptions affect the trust of the people, so we must uphold parliamentary conduct,” he said in his reflections on the session.

    Matter will be investigated: Nadda in Rajya Sabha over ‘shuddhikaran’ of venue after Kharge’s rally in Haldwani

    Leader of Opposition (LoP) in Rajya Sabha Mallikarjun Kharge brought up the incident of alleged ‘shuddhikaran’ (purification) ceremony held at Haldwani’s Ramlila Ground after the Congress president addressed a rally there on August 8, 2026.

    “Register a case against this act of untouchability. That is my demand,” Mr. Kharge said, claiming that the BJP had a role to play in the incident.

    To this, Leader of the House J.P. Nadda said the BJP does not subscribe to such activities. Condeming the incident, he said the BJP National President Nitin Nabin will take the matter seriously. “Strict action will be taken against those found responsible upon investigation,” he said, asking the Congress to not “politicise” the issue.

    “Untouchability is not a sin. We all condemn it irrespective of party or ideology. We are all pure and there is no need to purify us. I request the government to take this up for investigation and those responsible must be punished. No two ways about it,” Chair Radhakrishnan said in his observations.

    Centre softens stance on FCRA Bill following appeals by minority groups

    The first indication of the Union Government’s softening of stance on the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026, emerged on August 9, when several Christian organisations launched coordinated appeals on YouTube, Facebook and WhatsApp, urging the Centre to either refer the Bill to a Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) or withdraw it altogether. 

    The appeals were seen as an attempt to provide the government with an honourable route to step back from pushing the legislation through Parliament.

    On Wednesday (August 12), Minister of State for Home Nityanand Rai moved a motion in the Lok Sabha to send the Bill to a JPC. Amid din, the motion was adopted, giving Parliament more time to examine the contentious provisions in the Bill.
