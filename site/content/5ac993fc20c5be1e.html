From Lal Bahadur to Dharmendra Pradhan: Famous ministerial resignations in India

• THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-07-26

In a historic culmination of weeks of massive, student-led nationwide protests, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan tendered his resignation on July 25, 2026, over the widespread NEET exam paper leak scandal.

The agitation gained unprecedented momentum after renowned activist Sonam Wangchuk joined forces with the student-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), leading a 26-day hunger strike to demand institutional accountability. Alongside the Congress and various student groups, the movement made the resignation of Mr. Pradhan a non-negotiable term.

This immense public pressure ultimately led Mr. Pradhan to officially step down from his post. As the country processes this massive political shift, it has fundamentally revived the debate over political accountability in India.

But have cabinet ministers in India resigned from their positions under the weight of scandals in the past? Let’s dive into it.

Lal Bahadur Shastri

A key moment for political accountability in India occurred under Lal Bahadur Shastri when he was the Union Railway Minister. After a serious train derailment in Ariyalur, Tamil Nadu, which caused over 150 deaths, Shastri accepted full moral responsibility for the incident and resigned from his position in Novembor 25, 1956. Although former Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru initially refused to accept Shastri's resignation to maintain his cabinet, Shastri insisted on stepping down.

T.T. Krishnamachari

He resigned as Union Finance Minister in February 1958 amid the controversy surrounding the Mundhra affair. He returned to the Finance Ministry in September 1963. However, in 1965, he stepped down after the Prime Minister asked the Chief Justice of India to conduct an informal inquiry into allegations of favoritism against him.

V.K. Krishna Menon

Former Defence Minister V.K. Krishna Menon resigned on November 9, 1962, after public opinion pressured Jawaharlal Nehru to remove him from the defence portfolio following the Chinese debacle, leaving him with only responsibilities for defence production. By that, he acknowledged his role in the country’s significant military unpreparedness and setbacks experienced in the early weeks of the Sino-Indian War.

V.P. Singh

In 1987, V.P. Singh, who was serving as Defence Minister at the time, resigned following investigations of allegations of commissions being taken in a defence deal for the purchase of submarines. He had also resigned as Prime Minister of India on November 7, 1990, following a dramatic collapse of his minority coalition government.

Madhavrao Scindia

Madhavrao Scindia stepped down from the Union Cabinet twice throughout his political career. The first resignation occurred in January 1993 when he served as the Civil Aviation Minister, as he took moral responsibility for the crash of a leased Russian Tu-154 plane. His second resignation as Human Resource Development Minister took place in January 1996 in connection with the Hawala corruption scandal.

Nitish Kumar

He stepped down as Railway Minister in August 1999, taking moral responsibility for the Gaisal train collision in West Bengal, which killed nearly 290 people. He submitted his resignation to then-Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, resigning in the aftermath of the devastating head-on collision between the Awadh-Assam Express and the Brahmaputra Mail, which had led to a significant loss of life. His resignation was officially accepted, placing him among one of the few ministers in independent India to resign on moral grounds following a major rail tragedy.

Shivraj Patil

Shivraj Patil resigned from his position as Union Home Minister, accepting moral responsibility for the security failures that contributed to the 26/11 Mumbai terrorist attacks. In September 2008, following the Delhi serial blasts, he appeared before the media and changed outfits three times. The images of this incident quickly circulated on television and social media, ultimately shaping his public image. Two months later, he stepped down, acknowledging his moral accountability.

A. Raja

A. Raja resigned as Minister of Communications and IT in 2010 during his tenure in the Manmohan Singh cabinet, after getting involved in allegations of corruption in 2G spectrum allocation. However, Mr. Raja continued to maintain that he was following the policy laid down by the previous National Democratic Alliance government and was not guilty of any wrong doing.

Dayanidhi Maran

Mr. Maran resigned as Union Minister of Textiles in 2011, in the wake of the CBI submitting a status report to the Supreme Court, alleging that he forced telecom promoter C. Sivasankaran, at the time he was IT and Communications Minister, to sell his Aircel stake to the Malaysia-based Maxis group.

Pawan Kumar Bansal

Mr. Bansal’s resignation came following a report suggesting that his nephew, Vijay Singla, had accepted a bribe from a Railway Board member to facilitate a lateral promotion, which hit the headlines. He resigned as Union Railway Minister in 2013 after getting caught up in this high-profile bribery scandal.

Ashwani Kumar

Ashwani Kumar resigned as Union Law Minister after the Supreme Court criticised the government’s handling of the coal block allocation (Coalgate scam) investigation. He resigned in May 2013. While the controversy was legal and political rather than an accident, his resignation was presented as an act of accountability.

M.J. Akbar

Former Union Minister M.J. Akbar resigned in October 2018 after several of his former colleagues accused him of sexual harassment, allegations that were first brought to light by journalist Priya Ramani. Mr. Akbar decided to resign in order to file a personal defamation lawsuit against Ramani concerning these accusations.
