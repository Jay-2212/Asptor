NIA’s anti-terror conference discusses cross-border terrorism, threat from drones

By Vijaita Singh • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-21

“The National Investigation Agency’s (NIA) sixth Anti-Terror Conference concluded on Thursday (November 19, 2026) with a focus on combating cross-border terrorism, dismantling terror ecosystems, strengthening prosecution of terror cases and enhancing coordination between Central and State agencies,” NIA Director General Rakesh Aggarwal said on Friday (November 20, 2026).

Drone smuggling of arms, narcotics, cash and explosives was a key concern, the official said adding that cheap and easily available drones are increasingly being used in cross-border operations.

The two-day conference, held at DRDO Bhawan in New Delhi, was inaugurated by Union Home Minister Amit Shah and attended by senior officers of State police forces, Central agencies and specialised anti-terror units. According to the NIA, 40 Directors General and heads of State police and Central agencies, along with 230 participants from Anti-Terror Investigation units, participated in the event.

Addressing the conference, Mr. Shah reiterated the Centre’s “zero tolerance” policy towards terrorism and stressed the need for a coordinated approach to dismantle terror networks, including cross-border handlers, financial channels, organised crime linkages and local facilitators.

Global cooperation

A key theme of the conference was international cooperation in counter-terror investigations, including extradition, deportation and evidence-sharing, Mr. Aggarwal said.

The conference deliberated on the organised crime-terror nexus, radicalisation, cross-border terror financing, technology-enabled investigations and international cooperation. Discussions covered the use of drones for smuggling arms, narcotics and explosives, online radicalisation, lone-wolf threats, hawala networks, trade-based money laundering and the growing use of artificial intelligence (AI) by terrorist organisations.

Prosecution of terror cases

A separate segment on the prosecution of terror cases, introduced last year and made a regular feature from this year, brought together investigators, prosecutors and legal experts. The sessions focused on the application of new criminal laws, admissibility of digital evidence, trial strategy, witness protection and the use of platforms such as e-Sakshya, e-Prosecution and e-Forensics. Participants stressed the need to ensure that professional investigations translate into timely convictions.

The Home Minister also launched ‘Pushti’, a citizen-facing verification portal that allows people to verify the authenticity of NIA notices and helps prevent fraudsters from misusing fake notices in so-called “digital arrest” scams.

In his valedictory address, Union Home Secretary Govind Mohan called on States to strengthen Anti-Terror Investigation units in line with a model ATS structure, expand forensic and cyber capabilities and integrate financial investigations with terror probes from the outset. He also urged greater use of INTERPOL mechanisms, updated dossiers and the Extradition Portal to secure the return of fugitives operating from abroad.

The NIA used the conference to highlight its recent performance. The agency said it had registered 82 cases in 2026 so far. Of the 29 cases decided by courts, convictions were secured in 27 cases, resulting in a conviction rate of 93%. A total of 123 accused were convicted, including 10 death sentences and 14 life terms.

To speed up trials, 25 exclusive NIA courts have been established across the country, with another 14 courts expected to be notified in the coming months. The agency also said it had strengthened its Foreign Investigation Unit with international legal experts to improve its extradition and overseas investigation capabilities.
