Ladakh announces hill council elections, but fails to assuage protesting civil society groups

By Peerzada Ashiq • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-11

The Ladakh administration’s move on Tuesday (November 10, 2026) to announce hill council elections in the Union Territory, in addition to panchayat and municipal elections, failed to assuage the growing anxiety over the Centre’s handling of the region, which has witnessed frequent protests over demands for Statehood and Sixth Schedule status.

The announcement came even as a shutdown was observed in Kargil to protest the timing of the elections, given the harsh winter conditions in the region.

“Elections to all eight Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDCs), covering 100 constituencies across Ladakh, will be held on December 4. Following the creation of eight districts from the erstwhile two districts of Kargil and Leh, the number of LAHDC constituencies has increased from 26 each in Leh and Kargil to 100 for whole Ladakh,” Ladakh Chief Secretary Ashish Kundra said.

‘Hill councils will retain existing powers’

He said November 17 was the last date for filing nominations, November 18 for scrutiny, and November 20 for the withdrawal of candidature. “The entire election process is scheduled to be completed before December 8,” he added.

“For the first time, 36 out of the 100 LAHDC seats have been reserved for women, marking a significant step towards strengthening women’s representation in grassroots democratic institutions,” Mr. Kundra said. Putting all speculation to rest, Mr. Kundra clarified that the newly-elected LAHDCs “would retain all powers vested in them under the existing law”.

Shoutdown against winter polls

Mr. Kundra’s announcement on the hill council elections coincided with a shutdown in Kargil to protest the delimitation and demand that panchayat and municipal elections be postponed to next year due to the approach of winter in the region.

“Protests and shutdowns are symbolic. People in a democracy have the right to express themselves through such protests,” Mr. Kundra said. 

The shutdown call was issued by the Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA), a socio-politico-religious grouping which has been holding talks with the Union government on the demands for Statehood and Sixth Schedule status. However, this process hit a wall on October 22 after the Centre decided not to resume talks with the KDA and the Leh Apex Body (LAB) for unknown reasons.

“Our protest is against the administration’s bid to impose decisions unilaterally and ignoring public concerns over delimitation and the proposed timing of the elections,” said Sajjad Kargili, co-chairman of the KDA. The shutdown call impacted daily life in Kargil and its adjoining districts, where markets and offices remained closed and thin traffic plied on the roads.

‘Festival of democracy’

Addressing the concerns over delimitation, Mr. Kundra said, “The delimitation exercise was undertaken in accordance with the prescribed rules, taking into account population and the existing Panchayat Halqas and Municipal Wards.

The timely constitution of the hill councils was “essential”, he said, adding, “The newly elected LAHDCs would have January, February, and March available for preparation of the budget for the next financial year.”

Mr. Kundra also defended the move to hold the elections in the winter. “December 15 is the last day of school in Ladakh. Elections, to be held on December 4, is a festival of democracy. People should participate in it like they do in other festivals. Adequate facilities at polling stations, including heaters and separate queues for senior citizens, will be arranged. People, particularly youth, women, senior citizens, religious organisations and government employees, should actively participate to exercise their democratic right to vote,” he said.

‘The middle path’ on liquor

The Ladakh Chief Secretary said that out of 192 panchayat halqas, 55 sarpanches have been elected unopposed, while elections will be held for the remaining 137 seats. Also, 1,186 panch candidates have been elected unopposed out of 1,428. In the Leh Municipal Council, elections will be held in all 21 wards, while four of the 17 wards in the Kargil Municipal Council have elected their representatives unopposed. The municipal and panchayat elections are scheduled for November 21. 

On the concerns raised regarding the liberal excise policy, Mr. Kundra cited the teachings of Lord Buddha and “the principle of the middle path”. “We are aware about the religious sensitivities of the region and the interests of Ladakh as a tourist place,” said Mr. Kundra, in the wake of recent protests by women residents against allowing new liquor points in the region.

Asked about the stalled talks between the Union government and Ladakh civil society groups, Mr. Kundra said the government of India and the Ladakh administration are “committed towards strengthening democratic institutions and ensuring their early constitution”. 

‘Delaying tactics’

Meanwhile, LAB co-chairman Tsering Dorjey Lakrook called for a sit-in strike from November 12 over the Centre’s failure to keep its word. “The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) had promised to arrange a Sub Committee meeting on October 22, which never happened. It shows the Centre was not sincere and was using delaying tactics,” he said.

Mr. Lakrook also highlighted the delay in releasing the judicial enquiry commission’s report on the September 24, 2025 violence against protesters in Leh, which left four dead and around 80 injured. “Justice should be served to the martyrs and seriously injured in the indiscriminate police firing,” he said, also demanding rollback of the liquor policy. 

Mr. Lakrook backed the move to hold panchayat and municipal elections. “It will empower people at grassroots level,” he said. However, he said it was not a good idea to hold the LAHDC elections in December, given the harsh winter conditions in the region. “Most of the people, especially students and older people, of Ladakh will not be present,” he said.
