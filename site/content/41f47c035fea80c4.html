The new Constitutional Amendment Bill, accompanied by a fresh Delimitation Bill, proposes to increase the number of Lok Sabha seats in each State by 50%; they also bring into force the 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha

By Varghese K. George ,Nistula Hebbar • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-27

A new Constitutional Amendment Bill approved by the Union Cabinet on Friday (November 27, 2026) proposes an extension of the existing freeze on the inter-State distribution of Lok Sabha seats for another 25 years, until 2051, addressing the core concern of States that feared a loss of their proportionate strength due to the decline in their share of the national population.

The 132nd Constitutional Amendment Bill marks a significant turn from the 131st Amendment Bill moved by the Centre in April 2026 that sought to redistribute Lok Sabha seats among States according to the 2011 Census population.

The proposed amendment removes the linkage of the existing distribution of Lok Sabha seats to the 1971 Census and links it with provisions related to women’s reservation in Article 334A of the Constitution. The guarantee for a 50% increase in the number of Lok Sabha seats for each State is being written into this, providing that, for the purposes of giving immediate effect to such reservation for women, “the delimitation so undertaken shall provide for an increase of fifty per cent in the number of seats allocated to each State and Union territory in the House of the People.” The total number of seats in the Legislative Assembly of each State and Union territory will also increase similarly, “over and above the number of seats existing immediately before such delimitation”.

The new Bill, accompanied by a fresh Delimitation Bill, proposes to increase the number of Lok Sabha seats in each State by 50%, while the maximum strength of the lower House will rise from the current 550 to 850. If these Bills are passed by Parliament in its special sittings from November 30 to December 3, the path to immediate delimitation will open, which will also bring into force the 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha.

Amendments to Articles 81 and 82, which mandate the size and distribution of Lok Sabha seats among the States, propose to extend a sunset clause for the existing proportion until 2051.

The existing provision in Article 82 that says that “until the relevant figures for the first census taken after the year 2026 have been published, it shall not be necessary to readjust” the distribution of seats is being amended in the Bill and will be changed to read “until the year 2051”.

Article 82 currently has two definitions for population: for the purposes of distribution among States, the 1971 population; and for the purposes of delimitation of individual constituencies within the States, the 2001 Census. The proposed amendment will extend the sunset clause to “until the year 2051”, but the reference to the 1971 Census has been dropped.

The Bill proposes that the distribution among States will be determined “in accordance with Article 334A”, while the boundaries of individual constituencies will be determined by the “latest published census figures available on the date of the commencement of the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-second Amendment) Act, 2026”.
