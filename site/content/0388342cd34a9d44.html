When it comes to insurance, claims and settlements happen to be the most important metrics. Data from Asego, a global travel assistance and insurance provider, showed that non-medical claims account for over two-thirds of travel insurance claims in the first nine months of the financial year but just a fifth of total payouts.

Payouts are much better for medical claims, which account for just a third of travel insurance claims but make up 80% of total payouts. The data is based on Asego’s analysis of nearly 11,000 travel insurance claims.

“The analysis brings out two distinct dimensions of travel risk: the frequency of everyday travel disruptions and the potentially substantial financial consequences of medical emergencies,” noted Dev Karvat, Founder and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of Asego.

So, why is there such disparity between medical and non-medical claim settlements, and what does this data show us?

Why are non-medical claims so frequent?

Non-medical claims consist of issues such as flight cancellations, delays, baggage issues, and stolen belongings, among others. These are very common while travelling, leading to a substantially higher number of claims.

“Flight delays, cancellations, baggage delays, and missed connections are more commonly experienced by travellers. However, the individual payouts are generally smaller and subject to policy limits,” said Ramit Goyal, Chief Distribution Officer at Generali Central Insurance.

A changing global order has also led to higher claims. “Weather disruptions, airline operational issues, geopolitical developments, and complex flight itineraries have increased travellers’ exposure to non-medical risks, contributing to higher claim volumes in these categories,” Goyal added.

The Asego report noted that travel delays and cancellations were the biggest reason for non-medical claims, accounting for 27%. Trip cancellations accounted for 23% of the claims, while delayed baggage contributed to 14%.

“While these incidents may not always involve large individual payouts, their frequency makes them a significant component of travel insurance claims,” said Asego’s Dev Karvat.

Behind the payout gap

While non-medical claims are more frequent, they often cost less to resolve than medical claims.

“The data reflects a fundamental insurance principle — the most frequent claims are not necessarily the most expensive. A relatively small number of high-value medical claims can account for a significant proportion of total payouts,” said Goyal.

Healthcare costs are much higher in many international destinations, leading to medical claim payouts ballooning despite a lower number of claims.

Asego’s findings also show the same. According to the company, consolidated data from insurers showed that total cashless and reimbursement payouts between April and November were at $6.7 million, out of which medical claims accounted for $5.3 million and non-medical at $1.3 million.

“The data highlights how a relatively smaller proportion of medical incidents can translate into a significantly higher financial burden,” the report said.

Travel insurance has also become an increasingly popular tool for travellers to safeguard against losses arising from travel disruptions. This popularity is also behind a higher number of non-medical claims, which are more frequent, as per industry executives.
