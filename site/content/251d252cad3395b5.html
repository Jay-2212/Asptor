Stalin urges Tamil Nadu government to act against Kallakurichi TVK MLA over messages to Collector

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-24

DMK president M.K. Stalin on Tuesday (November 24, 2026) urged the Tamil Nadu government to take action against Kallakurichi MLA Arul Vignesh over allegations that he had sent inappropriate messages to the district Collector.

Reacting to The Hindu report, “Collector files complaint against Kallakurichi TVK MLA”, Mr. Stalin said the Chief Minister and the Chief Secretary should have spoken to the Collector and assured her of support when reports about the inappropriate messages emerged several weeks ago.

“They should have condemned the MLA and taken action against him. Any government concerned about the welfare and safety of women would have done so. Since the government sought to protect its party MLA and its minority government, there was a delay in the woman Collector filing the complaint,” he said.

The incident raised questions about what would happen to an ordinary woman if a Collector herself faced such a situation, Mr. Stalin said.

“What is shameful is that some leaders aspiring for positions stood by the MLA instead of extending support to the Collector. It is highly condemnable that the government failed to act even though the issue was brought to its notice several times,” he alleged.

Mr. Stalin also claimed that the incident lent credence to allegations that TVK functionaries were involved in sexual offences and drug smuggling.

Annamalai seeks transparent probe

We The Leaders founder K. Annamalai also said the State government must take appropriate action against the MLA if the allegations were found to be true.

In a social media post, he expressed shock over the incident and said the report published in The Hindu had raised serious concerns, particularly as the complainant was a woman IAS officer heading the district administration. Such an incident involving a senior woman official was unprecedented in Tamil Nadu, he said.

Referring to the questions raised in the Madras High Court and the report, Mr. Annamalai urged the State government to ensure a transparent investigation without political interference.

Mr. Annamalai said the government must take appropriate action if the allegations were established, warning that any failure to ensure a proper inquiry would undermine public confidence in the administration and the State’s commitment to women’s safety.
