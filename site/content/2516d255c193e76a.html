World needs peace, stability, predictability: Modi in Manila

By Suhasini Haidar • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-18

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Tuesday (November 17, 2026) called for “peace, stability, and predictability” in the world as he attended the ASEAN-India Summit, the East Asia Summit, and the Quad Leaders’ Summit in Manila, Philippines. He made a number of proposals aimed at maritime security, protecting critical minerals supply chains, and enhancing trade.

The Quad Summit, which was being held for the first time in a non-member country, was hosted by U.S. President Donald Trump and was also attended by Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, apart from Mr. Modi. The leaders issued a joint statement opposing economic and maritime “coercion” in the Indo-Pacific, and Mr. Modi announced that India would host the first Quad Business Forum in Mumbai in 2027.

The three back-to-back events had started with Mr. Modi’s meeting with the leaders of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) for the ASEAN-India Summit, where the Prime Minister said the world now needed peace, stability, and predictability more than ever.

“Together, India and ASEAN represent nearly one-fourth of the world’s population. That is why it is essential to ensure that our future is shaped not by competition, but by cooperation,” Mr. Modi told the leaders of Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste, and Vietnam. Myanmar’s military junta leader, President Min Aung Laing, was not invited, and the country was represented by an official.

At the event, he also promised that India would give e-visas free of charge to ASEAN tourists.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., and ASEAN leaders pose for an official family photo at the 23rd India-ASEAN Summit, in Manila on Tuesday. Photo: Narendra Modi Photo Gallery/ANI Photo

Following this, Mr. Modi took part in the East Asia Summit, which was attended by the leaders of India, China, Russia, the U.S., Japan, South Korea, and the ASEAN countries. At this summit, he proposed a seafarers’ safety initiative. He said that “whether it is the conflict in Ukraine or West Asia, India will continue to support every effort towards peace”.

The East Asia Summit members also issued a joint declaration on tackling cyber-crime, given the number of online fraud cases originating from Southeast Asian countries. India will host a summit on the issue at the National Forensic Sciences University in Gandhinagar and an East Asia Summit University Heads Conclave at Nalanda.

The ASEAN engagements were important as Mr. Modi had skipped the summits in Malaysia last year.

Quad leaders meet

Shortly before he left Manila, the Prime Minister met the leaders of the U.S., Australia, and Japan for the Quad Summit, which was last held in 2024. This was the first time that all the Quad leaders have met since the U.S.-Israel attacks on Iran and the blockade in the Strait of Hormuz began more than nine months ago — in a war where at least 23 Indians, mainly seafarers, have been killed.

From left, Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, Prime Minister Narendra Modi, President Donald Trump and Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese prepare fro a group photo during the meeting of the leaders of the Quad in Manila, Philippines, on November 17, 2026. | Photo Credit: AP

In remarks pointed toward China, the Quad countries strongly opposed “destabilizing or unilateral actions that seek to change the status quo, including by force or coercion, which escalate tensions and undermine regional peace and stability”, as well as “economic coercion, including through arbitrary export restrictions”. China has recently put new restrictions on the export of critical minerals and heavy equipment like tunnel boring machines that affect Indian development activity.

The Quad leaders also announced the launch of the Quad Business Forum, which will be held in Mumbai, to bring industry leaders from the U.S., India, Australia, and Japan together. The initiative is the latest effort at economic cooperation in the region, years after another U.S. initiative, the Indo-Pacific Economic Framework for Prosperity (IPEF), was shelved. Mr. Trump also announced an Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration and said that in 2027, the navies of the Quad countries would fly patrol aircraft for the first such exercise.

Impromptu interaction

Just before the Quad Summit, Mr. Trump unexpectedly spoke to the media and answered several questions, even as Mr. Modi, Mr. Albanese, and Ms. Takaichi stood silently next to him.

The U.S. President said he expected to have a “very good meeting” with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping on his next stop in Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Summit, scheduled for Thursday. He said he would push for Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin to also “get involved” to end the Russia-Ukraine and Iran conflicts.

The first three-way summit of global powers, dubbed the “G-3”, will be closely watched, with Trump, Xi, and Putin expected to discuss nuclear arms control, artificial intelligence governance, and ending conflicts.
