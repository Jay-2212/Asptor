Six Liangmai Naga civilians laid to rest in Manipur 187 days after abduction, killing

By Rahul Karmakar • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

The bodies of six Liangmai Naga civilians were laid to rest in Manipur’s Kangpokpi district on Monday (November 16, 2026), 187 days after they were abducted and subsequently killed almost a month later.

The family of the deceased and Naga organisations had refused to claim the bodies — kept at the mortuary of the Jawaharlal Nehru Institute of Medical Sciences in Imphal since June 10 — until their demands were met.

One of the main demands of the Naga civil bodies was the abrogation of the 2008 Suspension of Operations agreements with 25 Kuki-Zomi armed groups. The UNC and the families agreed to accept the bodies after receiving assurances of justice at a tripartite meeting involving the Union government, the Manipur government and Naga organisations in Guwahati on September 26, and after the completion of a safer alternative road for the people of Konsakhul village.

The families received the bodies on Monday and transported them to their home village of Konsakhul in the Kuki-dominated Kangpokpi district for the final rites. People from various communities gathered for the funeral.

‘Darkest day in State’s history’

Speaking at the funeral, United Naga Council (UNC) president N.G. Lorho said May 13, 2026, would remain the darkest day in Manipur’s history.

“On that day, six Naga men were killed, mutilated, and dismembered at Leilon Vaiphei village by KNF-P (Kuki National Front-Presidential), Kuki militants, and Leilon villagers. Their bodies were treated in a manner that brings shame to the State of Manipur and to India before the whole world,” he said.

He also noted that the funeral underscored the unity of the Nagas and other indigenous communities of Manipur.

“Our land is being invaded and ravaged. Our people are being killed, displaced, and terrorised. If this incursion is not stopped, the very existence, identity, and future of the indigenous people of Manipur will be at stake,” Mr. Lorho said.

The UNC, Manipur’s apex Naga organisation, urged the Centre to immediately end the “policy of appeasement and protection of militant groups” carrying out attacks and killings, and to fulfill its constitutional and moral responsibility to protect the lives, land, and identity of indigenous people.

What triggered abductions

The killing of three Thadou church leaders in an ambush in Kangpokpi district on May 13 prompted a series of abductions. The Thadous are divided over their Kuki identity.

Altogether 48 people — 28 Kukis and 20 Nagas — were abducted within hours of the ambush. Eighteen of the 20 Nagas were abducted from Leilon Vaiphei village.

After security forces brokered a hostage-exchange deal, Nagas released 14 of the 28 Kuki captives, while Kukis freed 14 Nagas on May 15. The Nagas set free included 12 of the 18 abducted from Leilon Vaiphei.

On June 9, Nagas released the remaining 14 Kukis, but the six abducted Nagas remained untraced until security forces recovered their bodies on June 10
