The affidavit asserted that the massive exercise (SIR), in which approximately 13 crore voters were excluded, was a unanimous decision of the ECI

By Krishnadas Rajagopal • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

The Supreme Court on Tuesday (November 17, 2026) agreed to have a detailed hearing of an application filed by Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi and INDIA bloc leaders, who have posed 48 ‘interrogatories’ directly to Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, and senior poll officials regarding the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

The interrogatories challenge the veracity of “bare assertions” made by the Election Commission of India (ECI)  in an institutional affidavit filed on October 26. One of these assertions was that the decision to hold a nationwide SIR, which saw around 13 crore voters excluded from the electoral rolls, was “unanimous”.

The interrogatories intend to compel the two Election Commissioners to individually state on oath whether they had objected to the SIR orders. The interrogatories require the CEC, the Election Commissioners, and poll officials to support their every answer with documentary proof in court.

A Bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant posted the case for December 9 after senior advocate Harish Salve, appearing for the ECI, enquired if the court would have enough time to hear the multiple petitions in the post-lunch session.

Senior advocates Kapil Sibal, A.M. Singhvi and advocate Prasanna S., for the INDIA bloc leaders, urged the Bench, which was about to rise for lunch after the morning session when Mr. Salve intervened, to issue notice on the application containing the interrogatories.

Though, initially, the court agreed to issue notice to the ECI for a response, Mr. Salve, along with senior advocate Maninder Singh, opposed the issuance of any notice before a preliminary hearing on the application.

“We will then hear the application and pass necessary orders,” the CJI said.

The application was filed as part of the Opposition leaders’ writ petition claiming the nationwide SIR exercise, the manner and extent of its conduct, was the product of a unilateral exercise of power by the CEC without the knowledge, approval or concurrence of the Election Commissioners.

Mr. Gandhi and the Opposition leaders submitted that posing the interrogatories was necessary as the controversy in their petition substantially rested upon facts that lie exclusively within the knowledge and the records of the ECI. The petitioners said they had no means of access to those facts except through the ECI and their officials.

So far, rather than disclosing those facts, the ECI has only made bare assertions.

The Opposition leaders said though the Supreme Court was not fettered by any rigid procedure, ‘interrogatories’ were recognised by the Supreme Court Rules Further, the court was empowered by the Rules to pass any orders “necessary for the ends of justice or to prevent abuse of the process of the court”.

The interrogatories covered detailed ground, including whether the October 26 affidavit claiming that SIR was a “unanimous decision”, was ever placed before Mr. Sandhu and Mr. Joshi. If so, when and how, and if they had suggested any changes in any assertions made in the document.

The Opposition leaders have asked for the number of the file clearing a nationwide SIR in which the decision of the Commission was taken, the date of the decision, whether it was taken at a meeting, upon circulation of a file or through electronic means, and the date on which each member signified approval.

They have queried about the contradictory stands taken by the ECI in the October 26 affidavit and an earlier Right to Information (RTI) reply from the poll body that had said “the Commission has so far not taken any decision on initiating an SIR across the country in 2025”.

Moreover, the INDIA bloc leaders have also asked the number of occasions, between February 19 last year to October 26, 2026, on which the two Election Commissioners allegedly recorded their written objections, dissent or reservation concerning any matter relating to the SIR, ECINET, Form 6 or the filing of appeals.

The petitioner-leaders have sought the total number of appeals pending in tribunals against the re-inclusion of names in West Bengal.

“They have also sought the names and designations of each officer who decided to institute these appeals and who signed or filed them.

“State whether any instruction, guideline, standard operating procedure or criterion for selecting the orders of inclusion to be appealed against was issued by the Commission, the Chief Electoral Officer, West Bengal, or any other authority; and, if so, furnish its particulars,” they sought.

The present status of those appeals

The Opposition leaders have asked whether ECINET had maintained an audit trail recording from June 24 last year, for every addition, deletion, modification and restoration of an entry, the user account, the date and time, and the order of the Electoral Registration Officer under which it was made.

The petitioners have sought information on whether any Member of the ECI had “objected to the incorporation of the Declaration (seeking mapping details) in the online Form 6; and, if so, state the date of each objection and the manner in which it was dealt with”.

They also asked for details from each State and Union Territory on the number of objections received under Form 7 (for deletion of voters from the electoral roll), how many of these objections were found to be false or filed in the names of non-existent persons, and if any action had been taken under the Representation of the People Act, 1950.
