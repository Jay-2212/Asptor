Supreme Court dismisses gangster Abu Salem’s plea claiming completion of 25-year prison term

By Aaratrika Bhaumik • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-09-10

The Supreme Court on Thursday dismissed a plea by 1993 Mumbai serial blasts convict Abu Salem seeking release from prison on the ground that he had completed the 25-year jail term he was required to serve.

Mr. Salem was extradited from Portugal to India in 2005 after the Indian government gave a sovereign assurance that he would neither be sentenced to death nor imprisoned for more than 25 years. Following his extradition, he was sentenced to life imprisonment by special courts under the Terrorist and Disruptive Activities (Prevention) Act (TADA) in 2015 for the 1995 murder of businessman Pradeep Jain and in 2017 for his role in the 1993 Mumbai serial blasts.

In view of the sovereign assurance given to Portugal, the Supreme Court in July 2022 commuted the life sentences imposed on Salem in the two cases to a maximum term of 25 years.

A Bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta said it found “no infirmity” in the Bombay High Court’s April 15 ruling dismissing his petition for release, holding that the sovereign assurance could not be used to claim an “artificial or accelerated” computation of his period of incarceration.

“The assurance so given was that the appellant would not be subjected to the death penalty or imprisonment beyond 25 years... The commitment, however, cannot be construed as conferring upon the appellant a right to an artificial or accelerated computation of the period of incarceration,” the Bench said.

The Bench further said that Salem’s claim for release could not be divorced from the “gravity” of the crimes for which he was convicted, which included attempts to “destabilise” the nation.

‘National consequences’

“The offences in question were not merely directed against individual victims; they formed part of acts calculated to destabilise the country and undermine its economic progress by targeting Mumbai, the financial capital of India. The larger national and economic consequences of such acts, therefore, cannot be lost sight of,” the Bench said.

Before the top court, senior advocate Rishi Malhotra, appearing for Salem, had argued that the 25-year jail term should be calculated from September 18, 2002, when he was detained in Portugal following a Red Corner Notice issued by Interpol at India’s instance. He had also contended that the time Salem spent as an undertrial and remission earned in prison should be taken into account while computing his sentence, which meant that Salem had already served 26 years, nine months and 22 days.

The Bench, however, rejected this contention, holding that the method of calculation “lacked legal foundation” as it counted overlapping periods of custody more than once, allowing Salem to claim a “double benefit”. In effect, he had sought to count the same period as undertrial custody in one case and as post-conviction custody in another.

“The distinction sought to be drawn between the expressions ‘undertrial custody’ and ‘post-conviction custody’ cannot justify such overlapping computation... The appellant cannot treat the period preceding the later conviction as undertrial custody for one sentence while simultaneously treating the same period, from the date of the earlier conviction, as post-conviction custody for the other sentence and thereby secure a double benefit,” the Bench said.

The top court also rejected Salem’s claim that three years, six months, and two days of remission earned in prison should be deducted from the 25-year period. It held that his sentence continued to be one of life imprisonment, while the 25-year cap flowed solely from the sovereign assurance given by India to Portugal.

“The appellant continues to stand convicted for life imprisonment, as imposed by the competent courts and affirmed by this court. The stipulation that he cannot be detained beyond 25 years is a consequence of the sovereign assurance extended by the Union government to the Government of Portugal. It does not convert the sentence of life imprisonment into a fixed-term sentence of 25 years,” the Bench said, while noting that the State government’s computation chart placed his probable date of release at January 31, 2046.

In July 2022, the Supreme Court had held that the Centre was bound by the sovereign assurance and would have to advise the President to exercise the power of remission once Salem completed 25 years in prison. It had, however, declined to commute his life sentence or reduce it to a fixed term of 25 years.

The assurance dates back to December 2002, when the then Deputy Prime Minister L.K. Advani assured the Portuguese authorities that Salem would “not [be] visited by death penalty or imprisonment for a term beyond 25 years”. He was subsequently extradited to India in 2005.
