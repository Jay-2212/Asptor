Bisleri controversy: Goa FDA steps up surveillance of packaged water brands

By Lavpreet Kaur • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-24

Bisleri on Tuesday (November 24, 2026) said the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) had found bromate levels within permissible limits in a re-tested sample of a disputed batch of its packaged drinking water manufactured at its Goa plant, while maintaining that it had sought a re-test of another parameter after discrepancies emerged in the findings.

In a statement on November 24, the packaged water company welcomed an FSSAI clarification issued to the media on November 23, concerning an explanation sought from the company over a single sample from batch GO 270, manufactured on September 27, 2025, at its Goa facility.

The matter relates to FSSAI’s examination of samples of Bisleri water in which polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were detected in one of the re-tested samples. PAHs are a group of chemical compounds that can enter food and water through environmental contamination and certain industrial processes. The regulatory scrutiny also involved bromate, a chemical compound whose presence in drinking water is subject to prescribed safety limits.

Bisleri said the FSSAI had categorically clarified that bromate levels in the re-tested sample were below the permissible limit, addressing one of the concerns raised during the testing process.

Explaining the findings on PAHs, the company said six samples containing water from the same source had initially been tested and all six had cleared the prescribed parameters. However, during a subsequent round of testing, one of the six samples from batch GO 270 reportedly recorded PAHs at 0.144 micrograms per litre. Another sample from the same batch cleared all parameters, it said.

Bisleri said it had highlighted the discrepancies in the findings and requested a re-testing of the sample for PAHs in February 2026. It added that it would continue to cooperate with the food safety regulator and defend its position in any proceedings.

The company maintained that the regulatory action was limited to one batch manufactured more than a year ago and that there were no stocks from the batch in the market. It said it produced more than 50,000 batches annually and that the FSSAI’s sale prohibition applied only to batch GO 270.

Seeking to reassure consumers, Bisleri said all its 143 plants across the country remained operational and complied with FSSAI regulations. It added that the Goa plant had not received any direction to suspend operations and that samples from its subsequent production had met all applicable food safety standards.

The company also said that no further adverse findings relating to PAHs had been reported in samples tested by FSSAI across its plants since the issue emerged.

The company added that it would consider appropriate legal recourse against false and misleading propaganda.
