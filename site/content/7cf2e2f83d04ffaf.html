Deepathoon row: Tamil Nadu files a review plea in Supreme Court against Thirupparankundram order

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Forty-eight hours before the lighting of the lamp at the deepathoon on Karthigai Deepam day on November 24, the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious & Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) department filed a review of the Supreme Court’s interim order allowing the “one-time limited arrangement”.

The review jurisdiction is a rare remedy, and not to be used as an appeal in disguise or as an opportunity to re-argue the case on merits. The parties seeking a review of an apex court decision must prove the decision was erroneous on the face of the record. The party urging a re-look of the court order must show that the findings were a result of a factual misconception or had ignored binding judicial precedents or violated a statutory mandate.

The review petition is placed before the same Bench which passed the decision under question. It is usually examined in chambers by circulation. 

On November 20, a Division Bench headed by Justice Aravind Kumar allowed the lighting of the lamp, as a restricted and one-off measure, on the deepathoon at the Thirupparankundram Hill on Karthigai Deepam day (November 24, 2026).

The Supreme Court repeatedly made it plain that this interim order allowing the lighting of the lamp on the deepathoon would not give any vested, enforceable right or entitlement to anyone.

It had underscored that the interim and singular arrangement for November 24, 2026 should not be shown as a precedent for claiming similar permission in any subsequent year.

“Neither the permission granted by this court nor the performance of the ceremony pursuant thereto shall be relied upon by any party as evidence of an established custom, precedent of religious usage, possessory right, entitlement to access or alteration of the existing legal position till adjudication of these appeals,” the court had clarified.

Justice Kumar had clarified that the larger issues of religious rights and tradition raised by the State in its appeal against a January 2026 judgment of a Division Bench of the Madras High Court, which had confirmed an earlier Single Judge direction to the Arulmigu Subramaniswamy Temple devasthanam to light the Karthigai Deepam on the stone pillar located at the summit of the Thirupparankundram Hill near a dargah, would be subject to detailed judicial review.

The Supreme Court had issued notice to the Hindu Dharma Parishad on the appeal. A detailed hearing on merits has been scheduled from the second week of January 2027.

“The issues concerning the nature of the alleged custom, the legal character of the disputed site, the rights flowing from earlier decrees and the statutory authority of the institutions concerned shall receive consideration at the appropriate stage,” the court had said.

The State appeal has vehemently opposed the High Court’s dismissal of its apprehensions that lighting a lamp on the deepathoon adjacent to the dargah would be a sure recipe for “disturbance of public tranquility and peace”. The High Court had dismissed the fears as an “imaginary ghost”.

The HR&CE department had argued that the unsubstantiated claims made by the Hindu Dharma Parishad (which was the petitioner in the High Court) about the “custom” of lighting a Karthigai Deepam on the deepathoon was “calculated to occasion a breach of peace upon a sensitive site with a recorded history of communal tension”.

The State had submitted that there was never any established practice, custom, or even a prior claim of lighting the Karthigai Deepam upon the summit of the Thirupparankundram Hill or on the stone pillar. The government said the Karthigai Deepam had been lit only at the deepa mandapam for over 150 years.

On November 20, the SC had imposed a series of restrictions and safeguards while directing that the Devasthanam would exclusively decide the members of the team to light the lamp on November 24.

The court had ordered that the composition and number of the team would be settled by the District Collector, Madurai, in consultation with the Temple Executive Officer, the Archaeological Survey of India and the Commissioner of Police, Madurai. The District Collector would act as nodal officer for the event. The Commissioner of Madurai Police must provide the ring of security. The Centre was directed to supplement security arrangements with CRPF personnel, if required.

The Bench had banned any kind of procession, public congregation, demonstration, slogan-raising or activity extraneous to the ceremonial lighting of the lamp at the site on the Hill.

The November 20 order saw the apex court emphasise repeatedly that the leeway given for Karthigai Deepam day this year should not be construed as a judicial recognition of an essential religious practice.
