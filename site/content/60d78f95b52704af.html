During his maiden official visit to India, Nepal’s Prime Minister Balendra Shah and PM Narendra Modi flagged off a train service between Ayodhya and Janakpur. The new Ayodhya-Janakpur Saryu Kamala Express is the first direct passenger train service connecting the two cities in India and Nepal.

While Ayodhya is revered as the birthplace of Lord Ram, Janakpur, in Nepal, is widely believed to be the region ruled by Goddess Sita’s father, King Janaka.

What is the route of the new train? What is the significance of Janakpur in the Ramayana? What is the strategic dimension to these cultural initiatives? We explain.

What is the route of the Ayodhya-Janakpur Saryu Kamala Express?

The weekly train service will cover approximately 604 km between Ayodhya Cantt in Uttar Pradesh and Janakpur Dham in Nepal, passing through several towns in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.

The train’s commercial stoppages include Mankapur, Basti and Gorakhpur in Uttar Pradesh; Narkatiaganj, Raxaul, Sitamarhi, Shisho, Madhubani and Jaynagar in Bihar; and Janakpur Dham in Nepal. The journey takes around 19 hours.

The decorated Saryu-Kamala Express inaugural special train, running between Ayodhya in India and Janakpur in Nepal, at the Ayodhya Junction ahead of its flag-off, on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. (PTI Photo)

The service will operate once a week, with its originating days being Saturday from Janakpur and Monday from Ayodhya.

The route is expected to benefit pilgrims travelling between the two religious destinations, as well as passengers travelling for business, education and family-related purposes.

Why is Janakpur significant?

Janakpur, located in Nepal’s Madhesh province in the Terai plains, is an important pilgrimage centre for Hindus. It is associated with the ancient kingdom of Mithila, whose king, Janaka, was Sita’s father. Sita is also known as Janaki, after her father.

The city is home to the Janaki Mandir, a prominent temple dedicated to Sita. Built in 1874 according to the Nepal tourism board, the three-storey structure is known for its blend of Mughal and local architectural styles. It is among Nepal’s most important religious landmarks.

The Janki Mandir in Janakpur, Nepal. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons)

Janakpur is associated with the wedding of Ram and Sita. Every year, the occasion is commemorated during Vivah Panchami, a festival that draws pilgrims from India and Nepal. The Vivah Mandap, near the Janaki Mandir, is traditionally identified as the site of their wedding. The city is also known for its many ponds.

Nepal has been keen to promote Janakpur as an important part of the Ramayana circuit, a network of religious destinations associated with the life of Ram. In December 2023, Nepal’s then Ambassador to India, Shankar Prasad Sharma, described Janakpur as an indispensable part of the circuit and said the two countries were working towards establishing a sister-city relationship between Janakpur and Ayodhya.

Where is Sita’s birthplace?

While some believe Sita’s birthplace to be Janakpur, others regard Punaura Dham, near Sitamarhi in Bihar, as the birthplace.

According to the Ramayana tradition, Sita was found by King Janaka while he was ploughing a field. She is believed to have emerged from the earth, which is also why she is referred to as Bhumija (daughter of the earth). The name Sita itself is associated with the furrow made by a plough.

In a 2023 interview with The Indian Express, Acharya Kishore Kunal, former chairman of the Bihar State Board of Religious Trust, cited the Valmiki Ramayana and historical accounts to argue in favour of Punaura Dham. He also noted that the name Janakpuri is a relatively modern one for the ancient Mithilapuri mentioned in the epic.

The region of Mithila was split between India and Nepal after the Anglo-Nepalese war ended with British victory and the treaty of Sugauli in 1816.

What does this new train mean for India-Nepal ties?

The new train is also part of India’s broader effort to strengthen its relationship with Nepal through cultural and religious connections.

India and Nepal share close historical, religious and people-to-people ties. The Ramayana circuit, which includes important pilgrimage destinations in both countries, and the Buddhist circuit are examples of India’s efforts to develop cross-border religious tourism.

There is also a strategic dimension to these initiatives. China has expanded its economic and infrastructure engagement with Nepal over the years. Strengthening cultural and religious links with Nepal can help India reinforce its traditional influence and maintain close ties with its neighbour.
