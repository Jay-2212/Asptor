    Delimitation long-term plan for forever fascism: Arundhati Roy

    Booker Prize-winning author Arundhati Roy criticised the government’s motives behind the delimitation bill and claimed it is a long-term plan for “forever fascism” that will break the back of democracy.

    Ms. Roy was speaking at the National Conclave of Writers, Artists and Cultural Practitioners at Jawahar Bhawan.

    “Delimitation will break the back of democracy. Do not let them fool you into believing that it is just about increasing the number of constituencies and the number of MPs. Do not believe that the women’s bill has anything to do with women’s rights. This has to do with redrawing the map of constituencies to consolidate the divisions of caste, religion and community,” she claimed.

    Ms. Roy said what has been happening in the country is “democratic fascism, in which fascists are trying to murder the meaning of democracy”.

    Mayawati welcomes Centre’s move for 33% women’s quota in Parliament

    BSP chief Mayawati welcomed the Centre’s move to facilitate 33% reservation for women in Parliament and State Assemblies, but demanded a separate quota for women from Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

    In a long post on X in Hindi, the former Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister said it would have been a historic step if women from SC and ST communities, who have faced exploitation and neglect for centuries, had been given separate reservation within the proposed quota.

    “It would have been good, rather historic, if separate reservation had been provided within this quota for women from SC and ST communities, who have been exploited, oppressed and neglected for centuries,” she said.

    She urged the Centre to consider the demand sympathetically, saying it would be an appropriate and just step.

    Ms. Mayawati said the BSP had consistently championed women’s dignity, security and adequate representation in politics, in keeping with the egalitarian ideals of the Constitution framed under the leadership of B. R. Ambedkar.

    Pawan Kalyan backs proposed delimitation reform

    Andhra Pradesh Deputy Chief Minister Pawan Kalyan expressed support for the proposed delimitation reform, saying it would strengthen democratic representation by giving every state and region an adequate voice in Parliament.

    The Deputy CM said a stronger democracy is built when every citizen is heard, and every state moves forward with confidence.

    “The proposed delimitation reform will strengthen democratic representation by ensuring that every state and region has an adequate voice in Parliament,” said Kalyan in a post on X late on Friday (November 27, 2026).

    According to the Deputy CM, the proposal reflected the spirit of cooperative federalism by recognising the contributions and progress of different states while ensuring that their parliamentary representation was protected.

    He also welcomed the proposed 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, saying it would boost their participation in decision-making.

    Congress' Madhu Goud vows to oppose 50% seat hike

    The proposed 50% increase in Lok Sabha seats has triggered a political divide, with Congress leader Madhu Yashki Goud alleging that the move would put southern states at a permanent disadvantage

    Opposing the proposed increase, Mr. Goud said the Congress, along with its INDIA bloc partners, would fight the Bill during the upcoming Special Session of Parliament, arguing that the Centre had not consulted southern states or Opposition parties before taking the decision.

    Calling the proposal unconstitutional, the Congress leader accused the Centre of not taking Opposition parties into confidence and said the decision raised concerns over cooperative federalism.

    “This is absolutely unconstitutional. The Centre is behaving like a dictator. It has never discussed the matter or taken Opposition parties into confidence. This is in the interest of the nation and will bring in cooperative federalism. Give equal opportunities to the southern states, rather than putting them in a disadvantaged position. The Centre should have consulted the southern states and Opposition parties,” he said.

    Special Parliament sitting eyes Assembly elections: Tejashwi

    RJD working president Tejashwi Yadav has voiced suspicion that the upcoming special sitting of Parliament may have been called with an eye on Assembly elections, due in a few months, in several states.

    Talking to reporters here on Friday (November 27, 2026), Mr. Yadav, also the Leader of Opposition in the Bihar Assembly, made it clear that his party would stick to its “old stand” on the delimitation bill, which, according to reports, may be tabled in Parliament afresh.

    “It may be that the sitting has been called in view of the elections,” said the former Deputy Chief Minister, upon being asked whether he thought the ruling BJP-led NDA might try to cash in on the issue of women’s reservation, linked to the Bill, in the assembly polls in Uttar Pradesh and Punjab.

    Asked about his party’s stand on the bill, which needs a two-thirds majority to get cleared, the RJD leader said, “It would have been better had the government taken all political groups into confidence. Nonetheless, our old stand is known to all.”

    Mr. Yadav, also a key INDIA bloc partner, sought to make light of Union Home Minister Amit Shah’s call for implementation of the Uniform Civil Code in all BJP-ruled states.
