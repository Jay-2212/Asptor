Cyclone Akvan brews over Bay of Bengal: What you need to know

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

A weather system brewing over the Bay of Bengal is expected to intensify into a severe cyclonic storm over the coming days, according to the Indian Meteorological Department (IMD).

The system has intensified into a Cyclonic Storm on Monday (November 16, 2026) evening, and is moving north-northwestwards towards Andhra Pradesh and has been named ‘Akvan’, a name suggested by Iran under the North Indian Ocean naming list.

What is Cyclone Akvan’s current trajectory?

It originated as a well-marked low-pressure area over the southeast Bay of Bengal on Saturday (November 14, 2026), the system intensified into a depression by Sunday (November 15, 2026) morning.

The storm may continue to move north-northwestwards and intensify into a Severe Cyclonic Storm over westcentral Bay of Bengal by Tuesday (November 17, 2026) evening and may cross the A.P. coast around noon on November 20, as a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm, with windspeed gusting to 145 kmph, similar to that of Arnab. The system is forecast to bypass Tamil Nadu and move toward the Andhra Pradesh coast around November 19, 2026, also impacting Odisha.

Rainfall alerts across States

Heavy rainfall warnings have been issued for coastal Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and Andaman and Nicobar Islands through November 20, 2026. In West Bengal, heavy rain is forecast for South 24 Parganas and Purba Medinipur from November 19 to 21, 2026, with downpour likely in the southern districts of Howrah, Paschim Medinipur and Jhargram on November 20 and 21.

According to the IMD, thunderstorms, accompanied with winds up to 40 kmph in Kolkata and most districts of Gangetic West Bengal is also likely.

A nip in the air over South Bengal is likely to dissipate as day and night temperatures will rise by two to three degrees Celsius from November 18, 2026, it said.

Cyclone Akvan is expected to cause rains in north coastal districts in Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal area, it said.

Unusual weather patterns

Along the north Andhra coast, at Visakhapatnam Fishing Harbour, the Fisheries Department and Indian Coast Guard have recalled trawlers and halted marine operations.

Andhra Pradesh already endured Cyclone Arnab in late October and Murasu this November, local boat crews face mounting tension and loss of of wages as they secure vessels against these adverse conditions. According to Regional Meteorological Centre officials, the formation of three cyclones within a single month is unusual and maybe linked to a strong El Niño and warm sea surface temperatures that may be triggering these disturbances.
