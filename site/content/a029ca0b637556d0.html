ECI still insisting on Form 6 for excluded voters in Delhi SIR, petitioners make urgent mentioning in Supreme Court

By The Hindu Bureau • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-04

The Supreme Court on Wednesday assured petitioners that no voter will face criminal prosecution, even as advocate Prashant Bhushan said the Election Commission of India (ECI) is still insisting that voters excluded after the special intensive revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in Delhi, under the Absent, Shifted, Dead, or Duplicate (ASDD) categories, must fill up Form 6, which is meant exclusively for new voters.

On October 28, the Supreme Court had made it clear that the ECI was in “error” to force pre-existing voters, deleted from the electoral rolls, to apply as new voters under Form 6 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.

The petitioners, activists Anjali Bhardwaj and Amrita Johri, represented by Mr. Bhushan and advocate Cheryl D’souza, had submitted on October 28 that the ECI was compelling voters to misrepresent themselves as new applicants, an offence which attracts imprisonment.

EC ignores SC nudge to use Form 8

On October 28, the top court had orally nudged the ECI, saying that such purged voters should be allowed to use Form 8, which voters can use to apply for a change in residence, or an enumeration form for the SIR process.

On Wednesday, Mr. Bhushan said voters were still in the same quandary despite the SC’s remarks. He referred to a November 3 press note issued by the ECI which had clarified that the submission of Form 6 for the purpose of inclusion in the electoral roll under SIR or afterwards was “not, by itself, false merely because the elector was previously enrolled”.

The ECI note had promised that no penal action would be taken under Section 31 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 by Electoral Registration Officers against electors “merely on the ground that they had been included in a previous electoral roll and had submitted Form 6 during the SIR process or afterwards”.

‘Horns of a dilemma’

Mr. Bhushan said the ECI note clearly showed that the court’s oral observations to allow pre-existing voters to apply through Form 8 or their enumeration forms were not put into practice, leaving the voters to still apply through Form 6 and risk prosecution.

“Now they are in the horns of a dilemma,” Mr. Bhushan submitted.

“We are dealing with the matter, how can they feel like that,” the CJI said.

The Bench asked the petitioners to file an application on this aspect, and said the matter would be heard on November 19.

The CJI noted Mr. Bhushan’s submission that the ECI has extended the deadline for filing claims and objections to November 25 from October 30. If required, the Bench said that the ECI could be asked to extend the date further.
