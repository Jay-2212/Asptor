Supreme Court flags ‘active connivance’ of civic authorities in ‘flagrant’

By Aaratrika Bhaumik • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-24

The Supreme Court on Monday (November 23, 2026) observed that unauthorised constructions often flourished with the “active connivance” of civic authorities in the hope that State governments would eventually regularise such structures, particularly ahead of elections. The court sought suggestions from States and Union Territories on how to address such flagrant violations of building bye-laws.

“The state of municipal inspection in our cities, for example, is in shambles, to say the least. It is often undertaken after the building has risen and the violation has become a fait accompli. Such inspections are, to say the least, a sham. It is also an open secret that unauthorised construction often flourishes with the active connivance of municipal authorities, hoping that State governments would ultimately regularise the same, particularly before an impending election,” a Bench of Justices B.V. Nagarathna and Sanjeev Sachdeva observed.

The Bench was hearing a case arising from Tamil Nadu concerning construction in violation of land-use norms and building bye-laws. The proceedings had earlier been heard extensively by a Bench headed by Justice Ahsanuddin Amanullah, which had expanded the ambit of the case to examine similar violations across the country. It had sought details from civic bodies in several State capitals on action taken against illegal constructions, besides issuing contempt notices to officials for failing to comply with its directions.

The case was, however, recently reassigned to the Bench headed by Justice Nagarathna following an administrative decision by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant. Another public interest litigation, which arose from a private university student’s grievance, was subsequently expanded into a nationwide judicial inquiry on the functioning of private universities, including concessions granted to them, was also transferred from Justice Amanullah’s Bench.

‘Practical difficulties’

On Monday (November 23, 2026), the Bench headed by Justice Nagarathna pointed to the “practical difficulties” of monitoring building bye-law violations nationwide, noting that differing regulations across States and Union Territories made it impractical to issue omnibus directions.

“Every State and Union Territory has its respective laws and bye-laws which are unique to each State and Union Territory... It is neither practical to pass common orders applicable to all States and Union Territories nor is it feasible for this court to examine breaches of building bye-laws during construction or repairs, State-wise and on a case-to-case basis,” the Bench observed.

Justice Nagarathna also cautioned that such sweeping directions could prompt private parties accused of violating sanctioned building plans to approach the top court for relief, while municipal authorities might invoke its orders to “run roughshod” over stays and other directions issued by tribunals or High Courts.

“We don’t want to be a party to such kind of chaos in the legal field,” she remarked.

The Bench accordingly confined the scope of the proceedings and directed parties to seek relief in their individual cases before the appropriate municipal authorities, tribunals or High Courts. It clarified that its intervention would be limited to seeking suggestions from State governments on tackling flagrant violations of building bye-laws and inaction by civic authorities.

The Bench also held that any action taken by civic authorities pursuant to orders earlier passed by Justice Amanullah’s Bench could be challenged before the appropriate forum, and that the pendency of the proceedings would not preclude municipal corporations and other authorities from acting in accordance with law.

‘Flagrant violations’

Observing that a city was a “shared civic space” rather than merely an aggregation of individual properties and entitlements, the Bench underlined that compliance with building regulations was an “elementary expression” of the fundamental duties enshrined in the Constitution and the civic responsibilities of both citizens and municipal authorities.

“We are disturbed to note the flagrant violations throughout the country. The Constitution of India envisages citizenship as participation in a common civic enterprise. Our cities, in turn, demand the same compact from citizens, namely that private ambition must remain within public limits. At the same time, we are also conscious that the rule of law is rendered hollow if the authority charged with enforcing it does not enforce it,” the Bench said.

It indicated that it would issue further directions at subsequent hearings to “strengthen the hands of municipal authorities and State governments” in acting against such violations. The matter was posted for further hearing on January 6, 2027, when the court will consider the suggestions submitted by the respective State governments.
