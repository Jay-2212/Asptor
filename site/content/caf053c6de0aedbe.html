Asian and American stock markets were in a lull in November amid anxiety that the AI (artificial intelligence) bubble could pop. That was until Jensen Huang’s comeback into the top job at Nvidia, which lifted these AI-driven markets to their best day in months.

After a sharp crash following Huang’s resignation earlier in the month, the US’ clean chit on the Chinese spy controversy and his comeback to Nvidia led to the chipmaker regaining nearly a third of its value, lifting other AI-led markets in the process.

Lull period for AI-led markets

The US markets have had a poor month so far, dropping as much as 11% until the sharp gains on Monday. The AI-related fears have pulled down the share prices of big names like Google’s parent company Alphabet, the newly-listed Anthropic, and Meta, among others.

These AI fears deepened after Huang, a top name in the AI space, exited the company, and after Democrats’ thumping win in the mid-term elections sparked concerns about a slowdown in AI spending.

The rut had been far deeper for Asian markets that are largely driven by AI. After reaching record highs amid AI optimism earlier this year, the South Korean, Japanese, and Taiwanese markets had fallen sharply from their peaks till Monday. The South Korean Kospi index had lost nearly half its value. The Japanese and Taiwanese indices had lost 20-30%.

While the US had some of the biggest names in the AI and tech space, Asian markets like Taiwan and South Korea are largely driven by AI stocks. For example, chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix hold almost half the weightage on the Kospi, while TSMC alone accounts for 40% of Taiwan’s benchmark index.

Huang’s return lifts sentiment

Nvidia’s board announced Huang’s return late on Monday, days after ruling out any such possibilities. US President Donald Trump also expressed support for this decision, giving Huang a clean chit on his extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

His reinstatement lifted shares of Nvidia, which had crashed over 40% since his extramarital affair allegations first surfaced in late October, by 31.5% – the biggest single-day gain since 2003. Other AI players like Meta, Alphabet, and Oracle shot up by 6-12%. This lifted the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite index by 6.5%, helping Wall Street recover from the 8% selloff last week.

“Nvidia is the biggest and most important name in AI, and the whole industry is dependent on their chips. And Nvidia and Huang are synonymous. So his comeback is a big positive for the industry in terms of stability and direction,” a head of research at a domestic broking firm said.

Major firms like Morgan Stanley and Bernstein, which had downgraded their view on Nvidia after Huang’s exit, calling it a “big setback”, also started to reverse their opinion after his comeback. Bernstein, for example, upgraded their rating on the stock on Monday, just days after downgrading it post Huang’s resignation.

The comeback also sparked a rally across Asia. The Kospi jumped 7% early on Tuesday. Taiwan’s Taiex index skyrocketed 9%. Japan’s Nikkei 225 also gained 4%. While the Indian markets are shut on the occasion of Prakash Gurpurb Sri Guru Nanak Dev, the GIFT Nifty was up 0.4%, signalling that the benchmark Nifty 50 and Sensex may open on a positive note on Wednesday.
