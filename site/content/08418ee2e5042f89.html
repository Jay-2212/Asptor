No proof from ECI to show SIR was a ‘unanimous’ decision, Rahul Gandhi to Supreme Court

By Krishnadas Rajagopal • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-17

Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi told the Supreme Court on Monday (November 16, 2026) that the Election Commission of India (ECI) failed to produce any proof to establish its claim that the special intensive revision (SIR) was a “unanimous” decision made by Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar, and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi.

“No minutes of the meeting, file notings, recorded approvals, agenda, deliberations or other primary material evidencing the participation and approval of all three members have been produced with the ECI counter-affidavit of October 26,” Mr. Gandhi submitted in his rejoinder.

Interrogatories filed

Mr. Gandhi and other INDIA bloc leaders have also separately filed an application containing a set of “interrogatories” directed at the CEC, the two Election Commissioners, and other senior ECI officials involved in the SIR. The pointed questions inquire about the number and details of each SIR file, as well as the specific occasions between February 19, 2025, and October 26, 2026, on which the two Election Commissioners objected in writing or expressed dissent or reservation regarding any matter related to the SIR, ECINET, Form 6, or the filing of appeals.

The case is scheduled for hearing on November 17 before a Bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant.

The reported friction between Mr. Kumar and the two Election Commissioners over the SIR and the CEC’s allegedly unilateral and authoritarian conduct, had triggered widespread protests across India demanding his removal from office.

The ECI’s October 26 affidavit, in response to a petition filed by INDIA bloc leaders led by Mr. Gandhi, denied any differences of opinion between the CEC and the Election Commissioners over the SIR process.

Mr. Gandhi said the court must not accept such “bare assertions” from the ECI. He questioned why the affidavit was filed by the ECI when Mr. Kumar, Mr. Sandhu and Mr. Joshi were separately made respondents.

The Opposition leader, represented by senior advocate A.M. Singhvi and advocate Prasanna S., said the EC chose not to explain when and where exactly the decision to conduct a nationwide SIR was made.

The ECI affidavit’s blank assertions contradict its own Right to Information (RTI) response that “the Commission has so far not taken any decision on initiating a special intensive revision (SIR) across the country in 2025”.

Mr. Gandhi said the ECI’s claim of a “unanimous” Commission did not quite agree with the fact that Mr. Sandhu and Mr. Joshi took the unprecedented step of writing directly to the Cabinet Secretary, conveying their displeasure over the conduct of affairs within the poll body during the SIR exercise.

“That two members of the Commission found it necessary to address the Cabinet Secretary, and to issue a joint direction of their own, is itself inconsistent with the picture of unanimous functioning,” the LoP said, adding their communications cannot be brushed aside as “internal differences or administrative exchanges”.

Form 6 issues

Mr. Gandhi questioned the logic behind the EC’s claim that the additional declaration form annexed to the statutory Form 6 was separate and “not a part of Form 6”.

He pointed out that the declaration “was entered into the online page of Form 6” and that the online application and declaration were combined for the purpose of a single OTP and electronic signature.

“An applicant accessing the online Form 6 process was required to encounter and complete the additional declaration as part of that process,” his rejoinder argued.

He highlighted the statements made by the ECI in its October 26 affidavit that over 16 lakh appeals against inclusion of voters in West Bengal were filed by officers of the State’s Chief Electoral Office, and that too, without consulting the poll body.

“This confirms the very grievance reportedly raised by the Election Commissioners, namely, that appeals were filed in the name of the Commission without the knowledge of its Members,” Mr. Gandhi contended.

If not, the ECI has to bring on record relevant approvals for filing such appeals against inclusions in the West Bengal electoral roll.

Opaque ECI platforms

The LoP said the ECI’s explanations about ECINET and ERONET, the digital platforms managing voter registration and electoral rolls, failed to address specific concerns raised in court about their design, configuration, operation and control.

The petitioners argued that the functioning of ECINET and ERONET systems was both centralised and opaque, which led to large-scale exclusion of electors.

Mr. Gandhi urged the court to note that the ECI was “entirely silent” on several issues like the reduction of approximately 20.66 lakh names from the electoral roll of Punjab and the approximately 12.8 lakh electors, the non-availability of the orders and guidelines governing the 2003 intensive revision used as a benchmark for mapping electors, the bulk filing of objections in Form 7, including in allegedly forged names, and the analysis indicating that women constituted approximately 62% of the electors excluded in West Bengal.

“The present proceedings cannot be disposed of on the basis of general denials or subsequent explanations and showcasing of woefully belated course-correction,” Mr. Gandhi requested the court.
