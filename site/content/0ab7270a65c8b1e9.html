India Inc reported record profit during second quarter of the financial year 2027 (Q2FY27), outperforming the Street’s expectations. This comes despite the challenges, which included high raw material costs across most industries due to the ongoing West Asia war.

The 2,600-odd listed companies tracked by the Bank of Baroda reported a record net profit of around Rs 4.5 lakh crore during Q2FY27, up nearly 26% year-on-year (y-o-y). The net sales of these companies grew 21% to around Rs 36 lakh crore, said Aditi Gupta, an economist at the bank.

So, who is making the most money among these companies, and where is the cash going?

Who makes money?

Reliance Industries Ltd earned the most profit in the Indian listed space, with its oil business boosting its net profit for the July-September quarter to a record Rs 29,550 crore. State Bank of India’s net profit came in at Rs 23,038 crore. Indian Oil Corporation Ltd reported a profit of Rs 21,630 crore, a sharp reversal from the Rs 2,661 crore loss in the previous quarter.

HDFC Bank Ltd and Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Ltd reported profits of Rs 19,510 crore and Rs 18,538 crore.

Net profit of top five companies.

Together, the top five accounted for a quarter of India Inc’s total profits.

Collectively, the top 10, which also consists of ICICI Bank Ltd, Tata Consultancy Services Ltd, LIC, Bharti Airtel Ltd, and Infosys Ltd, accounted for 38% of India Inc’s profits and nearly 47% of its revenues.

In fact, the top 50 companies, or Nifty 50, make up around 55% of India Inc’s total profits, highlighting that only a handful of companies are driving aggregate profit to record levels. The pace of profit growth was strong all around, with earnings of Nifty 50 companies growing at around 27% y-o-y, while Motilal Oswal’s coverage universe of around 400 companies saw around 32% growth.

The growth has been driven by higher volumes across most sectors such as automobiles, banking, and oil, even as margins remain under pressure due to higher costs in the wake of the war in West Asia.

Banks, financial services companies, and oil exploration companies were among the top performers during the quarter, as a high interest rate environment and high crude oil prices proved to be tailwinds. In fact, in the first half of FY27, the profitability of public-sector banks jumped 17% y-o-y to a record Rs 1.1 lakh crore.

Where the cash is going

While Indian companies historically get the heat for not reinvesting enough into their businesses and instead hold on to large piles of cash and pay out substantial dividends, data shows that these companies may finally be spending these increased earnings on capital expenditure. As per Emkay Global, operating cash flow to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA) ratio of top 500 BSE companies fell to 64% in first half of FY26 from nearly 83% in FY26 and well below the 78-83% seen across FY22-26. This meant companies had lower free cash on their books compared to their operating profit.  “The weakness was concentrated in the cyclicals. Discretionary (25.4% vs 68.0% in FY26), industrials (44.3%), and materials (42.9%). Energy (82.9% vs 106.5%) also moderated,” the report said.

“Some of this is likely due to working-capital build-up in a high-growth phase, and half-year ratios are not strictly comparable with full-year ones; even so, this is the number to track in 2HFY27,” it added.

While this does not mean that cash is being reinvested, as companies also pay out large dividends, data shows that capital expenditure from corporate sector has been picking up in recent times.

While official data for FY27 is not yet out, the Reserve Bank of India’s projections show that private corporate investment may rise about 23% to Rs 3.2 lakh crore in FY27.
