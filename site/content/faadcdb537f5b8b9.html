India’s Hindu and Muslim populations will grow by 2070, but only Muslims’ share will rise. Here’s why

By 2070, Hindus will continue to comprise the largest religious group in India but will form a smaller share of the total population — around 75.8%, compared to 79.4% in 2020. The share of Muslims, the second-largest religious group, will rise from 15.2% in 2020 to 19.6% by 2070. The absolute number of both Hindus and Muslims will continue to rise until then.

These findings come from a new Pew Research Center report. The US-based institution regularly analyses and projects trends in demography and religion.

The latest report (“The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2020-2070”) was published on Wednesday (November 18) and highlights a major global trend that will be witnessed much sooner. “For centuries, Christianity has been the world’s largest religion. That may soon change. Muslims are expected to outnumber Christians by 2035,” the report says.

Here is what it says about India’s religious composition, as well as the larger changes unfolding globally.

What does the report forecast about India’s religious composition?

India religious composition via Pew Research.

Pew considers national surveys and Census data to estimate population changes. It notes that events such as wars, epidemics and natural disasters could affect population changes, but those are outside its ambit. In India’s case, it also noted that the last completed Census was in 2011 and served as the basis of estimation.

With these caveats, here are some of the report’s highlights with respect to India:

* The reason that the share of Muslims will grow as a percentage, even though both Hindu and Muslim populations will increase in absolute numbers, is “mostly because Muslims are younger and have more children than Hindus.”

* While India’s overall fertility rate has fallen rapidly in recent decades, including that of Muslims, it found that “Muslims in India still have more children per woman than Hindus do.” Fertility rate is the average number of children a woman is expected to bear in her lifetime.

As The Indian Express previously reported, according to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare’s National Family Health Survey (NFHS) – 5 from 2022, Muslims’ fertility rate saw the sharpest decline among all religious communities over the past two decades. It fell to 2.3 in 2019-2021 from 2.6 in 2015-16, but was still the highest among all religious communities at the time. It is expected that the gap among communities will continue to narrow over time.

* “Given this fertility gap and Muslims’ younger age profile, Hindus are projected to decline as a portion of the Indian population, while Muslims are expected to rise,” the report adds.

* India has the largest share of Hindus in the world — around 94.5% of the total global Hindu population. “The number of Hindus around the world is projected to increase by 199 million between 2020 and 2070, reaching a total of nearly 1.4 billion,” it says. Non-Hindus, however, will grow at an even faster rate. “As a result, Hindus will shrink slightly as a share of the global population, falling from 15% in 2020 to 14% in 2070.”

* By 2070, India will have the world’s second-largest population of Muslims at 334 million, only behind Pakistan at 461 million. Currently, Indonesia has the largest population of Muslims of any country, while India stands at the third position after Pakistan.

What explains these numbers?

India is the most populous country in the world. Even as fertility rates have fallen significantly in just a few decades, due to improvements in health and infrastructure, women’s education, and shifts in societal attitudes, it continues to have a young population.

As Pew’s report puts it, “The youthfulness of a population is an important factor in future growth.” All else being equal, a younger population means more people in their prime childbearing years, meaning faster population growth than in an ageing society like, say, Japan.

Combined with the age factor, Hindus and Muslims have a 99% “retention rate” — meaning they are extremely unlikely to change their religions over their lifetime — and indicate potential for population growth within those groups

And what are the major global trends?

ON MUSLIMS OVERTAKING CHRISTIANS: The report says that by 2070, if recent trends continue, there will be more than 3.6 billion Muslims and just under 3 billion Christians worldwide.

It notes that Muslims’ high global fertility is in part driven by sub-Saharan Africa, which is one of the world’s poorest regions. “With a total fertility rate of 5.1 children per woman, Muslims in sub-Saharan Africa far surpass Muslims elsewhere in fertility.” Across religions, too, the region is expected to see higher-than-average population growth in the near future, even as much of the world is witnessing declining fertility rates.

ON THE DECLINE AMONG CHRISTIANS: “By 2070, the number of Christians living in sub-Saharan Africa is expected to rise by 896 million, while the number of Christians in the rest of the world is expected to fall by 210 million,” the report says.

The United States currently has the largest Christian population of any country in the world, but the Democratic Republic of the Congo (also a sub-Saharan nation) is projected to assume the top slot by 2070. Declines are also expected in Europe from both demographic factors (i.e., aging populations with low birth rates) and religious switching.

ON PEOPLE WITHOUT RELIGIOUS AFFILIATIONS: “People who have no religious affiliation – sometimes called religious ‘nones’ – are projected to drop substantially as a share of the world population, from 24% in 2020 to 17% in 2070. This is a function of the decline in the overall population of China, which has been contracting for a few years now. The communist nation has strict controls on the practice of religion.

However, 41% of North Americans will be religiously unaffiliated in 2070, up from 30% in 2020. Europe’s non-affiliated people will comprise 33% of the population by then, up from 25% in 2020.

OTHER RELIGIONS: “All other religions combined (including Baha’is, Daoists, Jains, Sikhs, adherents of folk religions and many other groups) are expected to experience a modest increase in number. However, they will slip below 2% of the global population by 2070,” the report adds.
