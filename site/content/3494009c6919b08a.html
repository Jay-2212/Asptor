CJP plans to take ‘Cockroach Club’ across India, shifts youth movement beyond street protests

By Lavpreet Kaur • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-22

Moving beyond street mobilisation, the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) is looking to build a nationwide network of cultural and discussion spaces with its newly launched ‘Cockroach Club’. The party wants the initiative to reach every part of the country, including remote villages, as it seeks to give young people a platform to debate public issues, exchange ideas and envision the future they want for India, its co-convenor Ashutosh Ranka said during an interaction with The Hindu.

The club’s first gathering, titled ‘Legacy of Bapu’, was held in Pune on November 21 and brought together more than 300 people for an event lasting around three hours. The programme featured comedy, satire and conversations on Mahatma Gandhi, while participants discussed local issues concerning Maharashtra. Performances addressing larger issues facing the country were also part of the event.

“This is a platform where we can all come together and articulate the future we want for ourselves and answer some difficult questions we want to address. The idea of Cockroach Clubs is to have a space where people can come, talk, brainstorm, collaborate and envision what they want our country to be,” Mr. Ranka said.

Explaining why Pune was chosen for the first gathering, he said the party had a large support base in Maharashtra, while Delhi was also an important centre for the organisation and would soon feature in its plans.

“The idea is for the Cockroach Club to go to every corner of the country. This is a truly national movement and everything has to come together,” he added.

CJP co-convenor Saurav Das said the club was envisioned as a “physical space for the younger generation” to come together, exchange ideas, question, learn and grow. It would host lectures and discussions on the ideas of Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, B.R. Ambedkar and other thinkers, besides film screenings, live performances and stand-up comedy.

The space would also offer young people a quiet corner to read, study, and prepare for examinations, while allowing them to unwind and interact, he said.

“This was just a trailer. As we go ahead, we will know what can be bettered. We will use this platform to discuss how to take this movement forward, how to get more and more people to join, and channelise the energy of the youth in a structured manner to bring a change in the country,” Mr. Das said.

He said the club would encourage young people to think for themselves rather than have opinions handed down to them or ideologies imposed on them. “We the young will develop our Idea of India at the Cockroach Club,” he said.

Funding, format and the name

The first event was not ticketed, Mr. Ranka said. The local Pune team initially pooled money to organise the event, followed by local fundraising. A collection was also made at the venue, where participants contributed to their capacity.

The party is yet to decide how it will fund the initiative as it expands. It could consider introducing tickets for future events or continue with the local fundraising model, he said.

The format of future gatherings is also being discussed. Mr. Ranka said the party was considering moving away from having a specific theme for every event and making the gatherings a more regular activity. It is also exploring whether the events could be presented as regular episodes, along the lines of stand-up comedy shows on OTT platforms.

The choice of the name ‘Cockroach Club’ has drew criticism online, particularly given that the first event centred on Gandhi’s legacy. Responding to the criticism, Mr. Ranka said the younger generation was an “unseriously serious generation” and that the initiative needed to reflect that sensibility. “Everything can’t always be 100% serious,” he said.

Netizens also questioned whether the CJP is shifting its focus from street mobilisation to stand-up comedy and other cultural activities.

The party initially gained attention by channelising public anger over unemployment, examination irregularities and the lack of opportunities for young people. Its supporters took these demands beyond social media and onto the streets, which led to the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan on July 25, 2026.

Mr. Ranka, however, described the club as part of a larger national effort to bring young people together, deliberate on the country’s pressing concerns and work towards shaping its future.
