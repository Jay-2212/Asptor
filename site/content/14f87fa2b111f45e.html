Supreme Court issues notice on contempt plea against lawyer over remarks about CJI

By Aaratrika Bhaumik • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-19

The Supreme Court on Wednesday (November 18, 2026) issued notice on a petition filed by the Supreme Court Bar Association (SCBA) seeking initiation of contempt proceedings against a lawyer for allegedly making disparaging remarks about Chief Justice of India Surya Kant in open court on November 16.

A Bench of Justices Dipankar Datta, Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana observed that a prima facie case had been made out for proceeding against advocate Pravin Singhal. The Bench noted that the petition was accompanied by the written consent of the Attorney General for India (AG) under Section 15(1)(b) of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, a statutory prerequisite for a private party seeking to initiate criminal contempt proceedings.

“Having perused the papers before us, which include the consent of the AG under Section 15(1)(b) of the Contempt Act, we are prima facie satisfied that the motion of SCBA for initiation of [proceedings] against the sole respondent referred in the petition requires to be taken forward,” the Bench said.

The court sought Mr. Singhal’s response by January 8 and directed him to remain personally present when the matter is heard on January 20.

Solicitor General Tushar Mehta supported the SCBA’s petition, submitting that “the majesty of the court cannot be compromised”. The Bench indicated that his assistance would be required in the proceedings.

SCBA president and senior advocate Pradeep Kumar Rai described the alleged remarks as “unacceptable” and urged the court to deal sternly with such conduct.

“The contemnor [Mr. Singhal] is not a member of SCBA, but it has been observed that several lawyers come to courts to make videos and the authorities are unable to do anything as the social media platforms are required to remove them. Such persons feel no action can be taken against them… The language used by them against the judiciary is unacceptable,” he submitted.

The Bench, however, pointed out that although the SCBA had sought directions against certain social media platforms, it had not impleaded them as parties to the proceedings. It accordingly permitted the Association to amend its petition.

Earlier, SCBA secretary Praghya Baghel and joint secretary Meenesh Dubey had informed the CJI, during an oral mentioning, that Attorney General R. Venkataramani had granted the requisite statutory consent to initiate contempt proceedings against the lawyer. The office-bearers had also claimed to possess recordings of Mr. Singhal making objectionable remarks against the CJI on previous occasions.

The incident occurred shortly after the Bench, also comprising Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana, assembled at 2 p.m. on November 16. Mr. Singhal complained that he had sought urgent listing of a matter on November 2 but had received no response for nearly a fortnight.

When the CJI assured him that he would examine the matter, Mr. Singhal insisted that it was urgent and that he was unwilling to wait. He then allegedly made a derogatory remark questioning the CJI’s integrity, following which the audio-video feed of the proceedings was immediately paused. Security personnel subsequently escorted him out of the courtroom.

The SCBA subsequently approached Mr. Venkataramani, seeking his consent to initiate criminal contempt proceedings against Mr. Singhal.

“Having regard to the extremely scandalous and unpardonable conduct and remarks in the open court by Pravin Singhal, I consider it a fit case for granting my consent under Section 15(1)(b) of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 for initiating criminal contempt against Pravin Singhal,” Mr. Venkataramani said while granting consent.

The proceedings come amid several instances in recent months of the Supreme Court initiating contempt proceedings over allegedly derogatory remarks directed at the CJI and the judiciary.

Earlier this month, the court issued contempt show-cause notices to office-bearers of the Kerala Pradesh Congress Committee over a since-deleted social media post targeting the CJI. Observing that “the independence of the judiciary is not a matter of political convenience”, a Bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and Vipul M. Pancholi had remarked that while judicial decisions were open to criticism, any attempt to undermine the authority of the court requires to be “dealt with an iron hand”.

The same Bench is also hearing a criminal contempt petition filed by the SCBA against Shiv Sena (UBT) leader and Rajya Sabha MP Sanjay Raut and journalist Nikhil Wagle over allegedly objectionable social media posts concerning the CJI and the judiciary. During Tuesday’s hearing, Justice Kumar observed that Mr. Raut, as an “experienced parliamentarian”, ought to exercise restraint in his public statements. Referring to Mr. Wagle’s remarks, the judge said they were deeply objectionable and cautioned that professional recognition or awards did not entitle journalists to use unparliamentary language.
