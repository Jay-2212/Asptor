What the Ayodhya-Janakpur train says about India’s railway diplomacy

The Prime Ministers of India and Nepal, Narendra Modi and Balendra Shah, Monday flagged off the first passenger train between the two countries from Ayodhya to Janakpur. Named Sarayu-Kamala Express, the train will cover a distance of over 600 km in around 19 hours and will have stops at Gorakhpur, Basti, Raxaul, Sitamarhi, Madhubani, and Jaynagar.

The move will not only strengthen cross-border connectivity and diplomacy between the two countries, but it also provides a link between two significant religious sites for Hindus — Ayodhya (for Ram) and Janakpur (for Sita).

The project is also part of India Railways’ ambitious Ramayana, Buddhist and Shiv-Shakti circuits by linking Janakpur-Ayodhya, Lumbini-Bodhgaya-Sarnath-Kushinagar and Kathmandu (Pashupatinath)-Varanasi.

“In 2025 alone, around 30 crore pilgrims from across the country and the world visited Ayodhya. With the launch of the Saryu-Kamala Express train service today, we are adding a new dimension to this sacred bond. This will now make it easier for pilgrims visiting Ayodhya to travel to Janakpur to seek blessings of Mata Sita,” said PM Modi in his address.

He further said that India will work together to enhance connectivity between sacred sites in both countries.

Key projects

India and Nepal share religious, cultural and historical ties. The movement of people between the two countries has remained uninterrupted over the years, with people from either side frequently crossing the border for jobs, tourism, marriages, etc. The Railways have now undertaken several projects traversing these regions to facilitate the seamless cross-border movement of people.

Jaynagar-Bijalpura-Bardibas and Jogbani-Biratnagar are two important ongoing railway projects between India and Nepal. Apart from this, the Raxaul-Kathmandu railway line is another ambitious cross-border project.

Along with the flagging of the express train, the two leaders announced the finalisation of the Field Location Survey for the proposed Raxaul-Kathmandu electrified broad-gauge railway line, and announced that the two sides will collaborate in the development of the East-West Railway in Nepal.

“Apart from these projects, as part of capacity building, Nepal Railway officers and staff have been imparted training on various aspects related to train operations at Gatishakti Vishwavidyalaya, Vadodara,” said a senior Railway official.

Other cross-border projects

Nepal is not the only neighbouring country where Indian Railways is looking to extend its network. Last year in September, the government announced two cross-border railway projects to connect Bhutan with the bordering areas of Assam and West Bengal. The 69-km Kokrajhar (Assam)-Gelephu (Bhutan) and 20-km Banarhat (West Bengal)-Samtse (Bhutan) lines will cost Rs 3,456 crore and Rs 577 crore, respectively.

Currently, the Himalayan nation Bhutan does not have a railway network. It was the result of almost two decades of efforts made by India and Bhutan to formally announce the two strategic projects.

Earlier, India operated cross-border trains Maitree Express and Bandhan Express to Bangladesh, which was suspended in July 2024 following the unrest in the neighbouring country. Bandhan Express used to run between Kolkata (India) and Khulna (Bangaldesh) and Maitree Express between Kolkata to Dhaka Cantonment.

Indian Railways earlier also operated the famous Samjhauta Express and Thar Express between India and Pakistan. Both trains were suspended in August 2019 due to sustained tension between both countries after the government revoked the special status of Jammu & Kashmir.

India has also planned a rail link to Myanmar. The Jiribam-Imphal section (125 Km), which falls within Indian territory, is under construction. The remaining portion from Imphal to Moreh in India is yet to take off and the section from Tamu to Kalay (128) in Myanmar has not been sanctioned.
