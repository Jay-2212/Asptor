Mango mix-up: Why Maharashtra and Gujarat farmers are fighting over Hapus tag

A dispute over the name Hapus has pitted mango growers in Maharashtra’s Konkan region against those from Gujarat’s Valsad. While Konkan’s Alphonso mangoes received a Geographical Indication (GI) tag in 2018, an application seeking recognition for Valsad Hapus has been opposed by Konkan growers. They fear that letting another region use the name Hapus could weaken the identity and market value they have built over years.

The Geographical Indications Registry had suggested that the two sides explore a settlement, but Konkan’s GI tag holders have rejected a compromise. The final hearing has now concluded, with the decision reserved.

What is the dispute about, and what is at stake?

What does the existing GI tag cover, and why is it important to Konkan?

Alphonso, popularly known as Hapus, is among the best-known mango varieties grown in the Konkan region. In October 2018, after a nearly decade-long effort by growers and the Dr Balasaheb Sawant Konkan Krishi Vidyapeeth, Alphonso mangoes from Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg, Raigad, Thane and Palghar received a GI tag.

A GI tag identifies a product with a particular geographical origin and recognises that its qualities or reputation are linked to that place. It protects the name from being used for products from elsewhere and allows registered growers from the recognised region to market their produce under that identity.

What is Gujarat seeking, and what is its argument?

The application for Valsad Hapus was filed on February 13, 2023, by Bharatiya Kisan Sangh, Gandhinagar, and Navsari Agricultural University. They argue that Valsad and the surrounding region have their own tradition of growing Alphonso mangoes, that the produce has distinctive qualities, and that it should be allowed to use the name Hapus.

In March 2025, the BJP MP from Valsad, Dhaval Patel, wrote to Union Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal seeking support for the application under the Centre’s One District One Product initiative. He described Valsadi Hapus/Hafus as having “exceptional taste, fragrance, shape, and colour”.

Patel also argued that Valsad and Navsari should have been included when the Konkan GI area was recognised, given their geographical alignment with the coastal Sahyadri region. He pointed to the difference in harvest periods: the Ratnagiri Alphonso season generally ends by the third week of May, when the peak season for Valsadi Hafus begins. A separate tag, he argued, would help Gujarat’s farmers establish their identity and protect them from having their produce sold under another region’s name.

Why are Konkan growers opposing the application?

Their objection is primarily to the use of the name Hapus. Growers say Valsad can seek recognition for its mangoes, but under a different name. They fear that allowing the same name for produce from two regions will confuse consumers and dilute the reputation of Konkan Alphonso.

“The question is not about Gujarat’s mango, but about who will have the right to use the name Hapus. Hapus is only from Konkan and no other region should use it,” said Vilas Sawant, president of the Sindhudurg Fruit Growers Farmers Association. He argued that Konkan’s soil, climate and coastal conditions contribute to the fruit’s taste, aroma and quality.

Amol Bole, a Hapus grower from Shivane Khurd in Ratnagiri’s Rajapur taluka, said Hapus is the identity of the Konkan region and Gujarat’s application must be opposed.

Ratnagiri grower Mangesh Chavan, who also cultivates Alphonso, questioned why Valsad growers insist on using the name Hapus. If their mangoes have such a distinct taste, he argued, they should use their own name instead of the name of Konkan mangoes.

The stakes are economic as well as cultural. According to estimates, Konkan Hapus earns around Rs 200 crore in the domestic market and Rs 80 crore in exports. Growers fear that a second GI using the same name could weaken the premium associated with their produce.

The Maharashtra State Agricultural Marketing Board has also instructed market committees to ensure that mangoes are sold under the name of the state and variety they originate from. It has recommended GI logos, details of registered growers and QR codes to help buyers identify genuine Konkan Hapus.

Political response

The issue has also reached Maharashtra’s political leadership. Industries Minister Uday Samant has said that nobody can replace Konkan Hapus, while Cabinet Minister Nitesh Rane has expressed support for the growers and offered help. Farmers, however, say political assurances must be backed by action before the GI Registry.

Growers are also trying to build a wider campaign. A mango growers’ conference has been announced in Devgad for December 3 to bring farmers and organisations together and decide the next steps. Their demand is that Valsad should seek a distinct tag, as Goa’s mangoes have done with names like Hilario and Mussarad.
