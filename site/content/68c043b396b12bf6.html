    Dr. Singh talks about the amendments introduced to the bill

    They mandate that investigations into paper leak cases be completed within two months and empower States and Union Territories to designate Sessions Courts as special fast-track courts to conduct day-to-day trials and complete them within three months of the filing of the chargesheet.

    The Bill also enhances penalties for offenders. Individuals found guilty of paper leaks or other unfair means in public examinations will face imprisonment ranging from five to 10 years and fines of up to ₹50 lakh. Organised rackets will attract a minimum sentence of seven years and fines of up to ₹10 crore. In addition, the Centre has been empowered to constitute special task forces to investigate cases whenever necessary.

    Congress MP moves Adjournment Motion to discuss new Anti-Defection Law

    Congress MP Manish Tewari on Thursday (July 30, 2026) submitted an Adjournment Motion Notice in the Lok Sabha, seeking a discussion on the contours of a new Anti-Defection Law.

    In his notice, Mr. Tewari called for a legal framework that can prevent “mass political defections driven by opportunism” and those not based on genuine ideological or policy differences.

    The Congress leader stressed the need for a balance between curbing political defections and protecting the right to “honest and critical dissent” among elected representatives inside and outside Parliament and state legislatures. He has urged the House to take up the matter for a detailed discussion, highlighting the need to revisit the existing anti-defection provisions and strengthen democratic accountability.
