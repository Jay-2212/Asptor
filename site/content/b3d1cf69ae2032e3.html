CJP’s Ashutosh Ranka seeks ₹3 crore in damages from Jitendra Singh over Pakistan funding remarks

By Lavpreet Kaur • THE HINDU NATIONAL • 2026-11-27

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has served a defamation notice on Union Minister Jitendra Singh over his alleged remarks that party co-convenor Ashutosh Ranka received funds from Pakistani politicians, demanding a public apology, retraction of the statements and ₹3 crore in damages.

The party’s legal cell served the notice on behalf of Mr. Ranka over remarks made by Mr. Singh at a BJP press conference in Bengaluru on November 3, 2026. Speaking to The Hindu, Mr. Ranka said he would also file defamation case with the Delhi Police under Section 356 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS).

🚨#ImportantAnnouncement🚨 I have sent a legal notice to @DrJitendraSingh for his obnoxious remarks on me saying that I had received "funds from Pakistan". Unlike the kids of these arrogant and uneducated politicians, I worked hard to get into IIT Kanpur and London School of Economics. I gave up on a high-paying job at McKinsey and a comfortable life in London to come back and serve my country. I have toiled hard in public life for the last two years to raise issues that matter to the people of India. All my professional engagements with McKinsey, GAVI and other organisations have been well documented and are duly in compliance with the tax and accounting laws of India. Interestingly enough, the Govt of India happens to have great personal and professional relations with these organisations, including an ongoing donation of $20mn to GAVI. I will not tolerate the likes of Jitendra Singh questioning my integrity and love for the nation. India is all I live for and India is all I will die for.

— Ashutosh Ranka (@AshutoshRanka) November 27, 2026

According to the notice issued by advocate Abhik Chimni, Mr. Singh had alleged: “Ashutosh Ranka paid by Pakistani politicians, and also receiving funds from his handler Sania, even while Operation Sindoor, [he was] was receiving funds.”

Mr. Ranka has rejected the allegations as “false, malicious, and wholly devoid of factual foundation”. “You did not name a single Pakistani politician who is using their personal or party funds to remit money to my client. You did not state a single amount, date, transaction, account, document, or source. The allegation was deliberate while being unsubstantiated. An attempt has been made to pass these allegations off as official facts issued from a press conference,” the notice read.

It alleges that the remarks, made publicly by a Union Minister without furnishing any particulars, were intended to damage Mr. Ranka’s reputation by portraying him as anti-national.

The notice also states that Mr. Ranka’s professional engagements, including his consulting work with McKinsey and Gavi, were legitimate sources of income. It says the remittances received over the past two years were routed through regulated banking channels, declared in his income tax returns and assessed in accordance with the law.

Taking to his X, Mr. Ranka said, “I gave up on a high-paying job at McKinsey and a comfortable life in London to come back and serve my country. I have toiled hard in public life for the last two years to raise issues that matter to the people of India. All my professional engagements with McKinsey, GAVI and other organisations have been well documented and are duly in compliance with the tax and accounting laws of India,” he said.

“I will not tolerate the likes of Jitendra Singh questioning my integrity and love for the nation,” he added.

Through the notice, Mr. Ranka has demanded that Mr. Singh produce evidence supporting his allegations, withdraw the remarks and issue an unconditional public apology. He has also sought ₹3 crore in damages, with the entire amount proposed to be donated to a charitable organisation working towards primary education in India.

The notice warns that failure to comply would leave Mr. Ranka with no option but to pursue appropriate civil and criminal remedies.

CJP activist Saurav Das, who also rejected the allegations on Thursday (November 26, 2026), called them “baseless and fabricated” and challenged the government to provide evidence.

He said the group had been asking from “day one” what the basis of the allegations was, even before its first protest on June 6, 2026, and its first press conference on June 3, 2026. “What is the basis of your so-called allegation?” he asked.

Mr. Das said no evidence or justification had been provided in “four to five months”. He said the same claim had earlier been peddled by right-wingd groups and extremist networks, and was now being repeated by a senior Minister in the government. “It is a shame,” he said.

The CJP also criticised the Minister’s remarks in a post on X, saying a Union Minister “can say anything and get away with it”.

The exchange took place as Mr. Das and Mr. Ranka appeared at the Delhi Crime Branch office, after being summoned in connection with the October 13, 2026, Jantar Mantar trespassing case.

Speaking after leaving the office, Mr. Ranka said it was a “very routine” investigation and that the activists would cooperate fully with any agency. Displaying the Tricolour at Jantar Mantar, or anywhere else, should not be considered a crime, he said.

Mr. Singh had alleged at the Bengaluru press conference that CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke received funds from United States-based donors, and that Mr. Ranka received money from Pakistan. “Ashutosh Ranka has been paid by Pakistani politicians and is also receiving funds through his handlers,” he alleged.

He said anti-India organisations, foreign-funded NGOs and “deep state” operatives were financing the campaign against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, and cited the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act (FCRA).

“There is sufficient evidence to show that they are being funded by unauthorised sources, including anti-India NGOs and anti-India organisations operating within and outside the country,” he said. He also claimed that a woman who took part in the anti-SIR campaign was receiving funds during Operation Sindoor.
